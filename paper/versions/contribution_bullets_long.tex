\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{DoomShift, a benchmark that isolates a scene shift.} Seventeen Doom maps run on exactly the same physics and game engine and differ only in their surroundings. We build the pipeline end to end (Figure~\ref{fig:method}): every-tic recordings, three diffusion world models initialized from pretrained diffusion image models and trained on four maps under one recipe, a decoder fine-tuned for the game, and a standard adapter for a new map, scored on the 13 maps the models never saw, zero-shot and after adaptation, against recorded frames and their decoder's own reconstruction, which separates the model's error from the decoder's. Recordings, models, decoders and adapters are released (link withheld).
\item \textbf{A scene shift breaks appearance, not control.} On the unseen maps all three models keep their turn response (directional score 0.80 to 0.81, against 0.84 to 0.86 on the training maps) while their perceptual error roughly doubles (LPIPS 0.126 to a median 0.262 for SD~3.5) and their scene PSNR drops 2.8 to 3.3~dB. The loss is the same for a U-Net and two transformers from 0.6 to 2.3~B parameters, so across our three backbones it follows what the models were exposed to, not their architecture or scale. It is a step, not a slope: every unseen map is worse in LPIPS than every training map. And it lives in the model, since the decoder renders the unseen maps nearly as well as the training maps (Table~\ref{tab:unseen}, Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}).
\item \textbf{How much adaptation it takes: very little.} A standard LoRA adapter on the frozen model (0.49 percent of the U-Net's parameters), trained on eight episodes of the new map, recovers half of its lost perceptual quality in a median 250 updates. By 4k updates, 2 percent of pretraining and about one A4000 GPU-hour, it recovers 77 percent of SD~3.5's lost perceptual quality and half of its lost PSNR; unchanged, the same procedure recovers 72 and 76 percent on the U-Net and PixArt-$\alpha$. A full fine-tune with 205 times the trainable parameters recovers 87 percent against the adapter's 71 on four maps (Tables~\ref{tab:unseen} and~\ref{tab:cost}, Figure~\ref{fig:adapt}).
\end{itemize}
