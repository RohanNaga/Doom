\textbf{Adaptation.} To measure how much adaptation a map needs, we keep the trained model frozen and train a standard adapter on a few episodes of that map, then score the adapted model on held-out episodes at each update budget.
For each unseen map we adapt each backbone's 200k EMA weights with rank-16 LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} on every attention projection of the frozen backbone~\citep{gao2024vista} and train the control MLP, input projection and noise-bucket embedding in full (4.2M parameters, 0.49 percent of the U-Net; 25.9M, 4.1 percent, of PixArt-$\alpha$; 40.6M, 1.8 percent, of SD~3.5)~\citep{xie2023difffit}.
We train on 8 adaptation episodes (for the U-Net about 1 A4000 GPU-hour per map for 4k updates) and score the non-EMA weights at 0, 50, 100, 150, 250, 500, 1k, 2k, 4k and 8k updates (PixArt-$\alpha$ to 4k; SD~3.5 at 0, 250, 500, 1k, 2k and 4k), with the control checks (forgetting, the scene PSNR on the training maps, and the directional check) at 0 and 8k. 4k updates is the elbow of the curve, and we report it everywhere: across the 13 unseen maps it captures most of the benefit (on average 96 percent of each map's 8k gain; Figure~\ref{fig:adapt}) at the smallest budget; 8k serves as the check. As a baseline we fine-tune all 860.5M parameters of the U-Net at $2\times10^{-5}$ on maps 6, 7, 8 and 16 with the same eight episodes and score it at 4k.
A map's budget is the first grid point at which it recovers at least half of its zero-shot LPIPS rise over the training maps; maps that do not by 8k are right-censored~\citep{taylor2009transfer}.
