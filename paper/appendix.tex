
\section{Protocol details}
\label{app:protocol}

\textbf{Recording.} \texttt{record\_arnold.py} replaces Arnold's evaluation loop with one that steps the engine one tic at a time and stores each frame, then the control it is about to send, then steps. The raw \texttt{buttons} field is the list Arnold \emph{requested}; the engine executes its first 19 entries. Arnold's action builder appends ten weapon-selection names to that list at every game start, so a weapon request falls inside the executed range only in a recorder worker's first episode, and later weapon requests are silently dropped while movement and firing still execute. Requested and executed controls agree on 89 percent of tics, almost all of the rest are dropped weapon requests, and 0.15 percent are anti-stuck overrides. The sidecars store the executed 19-bit string, which is what the models condition on. For the unseen arenas, 328 episodes were newly recorded by 16 workers under one pinned protocol (same Arnold build, 8 bots, 150~s, every tic, seeds declared in the corpus README and disjoint from every earlier batch), and each worker's first episode was set aside, leaving 24 per arena. The kill and frag counters never increment in these recordings, although enemies visibly die on screen, so the game variables do not record kills. The earlier batches, which only the pre-registered distance test below uses, held 4 episodes on arenas 1 and 9 to 15, 10 on 16 and 17 and 20 on 6 to 8; arenas 6 to 8 came from a separate batch, a batch-map confound the single-protocol recordings remove.

\textbf{Windows.} A one-tic window is 32 consecutive recorded tics plus the target, inside one life on one map; deaths cut windows, which keeps 85 (arena 1) to 99 percent of candidate starts. The in-distribution evaluations use 512 windows drawn with seed 0 from validation episodes 6000 to 6099 (25 per training map); the per-arena evaluations use 256 windows per arena, 32 from each of the 8 held-out episodes.

\textbf{Recipe notes.} The 1,000-step linear schedule ($\beta$ from $10^{-4}$ to 0.02) is neither backbone's native schedule: 273 of its timesteps are noisier than anything SD~1.4 saw in pretraining, and SD~3.5 was pretrained with rectified flow. Context noise augmentation blends $\sqrt{1-q}\,c+\sqrt{q}\,\epsilon$ with one level $q$ per example drawn below 0.7 and embedded as one of 10 buckets; inference uses clean context and bucket 0. Fused AdamW runs at $5\times10^{-5}$ after 2,000 linear warmup updates, with no weight decay, gradient clipping at 1.0, bf16 autocast over fp32 parameters, seed 0 and no action dropout. The EMA decays at 0.9999 per update, applied as $0.9999^8$ every eighth update, in fp32. Training sees windows from episodes 0 to 1,999 (500 per training map, 9.66M valid windows); 200k updates at batch 32 cover about two thirds of one pass.

\textbf{The decoder.} We fine-tune the SD~1 decoder $D$ on training-map frames from their ground-truth latents with the encoder frozen, using MSE plus 0.1~LPIPS (3,486 updates, learning rate $10^{-5}$, batch 24, 4.0 hours on one A6000), validate on episodes 6000 to 6099 and keep the terminal checkpoint; it passed its gate at $+4.33$~dB and LPIPS $-0.039$ on the development set. On 200 validation frames its scene rows go from 26.93~dB and LPIPS 0.092 (stock) to 28.44 and 0.060, and the HUD rows from 17.83 to 30.67~dB. An MSE-only objective, the recipe GameNGen describes, reaches 29.09~dB on the scene but roughly triples the full-frame LPIPS (0.094 to 0.285 by 2,000 updates): it sharpens the HUD and blurs the scene. The stock decoders saw no Doom frame; the SD~3.5 decoder, fine-tuned the same way, raises SD~3.5's scene reconstruction on the 512 validation windows from 29.99 to 31.84~dB and lowers its LPIPS from 0.045 to 0.028.

\textbf{What the variation between arenas follows.} Across the 13 unseen arenas the model's absolute scene PSNR tracks how static the recordings are (Spearman 0.95 with the PSNR of the last frame copied forward), while its gap to the reconstruction upper bound does not ($-0.21$), which is why every arena is read against its upper bound. On the full frame the 32 HUD rows, which the stock decoder renders at about 18~dB and which barely change from one tic to the next (the previous frame matches them at 88 to 99~dB), carry 37 to 62 percent of the stock decoder's reconstruction error per arena; every pixel score in the paper therefore uses the scene rows. The fine-tuned decoder raises the model's raw scene PSNR by 0.24 to 0.71~dB (median 0.35) and lowers its LPIPS on all 13 arenas (by 0.019 to 0.044).

\section{The distance}
\label{app:distance}

\textbf{Definition, frozen Sep 25 before any map was scored.} A map is a cloud of 1,000 frames: 4 episodes $\times$ 250 frames at least 32 tics into a life, averaged over ten random 4-episode subsets where a map has more episodes. Features are SD~1.x latents with the padding rows removed, pooled $5\times5$ to 192 dimensions. Each frame is weighted by its latent motion $\lVert z_t-z_{t-1}\rVert$ plus a floor that gives the quietest half of the cloud 25 percent of the weight; the unweighted distance differs by at most 0.006 and swaps two adjacent ranks, so the weighting is immaterial here. The reference is 50 seeded training episodes per training map, 250 frames each, 25 per motion decile. $d(m)=\min_k \mathrm{SW}_2(\text{cloud}_m,\text{reference}_k)$ over the four training maps, the sliced Wasserstein-2 distance with 1,000 projection directions shared by every distance; the nearest map, not the pooled corpus, because transport to a mixture charges a seen map for not resembling the other three. A disjoint second reference draw gives the train-versus-train floor, 0.022 to 0.093; on the evaluation recordings the 13 arenas lie at 0.118 to 0.270. Pixel-space and SD~3.5-space versions rank the 30 maps of the pre-registered set like the primary (Kendall $\tau$ 0.70 and 0.76).

\textbf{The pre-registered test.} On 30 maps of the earlier episode set (the 17 arenas and 13 campaign maps of another WAD that the paper otherwise leaves in the dataset release), the partial Spearman of the U-Net's one-tic raw gain (the registered outcome: its scene PSNR minus that of the last frame copied forward) on $d$, controlling for the copied frame's PSNR, is $-0.73$ [$-0.84$, $-0.32$] (permutation $p=10^{-4}$), negative in all 30 leave-one-map-out refits and $-0.80$ at four tics. Within the 17 arenas it is $-0.57$, within the campaign maps $-0.38$ with an interval that crosses zero, and with a training-map indicator and a group indicator as covariates $-0.42$ [\prov{$-0.63$}, \prov{$+0.13$}]. The dated amended assessment reads ``qualified evidence for the pooled U-Net association; original gate failed; unseen-only trend inconclusive; cross-backbone replication pending''. We read it as a pooled group effect, not an ordering.

\textbf{Two transition-level distances.} $d$ sees single frames; the model predicts a transition from 32 frames and a control. We registered two distances on transition windows (state, recent motion at lags 1, 4, 16 and 31, the next change and the executed controls) before the adapted scores: a \emph{directed coverage} of the map's transition windows by the training maps' windows (three nearest neighbours from distinct episodes), and a \emph{transfer gap}, the error of a 16-nearest-neighbour transition predictor that searches the training maps' windows minus that of one that searches the map's own windows. Both fail the training-versus-unseen floor that $d$ passes: coverage is 0.524 to 0.609 on the training maps' held-out episodes and 0.526 to 0.613 on the arenas, and the transfer gap 0.005 to 0.012 against 0.009 to 0.038. Over the 13 arenas both follow how static the recordings are: coverage has Spearman $-0.69$ ($p=0.010$) and the transfer gap $+0.63$ ($p=0.021$) with the PSNR of the last frame copied forward, so both correlate with the raw zero-shot PSNR (coverage $-0.80$, $p=0.001$; transfer gap $+0.62$, $p=0.025$) and with the raw PSNR at 4k ($-0.77$, $p=0.002$; $+0.66$, $p=0.013$). Against the outcomes that remove this, the 4k gap to the reconstruction upper bound, the 4k gain and the budget, neither orders the arenas (coverage $+0.42$, $+0.25$, $+0.40$; transfer gap $-0.19$, $+0.14$, $-0.39$; all $p\ge0.15$, $n=13$); the two disagree with each other ($-0.53$) and with $d$ ($+0.37$, $+0.36$). In two studies a directed measure predicted transfer better than a symmetric transport distance~\citep{mensink2021factors,westny2026latent}; over 13 arenas of one game neither does.

\section{Closed loop}
\label{app:closedloop}

\textbf{The failure.} In SD~3.5's collapsed rollouts the latent's global scale stays inside the real range while one channel's per-frame mean is captured at a fixed value: channel 13, whose mean sits near $+0.45$ in real recordings, drops to $-1.39$ to $-1.50$ (17 of the 22 collapsed rollouts at 70k, both window sets) or $-2.60$ to $-2.73$ (the blank frames). Later EMA excursions shift channels 15 and 8 as well, so the absorbing states are a set of per-channel mean shifts. Noise augmentation trains against isotropic corruption of the context, not a structured shift in one direction of latent space, and teacher-forced evaluations never feed a prediction back, so neither protects against it.

\section{Latent skill and the skill deficit}
\label{app:skill}

The zero-shot latent skill score $S_0=10\,\overline{\log_{10}(\text{persistence latent MSE}/\text{model latent MSE})}$ is the mean log error ratio in dB, computed without a decoder. It gives equal weight to windows on the log scale, but near-zero persistence error can still make individual terms extreme; zero-error windows require an explicit handling rule. It compares predictions only within one backbone, because the latent spaces differ. Zero-shot, the U-Net scores 2.55 to 3.56~dB on the training maps and 1.14 to 1.96~dB on the arenas, and PixArt-$\alpha$ 2.44 to 3.47 and 1.28 to 2.21; $S_0$ was not computed for SD~3.5. In raw terms $S_0$ does not order the endpoints: its Spearman correlation is $+0.38$ with the gap to the reconstruction upper bound at 4k ($p=0.20$), $+0.43$ with the 4k PSNR gain ($p=0.14$) and $+0.31$ with the budget ($p=0.30$; $n=13$). A motion-matched skill deficit $\Delta$, the U-Net's mean skill shortfall against training-map windows of matched persistence error (10 quantile bins), passes the training maps' floor ($-0.62$ to $+0.46$ on the training maps, 0.92 to 1.86 on the arenas), and its Spearman with $d$ is $+0.05$; it does not order the raw outcomes either ($-0.31$ with the 4k gap to the upper bound, $p=0.31$; $-0.24$ with the budget, $p=0.44$; $n=13$). Because a full fine-tune also moves the input projection and every layer the decoder's input depends on, the adapter-against-full comparison is reported in $S$ as well: on arena 7, $S$ at 4,000 updates is \tbd{} for the adapter and \tbd{} for the full fine-tune.

\section{Public Doom world models}
\label{app:public}

Two open GameNGen reproductions (Stiegler's and Taketani's) release weights; both train on one map (\texttt{deathmatch\_simple}) at 320$\times$240 every tic, with 18 and 12 discrete actions that do not cover Arnold's combined controls. DIAMOND's released model is CS:GO, Oasis is a gated Minecraft model, and PlayGen and MultiGen release no Doom weights (as of September 2026). Scoring a one-map public model on our arenas would measure its own transfer to Arnold's recordings, confounded by map, assets, behaviour and recipe, not compare recipes; we leave it to a later version, scored on its own map and on ours with only the windows whose control history maps exactly.
