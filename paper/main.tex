\begin{abstract}
% depends: SD 3.5 200k rescore (Monday) for sentence 3's third backbone; sources: results/fresh_rescore/*_tuned (scene crop, fine-tuned decoder), paper/tables/tuned/adapt_summary.json, results/sd35_stability/collapse_rates_50k_130k.md
Game world models are scored on held-out trajectories of their training scenes, and absolute PSNR mostly measures how static the recordings are.
On an open 17-arena Doom benchmark scored against copy-last-frame persistence, we train three pretrained diffusion backbones on four arenas and ask what they keep, lose and relearn on the other thirteen.
Decoded through one decoder, the U-Net still beats copy-last on every unseen arena, but its advantage shrinks from 5.1~dB in distribution to 1.1--2.9~dB (median 2.0) and its LPIPS is worse than persistence's on 13 of 13 arenas, while its turn response survives.
A frame distance to the training recordings separates seen from unseen arenas but does not order the unseen ones, while the frozen model's own zero-shot latent skill score does.
Rank-16 adapters on the frozen backbone, trained on eight episodes of an arena, close half of its gap to the in-distribution advantage within 4k updates on 9 of 13 arenas (five within 500), recover a median 1.8~dB, and turn the perceptual loss into a win on 11 of 13 arenas (the other two within 0.01).
In closed loop, good one-tic scores do not guarantee stability, and the EMA reduces collapses without removing them.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{World models, Generalization, Persistence baseline}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A learned game simulator is judged on held-out trajectories of the scenes it trained on~\citep{valevski2024gamengen,alonso2024diamond,he2025matrixgame2}, yet a world model must generalize to a new level before it can plan or train a policy there.
We ask this for Doom: a model trained on four deathmatch arenas is scored on each of the thirteen other arenas of its WAD, then adapted to each from a few episodes.
We found no Doom world model scored on maps it did not train on (MultiGen does not say~\citep{po2026multigen}).
Absolute PSNR also ranks recordings rather than models, since a static scene scores high for any predictor; video prediction reported a copy-last baseline~\citep{mathieu2016deep,lotter2017prednet,villegas2019fidelity}, and game world models dropped it.
We make persistence, the last context frame copied forward, the zero line: every transfer score is a paired difference against it on the same windows, read beside the reconstruction upper bound~\citep{zheng2023occworld,karypidis2024dinoforesight}.

% depends: full fine-tune comparator (Monday) for item (3); SD 3.5 200k rescore (Monday) for item (2)'s third backbone
We contribute (0) three action-conditioned latent world models of Doom trained under one next-tic recipe (SD~1.4 U-Net, PixArt-$\alpha$ and SD~3.5 Medium; 32-tic context, executed 19-button controls, $v$-prediction with noise augmentation, 200k updates), a matched decoder fine-tune, and the adapter path, all released with the benchmark (Figure~\ref{fig:method});
(1) an open 17-arena Doom benchmark (link withheld for review) scored per arena against persistence beside the reconstruction upper bound, with a directional check and closed-loop rollouts;
(2) the measurement that off the training maps the decoded advantage shrinks and the perceptual margin changes sign for all three backbones while the turn response survives (Figures~\ref{fig:teaser} and~\ref{fig:shift});
(3) per-arena adaptation curves for rank-16 adapters, with the budget to close half of each arena's gap to the in-distribution advantage (median 1k updates; 9 of 13 by 4k), a data ladder showing one episode recovers most of the gain, and a full fine-tune comparator (\tbd{}); and
(4) two negative results: neither a per-frame distance nor two transition-level distances order the arenas by zero-shot advantage or adaptation budget, whereas the frozen model's zero-shot latent skill does (Spearman 0.90 with the advantage at 4k, exploratory at $n=13$), and one-tic quality does not guarantee closed-loop stability.

\section{Benchmark and recipe}
\label{sec:bench}

\textbf{Data.} Every episode is ViZDoom~\citep{kempka2016vizdoom} deathmatch on one of the 17 arenas of the Arnold agent's WAD~\citep{lample2017arnold} with Freedoom assets: Arnold against 8 bots for 150~s, recorded at every tic (one game step, 35~Hz, 320$\times$240) with the 19 buttons the engine executed (\appref{app:protocol}).
The models train on arenas 2 to 5 (500 episodes each) and are scored on their validation episodes (512 windows); each unseen arena (1, 6 to 17) has 24 newly recorded episodes, 16 for adaptation and 8 held out (256 windows).

\textbf{Three backbones, one recipe.} We fine-tune the SD~1.4 U-Net~\citep{rombach2022ldm}, PixArt-$\alpha$~\citep{chen2023pixart} (a DiT in the same 4-channel latents) and SD~3.5 Medium~\citep{esser2024sd3} (an MMDiT with 16-channel latents) into next-tic world models under one recipe (Figure~\ref{fig:method}): $v$-prediction~\citep{salimans2022progressive}, context noise augmentation as in GameNGen~\citep{valevski2024gamengen}, AdamW at $5\times10^{-5}$, batch 32, 200k updates, an fp32 EMA at 0.9999, 10-step DDIM~\citep{song2021ddim}.
Backbone and latent space change together, so the backbones compare pretrained packages.
Rollouts stay in latent space, so the decoder only renders.
We fine-tune the SD~1 decoder on training-map frames from their true latents with the encoder frozen, using MSE plus 0.1~LPIPS, then freeze it; every pixel number below uses it unless marked stock.
An MSE-only objective sharpens the HUD but triples scene LPIPS (\appref{app:perarena}).

\textbf{What we measure.} With $x$ the true next frame, $x_\text{last}$ the last context frame, $z$, $z_\text{last}$ their latents, $\hat z$ the prediction and $D$ the decoder, the \emph{decoded advantage} $A=\mathrm{PSNR}(D(\hat z),D(z))-\mathrm{PSNR}(D(z_\text{last}),D(z))$ is the PSNR gain over persistence, a paired difference like Genie's $\Delta$PSNR~\citep{bruce2024genie}.
The \emph{perceptual margin} $M=\mathrm{LPIPS}(D(\hat z),x)-\mathrm{LPIPS}(x_\text{last},x)$~\citep{zhang2018lpips} is below zero when the model looks better than copying; the \emph{gap} $G=\mathrm{PSNR}(D(z),x)-\mathrm{PSNR}(D(\hat z),x)$ is the distance to the decoder's reconstruction upper bound; the \emph{zero-shot latent skill score} $S_0$ is $10\log_{10}$ of persistence's latent MSE over the model's.
The \emph{directional} score is the fraction of turning windows whose predicted image shift reverses when left and right are swapped in the newest control.
Pixel scores use the scene rows (0 to 207), since persistence copies the HUD almost exactly.
$A_\text{train}$, $A$ on the training maps (5.06~dB, episode-bootstrap interval 4.75 to 5.39), is the in-distribution reference, not an arena's own upper bound.

\section{What happens off the training maps}
\label{sec:shift}

% depends: SD 3.5 200k rescore (Monday); sources: results/directional_fresh/summary.json, results/fresh_rescore/*/map??/metrics.json, results/family_step/family_step.json
\textbf{Where the deficit sits.} In distribution all three backbones beat persistence at one and four tics and turn the right way (Table~\ref{tab:indomain}).
Off the training maps (Table~\ref{tab:unseen}, Figure~\ref{fig:shift}) the turn response survives: per-arena reversal rates are 0.73 to 0.86 for the U-Net (median 0.81, pooled 0.80; 0.69 to 0.90 across the three backbones) against 0.79 to 0.91 on the training maps (pooled 0.86), with the raw-frame reference at 0.89 and 0.89.
$A$ stays positive on every arena for all three backbones but falls from 5.06 to a median 2.03~dB.
The decoder's scene reconstruction PSNR is 28.6~dB in distribution and 25.2 to 29.7~dB unseen (median 27.3), while $G$ grows from 3.4 to 3.7--6.9~dB (median 5.0); the fine-tuned decoder raises $A$ over the stock one by 0.2~dB zero-shot and 0.7~dB after adaptation (\appref{app:perarena}).
Nor is the sign reversal of the perceptual margin the decoder's floor: the decoder's own reconstruction LPIPS (0.05 to 0.09) sits far below persistence's (0.18 to 0.28), and the rendered prediction still has higher LPIPS on 13 of 13 arenas ($M$ +0.03 to +0.17, median +0.08; in distribution $-$0.08).

\textbf{The distance orders families, not arenas.} Before scoring any map we froze $d$, the sliced Wasserstein-2 distance between the motion-weighted cloud of a map's per-frame SD~1 latents and that of its nearest training map (\appref{app:distance}).
The training maps' held-out episodes sit inside the train-versus-train floor (0.03 to 0.07 against 0.02 to 0.09) and every unseen arena outside it (0.12 to 0.27), but within the 13 arenas $d$ orders nothing: Spearman with $A$ $-0.22$, with the adaptation budget $-0.35$ (none below $p = 0.2$); two registered transition-level distances fail even the separation, and a pre-registered 30-map test failed its validation gate (\appref{app:distance}).
What does order the arenas is the frozen model's zero-shot latent skill $S_0$: it predicts where an arena ends up rather than how much it gains (exploratory Spearman $+0.90$ with $A$ at 4k, $+0.18$ with the gain).

% depends: nothing pending; collapse counts recomputed from results/sd35_stability/collapse_rates_50k_130k.md
\textbf{Closed loop.} Good one-tic scores do not guarantee stable rollouts: SD~3.5 has the best one-tic numbers, yet on 16 fixed windows read every 5k updates from 55k to 130k a frame under 10~dB occurs in 23 of 256 non-EMA and 9 of 256 EMA rollouts (11 and 8 without 70k), with the same sampler; the EMA removes checkpoint-specific collapses, not the failure mode (\appref{app:closedloop}).

\section{Crossing the shift}
\label{sec:adapt}

% depends: full fine-tune comparator (Monday); the 8k grid (tonight) replaces the 4k numbers
\textbf{Protocol.} For each unseen arena we adapt the U-Net 200k EMA weights with rank-16 LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} on every attention projection of the frozen backbone, as Vista does~\citep{gao2024vista}, and train the control MLP, input projection and noise-bucket embedding in full (4.2M parameters, 0.49 percent), since LoRA alone falls short in DiT transfer~\citep{xie2023difffit}.
We train on 8 adaptation episodes (learning rate $10^{-4}$, batch 32) and score the non-EMA weights at 0, 250, 500, 1k, 2k and 4k updates, with $A$ on the training maps (forgetting) and the directional score as control checks; a full fine-tune at GameNGen's rate on every arena is the comparator (\tbd{}: Monday).
An arena's budget is the first grid point at which $A$ reaches $\tfrac12(A_0+A_\text{train})$, half of its gap to the in-distribution advantage from its zero-shot $A_0$; arenas that never reach it by the final budget of the grid are right-censored~\citep{taylor2009transfer}.
Half of the gap in dB is not half of the error: closing 1.8~dB removes 34 percent of the persistence error, not 28.

% depends: forgetting and directional control checks on all arenas (8k chain tonight); sources: paper/tables/tuned/adapt_summary.json, results/adapt (ladder, seeds, recipe: RESEARCH_CONTEXT 2026-09-27 08:05)
\textbf{What it takes.} 9 of 13 arenas close half of their gap within 4k updates (5 within 500; median budget 1k, arena-bootstrap 250 to beyond 4k; arenas 1, 8, 12 and 16 censored), one reaches $A_\text{train}$, and the median $A$ rises from 2.04 to 3.80~dB (Figure~\ref{fig:adapt}, Table~\ref{tab:cost}); on average 69 percent of the gain lands by 250 updates.
At 4k the rendered prediction has lower scene LPIPS than persistence on 11 of 13 arenas, the other two within 0.01, and the median $G$ falls from 5.0 to 3.4~dB, the in-distribution value.
In stock-decoder checks one adaptation episode finishes within about 0.25~dB of sixteen on arenas 7, 8, 12 and 16, and a three- or five-fold learning rate or an 8k grid moves $A$ at 4k by less than 0.06~dB on arenas 12 and 16, inside the two-seed spread (0.02 to 0.06~dB): the level an arena reaches looks set by the arena.
The budget tracks $S_0$ (Spearman $-0.61$), not $d$ ($-0.35$); forgetting and the directional check land with tonight's 8k rerun (\tbd{}).

\section{Related work and conclusion}
\label{sec:related}
\label{sec:limits}

XEWorld holds out two robot embodiments, finds their error tracking an appearance distance, and fine-tunes on 25 to 75 episodes~\citep{chen2026xeworld}; AVID and AdaWorld adapt to a held-out game or environment~\citep{rigter2024avid,gao2025adaworld}; Pathdreamer scores unseen buildings against a reprojection baseline~\citep{koh2021pathdreamer}.
None scores per scene against persistence, and Doom map holdouts have been for agents~\citep{lample2017arnold,wydmuch2018vizdoom}.
Dataset distances predict fine-tuned outcomes over wide ranges~\citep{alvarezmelis2020otdd,mensink2021factors}; we found no per-target measurement of a world model's adaptation budget.
Our claims cover these 13 arenas of one WAD, one agent, one-tic scoring and one seed on most arenas.
Off its training maps the U-Net keeps its turn response and part of its advantage over copying and loses its perceptual advantage; eight episodes and 4k rank-16 adapter updates (1.1 GPU-hours) close half of the gap on 9 of 13 arenas (arena-bootstrap 46 to 92 percent) and restore the perceptual advantage on 11, and in our checks one episode gives most of that.
\label{body-end}
