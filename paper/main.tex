\begin{abstract}
% depends: fresh-set rescore of the 13 arenas (Sep 27; U-Net 200k EMA today, PixArt 200k and SD 3.5 200k pending), tuned SD 1 decoder, scene rows; 13-arena U-Net LoRA runs (grid 0 to 4,000) and the forgetting read; collapse counts from results/sd35_stability/collapse_rates_50k_130k.md
% hypothesis: sentence 3's reach to all three backbones (the numbers are the U-Net's; PixArt and SD 3.5 replicate after the rescore) and sentence 5 (the adaptation result)
% review (Sep 27): Fable's six-sentence structure with Astra's facts (A7, F1, F11; coordinator ruling): sentence 3 names the U-Net and the LPIPS comparison, sentence 4 says "no clear association", sentence 5 states the measurement with its result held in \tbd, sentence 6 takes A18's "do not guarantee"
Game world models are scored on held-out trajectories of their training scenes, and absolute PSNR mostly measures how static the footage is.
On an open 17-arena Doom benchmark scored against copy-last-frame persistence, we train three pretrained diffusion backbones on four arenas and ask what they keep, lose and relearn on the other thirteen.
Decoded through one decoder, the U-Net still beats copy-last on every unseen arena, but its advantage shrinks from \prov{3.2--4.3}~dB at home to \prov{0.7--2.7}~dB and its LPIPS is worse than persistence's on \prov{13 of 13} arenas, while its turn response survives.
A frame distance to the training footage separates seen from unseen arenas but has no clear association with the advantage among the unseen ones.
We measure what closing half of each arena's gap to the home advantage costs rank-16 adapters on the frozen backbone: \tbd{} updates on \tbd{} of 13 arenas, at \tbd{}~dB of forgetting.
In closed loop, good one-step scores do not guarantee stability, and the EMA reduces collapses without removing them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% fable (sentences 1 and 2; "is meant for" -> "is built for"); fable, edited (sentence 3, shortened; the "keeps, loses, relearns" clause now lives in the title)
A learned game simulator is judged on held-out trajectories of the scenes it trained on~\citep{valevski2024gamengen,alonso2024diamond,he2025matrixgame2}.
We need a world model to generalize to unseen levels before using it to plan or train a policy there.
We ask this for Doom: a model trained on four deathmatch arenas is scored on each of the thirteen other arenas of its WAD, then adapted to each from a few episodes.

% fable, edited: "one to five domains, pooled into one score" (the memos find no per-scene score); "we found no Doom world model" (MultiGen is ambiguous, lit-scene-generalization memo D); Spearman 0.945 recomputed on the 13 unseen arenas from the frozen h1 metrics; fable's Matrix-Game static-output clause cut for space
% depends: fresh-set rescore (the persistence range and the Spearman are from the pre-Sep-26 episodes)
The measurement is rare: we found no Doom world model scored on maps it did not train on (MultiGen does not say~\citep{po2026multigen}), and other held-out domains are an embodiment, a game, an environment or a building (Section~\ref{sec:related}).
Absolute PSNR also ranks footage, not models: on our unseen arenas persistence runs from \prov{19.4 to 23.2}~dB and the model's PSNR follows it at Spearman \prov{0.95}.
Video prediction reported a copy-last baseline~\citep{mathieu2016deep,lotter2017prednet,villegas2019fidelity}; game world models dropped it.
We make persistence, defined on every map without training, the zero line: each transfer score is a paired difference against it on the same windows, read beside the reconstruction ceiling~\citep{zheng2023occworld,karypidis2024dinoforesight}.

\section{Benchmark and recipe}
\label{sec:bench}

% depends: fresh 24-episode recording of the 13 unseen arenas (328 recorded Sep 26, 16 worker-first episodes set aside) and its SD 1 encode (Sep 27); window manifests from adapt_split.py
\textbf{Data.} Every episode is ViZDoom~\citep{kempka2016vizdoom} deathmatch on one of the 17 arenas of the Arnold agent's WAD~\citep{lample2017arnold} with Freedoom assets: Arnold against 8 bots for 150 game-seconds, recorded losslessly at every tic (35~Hz, 320$\times$240 with the HUD) with the 19-button control the engine executed (\appref{app:protocol}).
The models train on arenas 2 to 5 (500 episodes each) and are scored there on 25 held-out episodes per map.
Each unseen arena (1, 6 to 17) has \prov{24} fresh episodes under one pinned protocol, 16 for adaptation and 8 held out, with 32 fixed windows per held-out episode scored at every checkpoint.

% depends: tuned SD 1 decoder (training-map frames only, gated Sep 27); GameNGen facts from arXiv 2408.14837 v2 sec. 3.2.2, read Sep 26
\textbf{Three backbones, one recipe.} We fine-tune three text-to-image diffusion models into next-tic world models: the SD~1.4 U-Net~\citep{rombach2022ldm} (860M parameters, 4-channel SD~1.x latents), PixArt-$\alpha$~\citep{chen2023pixart} (a 628M DiT in the same latents) and SD~3.5 Medium~\citep{esser2024sd3} (a 2.27B MMDiT with its own 16-channel latents).
Backbone and latent space change together, so the rows compare pretrained packages.
The 32 context latents are stacked on channels with the noisy target, and the 32 executed controls enter as tokens.
The recipe is shared: $v$-prediction~\citep{salimans2022progressive}, context noise augmentation up to 0.7 in 10 buckets as in GameNGen~\citep{valevski2024gamengen}, AdamW at $5\times10^{-5}$, batch 32, 200k updates, an fp32 EMA at 0.9999, and 10-step DDIM~\citep{song2021ddim} (\appref{app:protocol}).
Rollouts stay in latent space, so the decoder only renders. Following GameNGen, we plan to tune it with MSE on training-map frames, then freeze it.

% depends: tuned SD 1 decoder and scene-only rows (scorer additions for the fresh-set rescore); the -0.77 is from the pre-Sep-26 episodes
\textbf{What we measure.} Let $x$ be the true next frame, $x_\text{last}$ the last context frame, $z$ and $z_\text{last}$ their latents, $\hat z$ the prediction and $D$ the decoder.
The \emph{decoded advantage} $A=\mathrm{PSNR}(D(\hat z),D(z))-\mathrm{PSNR}(D(z_\text{last}),D(z))$ puts the model and copy-last through the same decoder; it is averaged over windows, then equally over maps.
It is a paired difference on the same frame, as Genie's $\Delta$PSNR is between inferred and random actions~\citep{bruce2024genie}.
The \emph{perceptual margin} $M=\mathrm{LPIPS}(D(\hat z),x)-\mathrm{LPIPS}(x_\text{last},x)$~\citep{zhang2018lpips} is taken against raw persistence; below zero the model looks better than copying.
The \emph{gap} $G$ is the reconstruction ceiling $\mathrm{PSNR}(D(z),x)$ minus the model's PSNR; a decoder-free latent skill is in \appref{app:skill}.
The \emph{directional} score is the fraction of turning windows whose predicted image shift reverses when left and right are swapped in the newest control (0.91 on true frames).
Pixel quantities use the scene rows: per window the HUD carries \prov{39 to 68} percent of the stock decoder's squared error (geometric mean over windows) and persistence copies it almost exactly (\prov{88 to 96}~dB). We never rank maps on raw PSNR against persistence, which tracks how static the footage is (Spearman \prov{$-0.77$}).

\section{What happens off the training maps}
\label{sec:shift}

% fable, edited (the ceiling and the survival of the turn response, moved here from its introduction)
% depends: PixArt 200k and SD 3.5 200k final reads for the in-domain ordering; fresh-set rescore (Table 2, Figure 2), paired stock-versus-tuned decoding of the same predictions (Sep 28), U-Net 30-map directional; step-sweep numbers from dossier 4.2 (U-Net 200k live, training-map windows)
% hypothesis: PixArt and SD 3.5 show the same shrinkage and flip
\textbf{Where the deficit sits.} In domain all three rows beat persistence at one and four tics, within 0.85~dB of each other, and turn the right way (Table~\ref{tab:indomain}).
Off the training maps we read three legs side by side, never summed (Table~\ref{tab:unseen}, Figure~\ref{fig:arenas}).
The turn response survives (\tbd{} of unseen-arena windows, \prov{0.79 to 0.87} per arena so far, against 0.87 at home).
$A$ drops by about 2~dB (\prov{3.60} to \prov{1.51}), stays positive on every arena at one tic, and turns negative at four tics on arenas \prov{6 and 7}.
The rendering of true latents does not degrade: the ceiling is \prov{23.91}~dB at home and \prov{23.62}~dB unseen, so the loss is not the decoder's floor; decoding the same predictions with the stock and tuned decoders (\tbd{}) tests whether it also renders off-distribution latents worse.
Nor is the perceptual flip the decoder's floor: its own LPIPS (\prov{0.08 to 0.12}) sits below persistence's (\prov{0.15 to 0.23}), and the model, scored against the decoded true frame, still has higher LPIPS than raw persistence (\prov{13 of 13}).
Blur under uncertainty is one possible explanation for the PSNR--LPIPS tradeoff; the mechanism remains untested.

\textbf{The distance orders families, not arenas.} Before scoring any map we froze $D$, the sliced Wasserstein-2 distance between the motion-weighted cloud of a map's per-frame SD~1 latents and that of its nearest training map (\appref{app:distance}).
The training maps sit inside the train-versus-train floor (\prov{0.03 to 0.07} against 0.02 to 0.09) and every unseen arena outside it (\prov{0.115 to 0.282}), but within the 13 unseen arenas we do not establish an association between $D$ and $A$ (Spearman \prov{$-0.23$}).
The pre-registered 30-map test, which also held campaign maps of another WAD, failed its validation gate (\appref{app:distance}).
Its partial Spearman of $-0.73$ [$-0.84$, $-0.32$] falls to \prov{$-0.42$} with family indicators as covariates, so we read it as qualified evidence of a pooled association, not of an ordering.
$D$ sees how frames look while the model predicts transitions; a transition-level distance (\todo{name}), frozen before any adapted score, is the adaptation study's second axis.

% collapse counts recomputed from results/sd35_stability/collapse_rates_50k_130k.md (seed-0 windows, 16 reads from 55k to 130k)
% depends: nothing pending in this paragraph beyond the PixArt 200k rows of Appendix D
\textbf{Closed loop.} Good one-step scores do not guarantee stable rollouts: SD~3.5 has the best one-tic numbers, yet at 50k and 70k its live weights fall into an absorbing state, one latent channel's mean captured and frames blank (\appref{app:closedloop}).
On 16 fixed windows read every 5k updates from 55k to 130k, a frame under 10~dB occurs in 23 of 256 live and 9 of 256 EMA rollouts (11 and 8 without 70k): the EMA removes checkpoint-specific collapses, not the failure mode.
Both share the sampler, so the few-step sampling DIAMOND blames for drift~\citep{alonso2024diamond} is not what differs between them.

\section{Crossing the shift}
\label{sec:adapt}

% adapter facts: lora.py and adapt_wm.py at [anonymous revision]; U-Net trained total 4.21M = 0.49 percent (RESEARCH_CONTEXT 2026-09-26 20:10); the design also trains the control MLP's position table
% camera-ready: % adapter facts: lora.py and adapt_wm.py at eaf565a; U-Net trained total 4.21M = 0.49 percent (RESEARCH_CONTEXT 2026-09-26 20:10); the design also trains the control MLP's position table
% depends: full fine-tune comparator on arena 7 (Monday if a card idles and arena 7's LoRA curve is flat, else October)
% review (Sep 27): F20's two sentences; A22 via Fable adds the position table; Astra's scope fact (DiffFit is image generation) folded into the "because" clause
\textbf{Protocol.} For each unseen arena we adapt the U-Net 200k from its EMA weights with rank-16, $\alpha=16$ LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} on every attention projection of the frozen backbone, the rank Vista uses on a frozen world model~\citep{gao2024vista}.
The control MLP with its position table, the input projection and the noise-bucket embedding train in full (4.2M parameters, 0.49 percent), because in DiT-XL/2 image-generation transfer LoRA alone falls far short of full fine-tuning~\citep{xie2023difffit}.
We train on 8 of the 16 adaptation episodes (learning rate $10^{-4}$, batch 32) and score the live weights at 0 (the frozen model exactly), 250, 500, 1k, 2k and 4k updates, with the decoder frozen, and the directional score and $A$ on the training maps (forgetting) as guards.
% depends (A23, moved out of the text by cut c1): the comparator is conditional: Monday if a card idles, else drop this sentence, Table 3's third column and the Appendix E clause, and state it as a limitation
A full fine-tune of arena 7, the farthest, is the comparator.

% line 3.60 dB and the step-0 range recomputed from the frozen h1 metrics (stock decoder, full frame, pre-Sep-26 episodes); arenas already past half at step 0 today: 9, 11, 13, 15, 16
% depends: tuned SD 1 decoder lines, frozen from step-0 predictions on the training maps before any adapted score (cost-target decision); fresh-set step-0 rescore
\textbf{Cost.} An arena's headline cost is the first grid budget at which $A$ closes half of its own gap to the training maps' advantage $A_\text{home}$ (\prov{3.60}~dB), that is, reaches $\tfrac12(A_0+A_\text{home})$ with $A_0$ its zero-shot advantage; the second crossing is $A_\text{home}$ itself.
Both lines are frozen from step-0 predictions; we also report the value at 4k and the area under the curve~\citep{taylor2009transfer}, and right-censor arenas that never cross.
Every arena starts below $A_\text{home}$ (\prov{0.72 to 2.68}~dB), but \prov{5} already sit above half of it, so the line is per arena ($M$ curves: \appref{app:perarena-adapt}).

% depends: 13-arena U-Net LoRA runs (launch Sep 27 evening; arenas 8, 16, 6, 7 first as the fallback set), score_adapt.py rows and cost, the guards, the full fine-tune comparator on arena 7
% hypothesis: every clause states the expected direction (design page section 5; cost-target decision; LoRA forgetting less, lit-adapters memo)
\textbf{What it takes.} \tbd{} of 13 arenas close half of their gap within \tbd{} updates and \tbd{} reach the home line by 4k (Figure~\ref{fig:adapt}, Table~\ref{tab:cost}); \tbd{} tie copy-last in LPIPS by 4k.
The turn response holds and the training maps lose \tbd{}~dB, consistent with LoRA forgetting less~\citep{biderman2024lora}; the full fine-tune reaches \tbd{}~dB on arena 7 at \tbd{}~dB of forgetting.
Cost tracks the zero-shot advantage (Spearman \tbd{}) more than $D$ (\tbd{}) or the transition distance (\tbd{}), exploratory at $n=13$.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

% sources: related-work-map-2026-09-26.md sections 1 and 2; lit-scene-generalization (GameNGen, DIAMOND, MultiGen, Pathdreamer, Doom agents); lit-adapters (AVID, AdaWorld); lit-transferability (Mensink, Westny); adaptation-literature step 7 (EMA versus live is new); GameNGen numbers from arXiv 2408.14837 v1 and v2 sec. 4.2, read Sep 26
XEWorld holds out two robot embodiments, scored separately, finds held-out error tracking an appearance distance, and fine-tunes on 25 to 75 episodes~\citep{chen2026xeworld}; AVID and AdaWorld adapt world models to a held-out game or environment~\citep{rigter2024avid,gao2025adaworld}; Pathdreamer scores unseen buildings against a nearest-neighbour reprojection~\citep{koh2021pathdreamer}.
None scores per scene against persistence; Doom map holdouts have been for agents~\citep{lample2017arnold,wydmuch2018vizdoom}.
Dataset distances predict fine-tuned outcomes~\citep{alvarezmelis2020otdd,nguyen2025sotdd}; in two studies a directed measure, nearest-source coverage~\citep{mensink2021factors} or latent KL~\citep{westny2026latent}, predicted transfer better than a symmetric transport distance.
We found no measurement of the cost of crossing from one world model to many targets, nor of EMA against live weights in closed loop.
