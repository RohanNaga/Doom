\begin{abstract}
Neural game engines such as GameNGen and MultiGen simulate Doom with a Stable Diffusion U-Net, while the wider video and world-model literature has moved to Diffusion Transformers (DiTs). No controlled comparison of the two backbones exists for action-conditioned game simulation. We train a DiT-XL/2 and the Stable Diffusion 1.4 U-Net on the same lossless 320$\times$240 Doom recordings, the same latents, the same 32-frame context, action conditioning, noise augmentation, velocity objective, optimizer, and step budget, on 16\,GB GPUs, and compare them teacher-forced and autoregressively: per-frame PSNR and LPIPS against lossless frames, drift over 64-step rollouts, FVD, and action-following accuracy measured by an inverse dynamics model. We also release the recordings: every engine tic of 850 deathmatch episodes on 17 maps played by the Arnold agent, with actions, button states, HUD state, and player pose. \textbf{[Result sentence.]}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A neural game engine replaces a rendering and physics loop with a generative model that, given the recent frames and the player's action, produces the next frame. GameNGen \citep{valevski2024gamengen} showed this works for Doom with a Stable Diffusion U-Net, and MultiGen \citep{po2026multigen} extended the same U-Net to editable multi-map worlds with an external memory. In the same two years, video generation and the newest interactive world models moved to Diffusion Transformers \citep{peebles2023dit,oasis2024,he2025matrixgame,nfd2025}. Whether that move pays off for an action-conditioned game simulator has never been measured directly: every published comparison crosses papers, and therefore crosses datasets, resolutions, context lengths, augmentation schemes, and compute budgets at once.

We isolate the backbone. Both models see the same lossless 320$\times$240 Doom recordings, the same frozen VAE latents, the same 32-frame stacked context and single-action conditioning, the same GameNGen-style noise augmentation, the same velocity objective, optimizer, batch size, and step budget, and are evaluated with the same sampler, the same fine-tuned decoder, and the same held-out episodes. The only difference is the network in the middle: DiT-XL/2 warm-started from ImageNet, or the Stable Diffusion 1.4 U-Net warm-started from its release weights, each conditioned through its native pathway. We deliberately match compute rather than parameter count, because these are the published sizes that come with usable pretrained weights, and a practitioner choosing a backbone chooses between them as they exist.

Three things come out of this. First, a measured answer to the backbone question at a budget of tens of GPU-hours on 16\,GB cards, both teacher-forced and over 64-step autoregressive rollouts, with an action-following metric that catches a model that renders plausibly but ignores the controls. Second, a small-compute recipe with released weights, including the observation that the frozen Stable Diffusion decoder reconstructs Doom at only 23.7\,dB and the HUD at 17.9\,dB, so decoder fine-tuning is a precondition for any meaningful pixel metric. Third, the recordings themselves: every engine tic of 850 deathmatch episodes on 17 maps, lossless, with actions, button states, HUD state, and player pose, played by the Arnold agent \citep{lample2017arnold} that MultiGen also uses. No comparable public dataset exists; the ones that do are JPEG-compressed, downscaled to 60$\times$80, or confined to one arena.
\section{Related work}
\textbf{Diffusion game engines.} GameNGen \citep{valevski2024gamengen}, DIAMOND \citep{alonso2024diamond}, Oasis \citep{oasis2024}, MultiGen \citep{po2026multigen}, PlayGen \citep{playgen2024}. \textbf{DiT world models at scale.} Matrix-Game \citep{he2025matrixgame}, Hunyuan-GameCraft, Genie~3, Next-Frame Diffusion \citep{nfd2025}. \textbf{Controlled single-variable studies.} Echo-Memory \citep{echomemory2026} varies memory with the backbone fixed; Nano World Models \citep{nanowm2026} ablates scale and action injection; Po et al.\ \citep{po2025ssm} compare sequence mixers at matched parameters. None varies the backbone. \textbf{Evaluation.} MineWorld's IDM metric \citep{mineworld2025}, GameWorld Score \citep{he2025matrixgame}, iWorld-Bench \citep{iworldbench2026}, and long-horizon stability benchmarks \citep{worldroam2026} motivate our autoregressive protocol.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}
\textbf{Recording.} We run ViZDoom \citep{kempka2016vizdoom} deathmatch on the 17 maps of the Arnold WAD with 8 built-in bots and 150 game-seconds per episode, rendering 320$\times$240 RGB with the HUD, weapon, and crosshair. The player is the Arnold agent \citep{lample2017arnold}, the 2017 competition track-2 checkpoint used unmodified, with its own decision preamble (favourite-weapon switching and an anti-stuck turn); it decides every 4 tics and the engine holds the action in between. Every engine tic (35 per second) is stored losslessly with the action applied from that tic, the full button state, health, ammo, kills, deaths, frags, and player position and angle: 850 episodes, 4.21M tics, 33.4 hours of play, 50 episodes per map, 202\,GB.

\textbf{Why lossless, and why this resolution.} The public Doom sets are JPEG quality 85 or smaller. Re-encoding our frames at quality 85 gives 31.4\,dB PSNR against the original and 26.2\,dB on the HUD rows, so any PSNR computed against them is bounded by compression before the model enters. GameNGen trains and evaluates at 320$\times$240; a comparison at any other resolution is not one.

\textbf{Training view and split.} We take one frame per agent decision (stride 4), so the model predicts the next decision frame from the previous 32. Under this stride the copy-last-frame baseline scores 19.7\,dB on held-out windows, against 26.2\,dB on per-tic data, which is why the stride matters: at per-tic resolution a next-frame model is rewarded for doing nothing. The split is by episode: 10\% of episodes on maps 1--15 are held out, and maps 16 and 17 are held out entirely as unseen maps.
\section{Method}
\textbf{Latents.} Frames are padded from 320$\times$240 to 320$\times$256 and encoded once with the frozen \texttt{sd-vae-ft-mse} encoder to $4\times32\times40$ latents (scale 0.18215), the latent family both warm starts were trained on. The decoder is fine-tuned on 50k training frames, following GameNGen \citep{valevski2024gamengen}, because the frozen decoder reconstructs Doom frames at only 23.7\,dB and the HUD rows at 17.9\,dB (Table~\ref{tab:vae}): without it, no latent model can score above that ceiling regardless of backbone. We fine-tune with an MSE loss and with MSE plus 0.1$\times$LPIPS; the latter keeps most of the PSNR gain while cutting perceptual error five-fold, and every number in this paper is decoded with it. The encoder never changes, so both backbones train on identical latents.

\textbf{Input and conditioning.} The $L=32$ context latents are stacked along channels with the noisy target (132 channels at $32\times40$), as in GameNGen. The conditioning action is the one recorded at the last context frame, which under the recording semantics is the action held between it and the target. Following GameNGen, the context latents are corrupted during training with Gaussian noise at a level drawn uniformly up to 0.7 of the variance-preserving schedule, discretized into 10 buckets and fed as a conditioning id; this is what lets a model tolerate its own errors in autoregressive use.

\textbf{Objective and sampling.} Both backbones predict the velocity $v=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0$ \citep{salimans2022progressive} under a 1,000-step linear schedule, the target MultiGen uses; this also removes the learned-variance head the ImageNet DiT carries, so both models emit four channels. Sampling is DDIM with 50 steps and $\eta=0$, deterministic given the noise, so rollout comparisons reflect the backbones rather than sampler variance.

\textbf{Backbones.} \emph{DiT-XL/2}: 28 blocks, width 1152, 16 heads, patch 2, so 320 tokens per frame; 675M parameters. Warm-started from ImageNet DiT-XL/2-256 with the patch projection inflated (pretrained weights on the four target channels, zeros on the 128 context channels, so step 0 equals the ImageNet model applied to the noisy target), a 2D sin-cos positional embedding rebuilt for the $16\times20$ grid, and the class table replaced by a 29-way action table and a 10-way noise-bucket table summed into the adaLN-Zero vector with the timestep embedding. \emph{SD 1.4 U-Net}: 860M parameters, the input convolution inflated the same way, the action embedded as a single cross-attention token in place of text, the noise bucket through a class embedding added to the timestep embedding. Each backbone keeps its native conditioning pathway; forcing adaLN onto a U-Net or cross-attention onto a DiT would test a modification, not the backbone.

\textbf{Recipe.} Global batch 32, AdamW without weight decay (DiT $10^{-4}$, U-Net $5\times10^{-5}$, each family's published fine-tuning rate), 500 warmup steps, gradient clipping at 1.0, bf16 autocast over fp32 master weights, gradient checkpointing, 90k steps for both, and checkpoint selection by held-out velocity loss every 5k steps. On one 16\,GB A4000 the DiT trains at 0.50 steps/s (11.8\,GB) and the U-Net at 0.30 steps/s (14.8\,GB); the DiT ran on four GPUs and the U-Net on one with gradient accumulation, because DDP's gradient buffers push the U-Net past 16\,GB.
\section{Evaluation}
\textbf{Teacher-forced.} For 2,048 held-out windows the model receives 32 real context frames and the action and samples one frame, scored against the raw lossless frame: PSNR, LPIPS (AlexNet), and PSNR on the HUD crop (bottom 32 rows). Two rows anchor the table: the copy-last-frame baseline, which any model must beat to have learned dynamics, and the VAE ceiling, the score of the ground-truth latent decoded back, which no latent model can exceed.

\textbf{Autoregressive.} From 32 real seed frames and the ground-truth action sequence, the model predicts 64 frames feeding its own output back as context; 256 rollouts per checkpoint. We report PSNR and LPIPS per horizon against ground truth (drift curves), FVD on the predicted 16- and 32-frame clips against real clips, and action following: an inverse dynamics model, a small conv net trained on real consecutive latent pairs, reads the action back from consecutive generated frames, and we report agreement with the conditioning action, overall and on a movement-only collapse of the 29 actions. Its accuracy on real held-out pairs, 69.3\% top-1 and 71.6\% movement-only, is the ceiling of the metric.

\section{Limitations}
Not real time (50 DDIM steps); a fixed 32-frame context with no memory; a single scripted agent's behavior distribution rather than human play; one scenario type. Multi-map generalization is reported but not modeled with memory; MultiGen's map-conditioned memory is the natural follow-up with a DiT observation model.
