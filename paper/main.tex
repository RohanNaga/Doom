\begin{abstract}
% depends: SD 3.5 200k rescore (Monday) for sentence 3's third backbone; sources: results/fresh_rescore/*_tuned (scene crop, fine-tuned decoder), paper/tables/tuned/adapt_summary.json, results/sd35_stability/collapse_rates_50k_130k.md
Game world models are scored on held-out trajectories of their training scenes, and absolute PSNR mostly measures how static the recordings are.
On an open 17-arena Doom benchmark scored against copy-last-frame persistence, we train three pretrained diffusion backbones on four arenas and ask what they keep, lose and relearn on the other thirteen.
Decoded through one decoder, the U-Net still beats copy-last on every unseen arena, but its advantage shrinks from 5.1~dB in distribution to 1.1--2.9~dB (median 2.0) and its LPIPS is worse than persistence's on 13 of 13 arenas, while its turn response survives.
A frame distance to the training recordings separates seen from unseen arenas but does not order the unseen ones, while the frozen model's own zero-shot latent skill score does.
Rank-16 adapters on the frozen backbone, trained on eight episodes of an arena, close half of its gap to the in-distribution advantage within 4k updates on 9 of 13 arenas (five within 500), recover a median 1.8~dB, and turn the perceptual loss into a win on 11 of 13 arenas (the other two within 0.01).
In closed loop, good one-tic scores do not guarantee stability, and the EMA reduces collapses without removing them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A learned game simulator is judged on held-out trajectories of the scenes it trained on~\citep{valevski2024gamengen,alonso2024diamond,he2025matrixgame2}.
We need a world model to generalize to unseen levels before using it to plan or train a policy there.
We ask this for Doom: a model trained on four deathmatch arenas is scored on each of the thirteen other arenas of its WAD, then adapted to each from a few episodes.

% depends: nothing pending; persistence range and Spearman from results/fresh_rescore/unet200k_ema_tuned/map*/metrics.json (scene crop)
The measurement is rare: we found no Doom world model scored on maps it did not train on (MultiGen does not say~\citep{po2026multigen}), and other held-out domains are an embodiment, a game, an environment or a building (Section~\ref{sec:related}).
Absolute PSNR also ranks recordings, not models: on our unseen arenas persistence runs from 18.5 to 22.5~dB on the scene rows, and the model's PSNR follows it.
Video prediction reported a copy-last baseline~\citep{mathieu2016deep,lotter2017prednet,villegas2019fidelity}; game world models dropped it.
We make persistence, the last context frame copied forward and defined on every map without training, the zero line: each transfer score is a paired difference against it on the same windows, read beside the reconstruction upper bound~\citep{zheng2023occworld,karypidis2024dinoforesight}.

% depends: full fine-tune comparator (Monday) for item (3); SD 3.5 200k rescore (Monday) for item (2)'s third backbone
We contribute (0) three action-conditioned latent world models of Doom trained under one next-tic recipe (SD~1.4 U-Net, PixArt-$\alpha$ and SD~3.5 Medium; 32-tic context, executed 19-button controls, $v$-prediction with noise augmentation, 200k updates), a matched decoder fine-tune, and the adapter path, all released with the benchmark (Figure~\ref{fig:method});
(1) an open 17-arena Doom benchmark (link withheld for review) scored per arena against persistence beside the reconstruction upper bound, with a directional check and closed-loop rollouts;
(2) the measurement that off the training maps the decoded advantage shrinks and the perceptual margin changes sign for all three backbones while the turn response survives (Figures~\ref{fig:teaser} and~\ref{fig:shift}, Table~\ref{tab:unseen});
(3) per-arena adaptation curves for rank-16 adapters, with the budget to close half of each arena's gap to the in-distribution advantage (median 1k updates; 9 of 13 by 4k), a data ladder showing one episode recovers most of the gain, and a full fine-tune comparator (\tbd{}); and
(4) two negative results: neither a per-frame distance nor two transition-level distances order the arenas by zero-shot advantage or adaptation budget, whereas the frozen model's zero-shot latent skill does (Spearman 0.90 with the advantage at 4k, exploratory at $n=13$), and one-tic quality does not guarantee closed-loop stability.

\section{Benchmark and recipe}
\label{sec:bench}

\textbf{Data.} Every episode is ViZDoom~\citep{kempka2016vizdoom} deathmatch on one of the 17 arenas of the Arnold agent's WAD~\citep{lample2017arnold} with Freedoom assets: Arnold against 8 bots for 150 game-seconds, recorded losslessly at every tic (one game step, 35~Hz, 320$\times$240 with the HUD) with the 19-button control the engine executed (\appref{app:protocol}).
The models train on arenas 2 to 5 (500 episodes each) and are scored there on held-out validation episodes (512 windows).
Each unseen arena (1, 6 to 17) has 24 newly recorded episodes under one pinned protocol, 16 for adaptation and 8 held out, with 32 fixed windows per held-out episode.

\textbf{Three backbones, one recipe.} We fine-tune three text-to-image diffusion models into next-tic world models: the SD~1.4 U-Net~\citep{rombach2022ldm} (860M parameters, 4-channel SD~1.x latents), PixArt-$\alpha$~\citep{chen2023pixart} (a 628M DiT in the same latents) and SD~3.5 Medium~\citep{esser2024sd3} (a 2.27B MMDiT with its own 16-channel latents).
Backbone and latent space change together, so the backbones compare pretrained packages.
The 32 context latents are stacked on channels with the noisy target, and the 32 executed controls enter as tokens.
The recipe is shared: $v$-prediction~\citep{salimans2022progressive}, context noise augmentation up to 0.7 in 10 buckets as in GameNGen~\citep{valevski2024gamengen}, AdamW at $5\times10^{-5}$, batch 32, 200k updates, an fp32 EMA at 0.9999, and 10-step DDIM~\citep{song2021ddim} (\appref{app:protocol}).
Rollouts stay in latent space, so the decoder only renders.
We fine-tune the SD~1 decoder on training-map frames from their true latents with the encoder frozen, using MSE plus 0.1~LPIPS, then freeze it; every pixel number in Sections~\ref{sec:shift} and~\ref{sec:adapt} uses this decoder unless marked stock.
An MSE-only objective sharpens the HUD but triples scene LPIPS (\appref{app:perarena}).

\textbf{What we measure.} Let $x$ be the true next frame, $x_\text{last}$ the last context frame, $z$ and $z_\text{last}$ their latents, $\hat z$ the prediction and $D$ the decoder.
The \emph{decoded advantage} $A=\mathrm{PSNR}(D(\hat z),D(z))-\mathrm{PSNR}(D(z_\text{last}),D(z))$, the PSNR gain over persistence, puts the model and copy-last through the same decoder; it is averaged over windows.
It is a paired difference on the same frame, as Genie's $\Delta$PSNR is between inferred and random actions~\citep{bruce2024genie}.
The \emph{perceptual margin} $M=\mathrm{LPIPS}(D(\hat z),x)-\mathrm{LPIPS}(x_\text{last},x)$~\citep{zhang2018lpips} is taken against the raw true frame on both sides; below zero the model looks better than copying.
The \emph{gap} $G=\mathrm{PSNR}(D(z),x)-\mathrm{PSNR}(D(\hat z),x)$ is the decoder's own reconstruction upper bound minus the model's PSNR, both against the raw frame.
The \emph{zero-shot latent skill score} $S_0$ is the same comparison as $A$ in latents: $10\log_{10}$ of persistence's latent MSE over the model's.
The \emph{directional} score is the fraction of turning windows whose predicted image shift reverses when left and right are swapped in the newest control (true frames: 0.885 on the training maps, 0.892 on the arenas).
Pixel quantities use the scene rows 0 to 207, since persistence copies the HUD almost exactly.
$A_\text{train}$, $A$ on the training maps' validation windows, is the in-distribution reference (5.06~dB, episode-bootstrap interval 4.75 to 5.39), not an arena's own upper bound.

\section{What happens off the training maps}
\label{sec:shift}

% depends: SD 3.5 200k rescore (Monday); sources: results/directional_fresh/summary.json, results/fresh_rescore (script: paper owner's recomputation of the session brief's Table 2 rows), results/family_step/family_step.json
\textbf{Where the deficit sits.} In distribution all three backbones beat persistence at one and four tics, within 0.85~dB of each other, and turn the right way (Table~\ref{tab:indomain}).
Off the training maps we read three measures side by side (Table~\ref{tab:unseen}, Figure~\ref{fig:shift}).
The turn response survives: per-arena reversal rates are 0.73 to 0.86 on the unseen arenas for the U-Net (median 0.81, pooled 0.80; 0.69 to 0.90 across the three backbones) against 0.79 to 0.91 on the training maps (pooled 0.86), with the raw-frame reference at 0.89 and 0.89; PixArt and SD~3.5 sit within 0.015 of the U-Net on the arenas.
$A$ drops by about 3~dB (5.06 to a median 2.03) and stays positive on every arena for all three backbones.
The fine-tuned decoder's scene reconstruction PSNR is 28.6~dB in distribution and 25.2 to 29.7~dB on the unseen arenas (median 27.3), while the gap $G$ between that reconstruction and the rendered prediction is 3.4~dB in distribution and 3.7 to 6.9~dB unseen (median 5.0). Decoding the same predictions with the stock and the fine-tuned decoder raises $A$ by 0.2~dB zero-shot ($-0.01$ to $+0.51$) and by 0.7~dB after adaptation (0.5 to 1.1), so the decoder's share of the deficit differs between frozen and adapted latents; every column is reported under both decoders (\appref{app:perarena}).
Nor is the sign reversal of the perceptual margin the decoder's floor: the decoder's own reconstruction LPIPS (0.05 to 0.09 scene) sits far below persistence's (0.18 to 0.28), and the rendered prediction, scored against the raw true frame like persistence, still has higher LPIPS on 13 of 13 arenas (margin $M$ +0.03 to +0.17, median +0.08; in distribution $-$0.08).
The shift is a gap between the training maps and every unseen arena, not a slope: for all three backbones every unseen arena has lower zero-shot latent skill than every training map, and among the arenas that skill does not follow the frame distance $d$ (Figure~\ref{fig:shift}a).

\textbf{The distance orders families, not arenas.} Before scoring any map we froze $d$, the sliced Wasserstein-2 distance between the motion-weighted cloud of a map's per-frame SD~1 latents and that of its nearest training map (\appref{app:distance}); the motion weighting changes $d$ by at most 0.006 and is immaterial.
The training maps' held-out episodes sit inside the train-versus-train floor (0.03 to 0.07 against 0.02 to 0.09; measured on the earlier episode set, \appref{app:distance}) and every unseen arena outside it (0.12 to 0.27), so $d$ separates training maps from unseen arenas; within the 13 unseen arenas it does not order them: Spearman with $A$ $-0.22$, with the adaptation budget $-0.35$, with $A$ after adaptation $+0.10$ ($n = 13$, none below $p = 0.2$), and a leave-one-arena-out linear fit is no better than predicting the mean for $A$ after adaptation or the budget.
Two transition-level distances we registered as alternatives, a directed coverage of transition windows by the training set and a nearest-neighbour transfer gap, fail even that separation: the training maps' held-out episodes overlap the unseen range on both (\appref{app:distance}).
The pre-registered 30-map test, which pooled campaign maps of another WAD, failed its validation gate (\appref{app:distance}); its partial Spearman of $-0.73$ falls to $-0.42$ with family indicators as covariates, so we read it as a pooled family effect, not an ordering.
What does order the arenas is the frozen model's own zero-shot latent skill $S_0$, one evaluation pass on the new map's recordings: it predicts where an arena ends up rather than how much it gains (exploratory Spearman $+0.90$ with $A$ at 4k, $+0.18$ with the gain, $-0.61$ with the observed budgets, arenas that never reach the threshold tied above all others); both persist after adjustment for motion or persistence PSNR, and the U-Net and PixArt zero-shot advantages agree in rank ($+0.96$), so the ordering is shared by two backbones trained on the same latents rather than particular to one.

% depends: nothing pending; collapse counts recomputed from results/sd35_stability/collapse_rates_50k_130k.md
\textbf{Closed loop.} Good one-tic scores do not guarantee stable rollouts: SD~3.5 has the best one-tic numbers, yet at 50k and 70k its non-EMA weights fall into an absorbing state, one latent channel's mean captured and frames blank (\appref{app:closedloop}).
On 16 fixed windows read every 5k updates from 55k to 130k, a frame under 10~dB occurs in 23 of 256 non-EMA and 9 of 256 EMA rollouts (11 and 8 without 70k): the EMA removes checkpoint-specific collapses, not the failure mode.
Both share the sampler, so the few-step sampling DIAMOND blames for drift~\citep{alonso2024diamond} is not what differs between them.

\section{Crossing the shift}
\label{sec:adapt}

% depends: full fine-tune comparator (Monday)
\textbf{Protocol.} For each unseen arena we adapt the U-Net 200k from its EMA weights with rank-16, $\alpha=16$ LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} on every attention projection of the frozen backbone, the rank Vista uses on a frozen world model~\citep{gao2024vista}.
The control MLP with its position table, the input projection and the noise-bucket embedding train in full (4.2M parameters, 0.49 percent), because in DiT-XL/2 image-generation transfer LoRA alone falls far short of full fine-tuning~\citep{xie2023difffit}.
We train on 8 of the 16 adaptation episodes (learning rate $10^{-4}$, batch 32) and score the non-EMA weights at 0 (the frozen model exactly), 250, 500, 1k, 2k and 4k updates, with the decoder frozen, and the directional score and $A$ on the training maps (forgetting) as control checks.
A full fine-tune at the GameNGen rate ($2\times10^{-5}$) on every arena is the comparator (\tbd{}: Monday).

% depends: the 8k grid (tonight) replaces the 4k numbers; sources: paper/tables/tuned/adapt_summary.json
\textbf{Budget.} An arena's headline budget is the first grid point at which $A$ closes half of its own gap to the source model's in-distribution advantage on its training maps, $A_\text{train}$ (5.06~dB, interval 4.75 to 5.39), that is, reaches $\tfrac12(A_0+A_\text{train})$ with $A_0$ its zero-shot advantage; the second threshold is $A_\text{train}$ itself.
Both lines are frozen from zero-update predictions; we also report the value at the final budget of the grid and the area under the curve~\citep{taylor2009transfer}, and right-censor arenas that never reach the threshold.
Every arena starts below $A_\text{train}$ ($A_0$ 1.07 to 2.88~dB), and because the line depends on $A_0$ we do not correlate the budget with $A_0$ itself ($M$ curves: \appref{app:perarena-adapt}).
Half of the in-distribution gap in dB is not half of the error: closing 1.8~dB removes 34 percent of the persistence error, not 28.

% depends: forgetting and directional control checks on all arenas (8k chain tonight), full fine-tune comparator (Monday); sources: paper/tables/tuned/adapt_summary.json, results/adapt (ladder, seeds, recipe: RESEARCH_CONTEXT 2026-09-27 08:05)
\textbf{What it takes.} 9 of 13 arenas close half of their gap within 4k updates (5 within 500; median budget 1k, arena-bootstrap 250 to beyond 4k; arenas 1, 8, 12 and 16 censored), one reaches the in-distribution line, and the median $A$ rises from 2.04 to 3.80~dB (Figure~\ref{fig:adapt}, Table~\ref{tab:cost}); on average 69 percent of the gain lands by 250 updates, and every curve still rises between 2k and 4k (+0.05 to +0.31~dB, mean +0.13).
Adaptation also closes the perceptual gap: at 4k the rendered prediction has lower scene LPIPS than raw persistence on 11 of 13 arenas, the other two within 0.01 ($M$ median $-0.02$ against $+0.08$ zero-shot), and the median gap $G$ to the decoder's own reconstruction falls from 5.0 to 3.4~dB, the in-distribution value (3.4).
Little of the gain needs data: in stock-decoder checks a single adaptation episode finishes within about 0.25~dB of sixteen on arenas 7, 8, 12 and 16 (75 to 101 percent of the sixteen-episode gain, \appref{app:perarena-adapt}), and tripling or quintupling the learning rate or extending training to 8k changes $A$ at 4k by less than 0.06~dB on arenas 12 and 16, inside the two-seed spread of 0.02 to 0.06~dB; on these checks the level an arena reaches looks set by the arena rather than by the budget, the rate or the data.
The budget tracks the zero-shot latent skill (Spearman $-0.61$) and not $d$ ($-0.35$) or the transition distances, exploratory at $n=13$ with censored budgets; the forgetting and directional control checks on all arenas and the full fine-tune comparator are \tbd{} (the checks land with the 8k rerun tonight, the comparator Monday).

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

XEWorld holds out two robot embodiments, finds their error tracking an appearance distance, and fine-tunes on 25 to 75 episodes~\citep{chen2026xeworld}; AVID and AdaWorld adapt to a held-out game or environment~\citep{rigter2024avid,gao2025adaworld}; Pathdreamer scores unseen buildings against a reprojection baseline~\citep{koh2021pathdreamer}.
None scores per scene against persistence; Doom map holdouts have been for agents~\citep{lample2017arnold,wydmuch2018vizdoom}.
Dataset distances predict fine-tuned outcomes~\citep{alvarezmelis2020otdd,nguyen2025sotdd}, and directed measures~\citep{mensink2021factors,westny2026latent} do so better than symmetric transport distances.
We found no per-target measurement of a world model's adaptation budget, nor of EMA against non-EMA weights in closed loop.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:limits}

Persistence is weak on fight recordings; adaptation is scored one tic ahead; one training seed per arena (two on four arenas), one agent, one WAD, 10 sampling steps; every claim is about these 13 arenas.
Off its training maps the U-Net keeps its turn response and part of its advantage over copying and loses its perceptual advantage; eight episodes and 4k rank-16 adapter updates (1.1 GPU-hours) close half of the gap on 9 of 13 arenas (arena-bootstrap 46 to 92 percent) and restore the perceptual advantage on 11, and in our checks one episode gives most of that.
