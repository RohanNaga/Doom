2. The paper and the single-dataset rule

The paper (base document: RESEARCH_CONTEXT.md section 0): an evaluation paper. Three backbones under one recipe, trained on four deathmatch arenas, beat copy-last on held-out episodes of those maps by 3 to 4 dB in the decoded comparison and by only 0.7 to 2.7 dB on the 13 arenas they never saw; against raw-frame persistence the decoder's reconstruction floor turns that shrinkage into a loss on static-footage arenas (reframed Sep 26 19:10 EDT after the Opus distance review, verified: the earlier 'sign flip' headline was the decoder floor showing through); the turn response survives; one-step quality does not predict closed-loop stability, which the EMA reduces but does not remove. The distance to the training footage separates families of maps but, as of tonight, does not order the arenas (section 4).

The rule (Rohan, Sep 26 evening). One dataset, one recording protocol, one set of distances, one set of results. The paper's evaluation set is the 17 arenas of Arnold's deathmatch WAD: training maps 2 to 5 scored on their 25 held-out validation episodes, and the 13 unseen arenas scored on the fresh 20-episode set. Everything the paper reports on the unseen arenas (zero-shot PSNR, LPIPS, latent skill, directional score, the distance D, the adaptation curves) is computed on that one set. The earlier recording batches and the campaign maps stay in the dataset release and out of the paper. "Here is the data, here is what we did, here is why."

The cohesive evaluation (three views converged, `.claude/analyses/evaluation-cohesion-decision-2026-09-26.md`). Two scales, two jobs: latent skill (decoder-free, in dB against copy-last) is the primary inside one backbone, for the unseen arenas and the adaptation curves; pixels through the tuned decoder against raw persistence are the primary for the table that compares the three backbones, because their latent spaces differ. Both decoders are scored on the same predictions once, with per-window rows (MSEs, four decoders, full frame and scene-only without the HUD, duplicate flags, fp16 predicted latents, one and four tics) so no table is rerun. The headline, corrected twice tonight and verified: the model's skill over copy-last shrinks off the training maps in every space; in squared error it stays positive at one tic on all 13 unseen arenas; perceptually it flips on every unseen arena, decoder or not, because the model blurs under uncertainty; raw-PSNR sign changes track motion, not the shift; the HUD carries 39 to 68 percent of the stock decoder's floor, so scene-only columns are required. The scorer additions are being built now.

On the metric. All three views agree: the paired PSNR difference against copy-last on the same windows is a skill score against a persistence reference, not a new metric. The table prints absolute model, copy-last and reconstruction rows (the OccWorld layout, arXiv 2311.16038); figure 1 plots the paired difference with axes reading "model minus persistence PSNR" and "persistence minus model LPIPS"; cite PredNet (arXiv 1605.08104, Copy Last Frame on an unseen dataset) for the practice and Genie's delta-PSNR for the form only. One method sentence, no name.

3. The one dataset

What all 17 maps share. Freedoom assets, ViZDoom engine and physics, Arnold with 8 bots, 320×240 frames with HUD, every tic recorded (35 Hz), 150 game-seconds per episode (about 5,000 one-tic windows). Arenas are compact deathmatch layouts; the agent fights, dies 3 to 12 times per episode, and the footage is violent (one-tic persistence 19 to 23 dB). What differs between arenas is the texture mix and the geometry, and, as it turns out, mostly how static each arena's footage is.

Protocol. Same Arnold build and checkpoint, 8 bots at the same skill, 150 game-seconds, every tic, seeds declared in the corpus README before recording and disjoint from every earlier corpus, 328 episodes recorded in all, the 16 workers' first episodes set aside so each arena keeps 24 (Arnold's weapon switch executes only there), encoded with the pinned encoder and canonical controls. 4. The distance question (second three-way done, Sep 27 06:00)

Result on the fresh set (`results/transition_distance/compare_fresh_sd1.*`, `results/distance_v2/`, `tools/distance_outcome_corr.py`). Against the 13 arenas' zero-shot advantage, endpoint at 4,000, gain and half-gap budget: D −0.24 / +0.06 / +0.42 / −0.27; directed coverage −0.17 / +0.12 / +0.26 / −0.22; transfer gap +0.02 / +0.02 / +0.12 / −0.01 (Spearman, n = 13, no p below 0.15); leave-one-arena-out fits are no better than predicting the mean; partialling out motion or persistence changes nothing. The family floor: D passes (training maps' validation D 0.026 to 0.069, unseen 0.118 to 0.270); coverage (training 0.52 to 0.61, unseen 0.53 to 0.61) and the transfer gap (training 0.005 to 0.012, unseen 0.009 to 0.038) fail. The zero-shot latent skill predicts the endpoint (+0.89) and the budget (−0.65) and halves the leave-one-out error, and PixArt's zero-shot advantage ranks the arenas the same way (+0.95).

Why the transition metrics failed (Opus, Astra): they mostly track motion (persistence PSNR −0.69 and +0.63, which is why they disagree with each other at −0.53); the transfer gap's neighbour predictor is worse than copy-last even on the training maps; shuffling the next-frame change moves coverage by 0.004 to 0.010 against a spread of 0.087 between arenas. In a fixed-engine game every map's transitions live on one manifold and a window's nearest neighbour is in its own episode; what differs between maps is whether the model's prediction rule transfers, which is a property of the model.

The paper: D as the family separator (figure 2 left); the ordering gate set before the runs stated as failed; coverage and the transfer gap in one sentence; the outcome scatter against the zero-shot skill (`fig2c_outcomes_by_skill`); no "distance predicts cost" claim. October: a constructed-shift experiment (retextured and re-laid-out training maps plus generated arenas chosen by distance strata before any outcome is read, two seeds, sealed test maps; 40 to 270 A4000-hours depending on scale) and one next-version metric (section 0 A).

7. Risks to name in the paper

  • The arena distance range is narrow (0.115 to 0.282, arena 7 two thirds of it) and the frame distance does not order arenas; the cost-versus-distance test on 13 arenas is exploratory and its x axis may be a transition distance chosen before the adaptation runs.
  • Arena footage is fights, so the persistence reference is weak and the model's gain depends on how static each arena's footage is; motion is reported per map and controlled for.
  • The pilot is four maps and two seeds on the fresh split; it is a preliminary figure, not the claim.
  • SD 3.5 numbers are provisional until its 200k rescore on Monday.
