Crossing the map shift: the LoRA adaptation study

Every parameter and decision for the experiment, with a recommendation and the alternatives, for the two of you to go through. Decisions are marked decide; the rest are facts from the data on disk (Sep 26 2026, 21:00 EDT).

0. The claim and the curve

Claim. A world model pretrained on four maps can be adapted to an unseen map with a small adapter on frozen backbone weights and a few episodes of that map, and the training-to-map distance predicts how much adaptation the map needs.

Headline figure. x = distance D of the map to the nearest training map; y = adaptation cost, the LoRA steps (at fixed data) or the episodes (at fixed steps) needed to bring the map's gain over persistence up to a target, for example half the training-map gain. One point per map, coloured by backbone. Supporting figures: the raw curves (gain against steps, gain against episodes) for maps near, middle and far.

Outcome per curve point, on held-out episodes of the same map, the same 256 windows every time: one-tic PSNR and LPIPS gain over persistence, the decoder-free latent ratio, and two guards: the directional score on that map (did control response survive adaptation) and the training-map validation gain (how much was forgotten). The distance definition can be refined later without touching any of this; the maps' order barely changes across the three spaces (rank agreement 0.86 to 0.90).

1. Datasets (settled Sep 26 evening by three independent views: main, Astra, Opus 5.5)

Two sets, as the paper names them. Training maps: arenas 2, 3, 4, 5 (Arnold's own training maps; 500 episodes each trained on; 25 held-out validation episodes each). Unseen maps: the 13 other deathmatch arenas of Arnold's full_deathmatch.wad (1, 6 to 17; the file holds MAP01 to 17 and nothing else) and the 13 Freedoom campaign maps. Adaptation targets are the 13 unseen arenas only. The campaign maps stay zero-shot: on 8 of 13 their disjoint-episode footage samples disagree by 11 to 29 percent, so a few episodes do not cover the level and a curve there would measure coverage, not adaptation. Same engine, same Freedoom assets, same agent (Arnold with 8 bots), same 320×240 frame with HUD on every map.

A fresh set, one protocol. Every target arena gets 20 new episodes recorded under one pinned protocol: the same Arnold build and checkpoint, 8 bots at the same skill, 150 game-seconds (about 5,000 one-tic windows) per episode, every tic stored, seeds declared in advance and disjoint from every earlier corpus, encoded with the pinned encoder. Each recording worker's first episode is discarded by design (Arnold's weapon-preference switch executes only there). Recording: 273 episodes, about 1 h of CPU on Spiderman at low priority, about 65 GB of raw frames; SD 1 encoding about 4.5 A6000-hours. The episodes that exist today stay what they are: the source of the frozen distance D and of the published zero-shot points. Nothing that trains the adapter or scores it touches them, which removes any leakage between D and the held-out set.

Adapt and held-out only, no validation split. Nothing is selected on the held-out episodes: the step grid is fixed, there is no early stopping, and the cost thresholds are pre-registered, so a third split would sit unused. If a later analysis picks anything by looking at scores (a threshold, a best checkpoint), it must carve that out of the 12 adaptation episodes, never out of the 8 held-out ones. The training maps' own 25 validation episodes serve the forgetting guard.

Nice numbers a reader should see. One training map received about 1.6M samples in pretraining (200,000 updates × 32 over four maps); one adaptation run sees 64,000, or 4 percent of that, drawn from 1.2 percent of a training map's episode count, in 15 minutes of play. The whole 13-arena step curve (13 maps × 2,000 updates) costs 13 percent of one pretraining run.

Known limit, stated in the paper. The arenas span D 0.115 to 0.282 and arena 7 carries two thirds of that range; within the 13 the zero-shot gain does not track D (Spearman +0.01). Thirteen points over this range give about 40 percent power to detect the cost relation, so the arena curve is an exploratory test of "D predicts cost"; the October fix is distance-stratified Obsidian-generated arenas chosen before any result.

Decision 1 · the per-map split settled12 adapt (nested 1, 2, 4, 6, 12) / 8 held out on every arena; 32 fixed windows per held-out episode; step 0 rescored on them. Three views converged (Astra's split adopted over main's 6/4 top-up and Opus's 8/12 because the data curve to 12 episodes is worth more than 4 extra held-out episodes).
Decision 2 · which maps in the pilot settledArenas 8 (near), 16 (middle), 6 (middle), 7 (far), U-Net, two seeds, on the episodes that exist today (20, 10, 20, 20), kept out of the headline as a pilot.

4. Evaluation of each curve point

  • Primary: one-tic PSNR and LPIPS gain over persistence on the map's held-out windows (the same 256 windows as the zero-shot score); the decoder-free latent ratio beside it.
  • Adaptation cost: the first step count at which the gain reaches the target; the target is half the training-map gain (about +0.5 dB PSNR, +0.013 LPIPS gain), reported at 25, 50 and 75 percent; Astra's alternative (decision 7) is the decoder-free latent skill ratio R = model latent error over copy-last latent error, cost = first budget with R ≤ 0.90, with R ≤ 1 and R ≤ 0.8 as sensitivities, frozen before the pilot. Maps that never cross are right-censored, never assigned the last step. Report the curve, not only the crossing.
  • Guards: the directional check on the map after adaptation (control response kept); the training-map validation gain after adaptation (forgetting); both at the 2,000-step point at least.
  • Secondary, subset: four-tic gain and a 16-rollout 256-tic read against copy-seed at the final point on the pilot maps.
  • Statistics: per-map bootstrap over held-out episodes for the gain; the cost-against-distance relation reported as Spearman with a bootstrap over maps, the same form as the zero-shot study; the family control stays.
Decision 7 · the adaptation-cost target decideRecommended: half the training-map gain, in both PSNR and LPIPS, plus the curve itself.
Alternative: "gain above zero" (beat persistence), which most near maps may reach at 250 steps and far maps never within 2,000.

6. Risks to name in the paper

  • The seeded agent explores rather than plays, and campaign footage is near-static on several maps; adaptation on 6 episodes of such footage tests a narrow slice of the map.
  • The 4-episode seeded maps cannot be split and stay zero-shot.
  • A curve on 18 maps with one seed is noisy; the pilot's two seeds size that noise before the full run.
  • Distance is the SD 1.x latent distance frozen on Sep 25; if the definition changes later the maps' order barely moves, but the x axis of the final figure must be the frozen one or a re-frozen one, stated.
