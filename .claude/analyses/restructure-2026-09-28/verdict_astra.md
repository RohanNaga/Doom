1. **Pick:** A

2. **Why, in five sentences at most.**

A's Section 4 retains the episode-ladder finding, “one episode already gives most of the gain of sixteen,” giving the adaptation study a data-budget result that B's “updates, episodes, parameters” paragraph omits.
A's full-fine-tuning comparison in Section 4 explains the practical tradeoff with measured time and hardware, whereas B gives the 205-fold parameter ratio without explaining that full fine-tuning actually finishes sooner on the larger GPU.
A's sentence “read on the same grid, the U-Net's median budget is also 250” prevents the 150-versus-250-update comparison from suggesting an architectural difference that the measurement grid cannot establish.
A also retains the in-distribution forgetting result in Section 4, making the recovery claim assessable alongside its cost to previously learned scenes.
In Section 1, A gives “a world model moved to a new map keeps playing the game but draws the wrong world” its own summary sentence, while B buries the same wording in a sentence with two colons, so A better preserves the author's empirical hook and its emphasis on what fails and recovers.

3. **Where the other draft is better.**

- **B, Section 3, “Measurements”:** Take across “A map's excess gap is how much further below this bound the model sits there than on the training maps.” This supplies a useful definition before the budget is introduced and avoids A's later definition that hard-codes the U-Net's 3.4 dB reference.
- **B, Section 3, “Adaptation as a measurement”:** The heading and opening, “To measure how much adaptation a map needs, we keep the trained model frozen and train a standard adapter,” explicitly identify adaptation as the experimental instrument. This serves the professor's requested framing without surrendering the empirical contribution.
- **B, Section 3, “Maps and data”:** “16 for adaptation (8 used here)” and “256 windows per map” resolve ambiguities in A's allocation and evaluation-unit descriptions; keep those clarifications, subject to reconciling the collection/release totals below.
- **B, Section 3, “Three backbones, one recipe”:** “changes the architecture at similar scale” is more accurate than A's “at fixed scale” for 628M versus 860M parameters.
- **B, Section 4:** Separating the cross-backbone result from the decoder diagnostic makes the scientific argument easier to inspect. Adopt that paragraph organization, but soften “The same loss in every backbone” and “The fault is in the model, not the renderer” as described below.

4. **What a skeptical reviewer would attack in your pick.**

The title asks how much adaptation world models need, but the principal evidence is one-tic prediction in one game, mostly one seed, and eight episodes; the four-map episode ladder needs enough detail to support the data-efficiency claim.
The reviewer can challenge whether a turn-reversal score establishes preserved control and whether three differently pretrained backbones isolate architecture or scale.
They can also challenge the reconstruction reference as a true upper bound and ask whether choosing the 4k “elbow” on the scored maps makes the reported budget optimistic.

5. **Anything wrong in either draft.**

- **Both, Section 3, reconstruction “upper bound”:** Decoding an encoded ground-truth frame gives a reconstruction reference, not a proven maximum PSNR over all possible predicted latents; another latent can in principle reconstruct that frame better. B's “the best a prediction rendered through that decoder can score” is especially categorical. Call this an empirical reconstruction reference, or justify the bound, and define excess-gap recovery relative to it.
- **Both, Section 1 and decoder discussion; B, Section 4, “The fault is in the model, not the renderer”:** Table 1's SD 1 reconstruction score falls from 28.55 to 27.32 dB, a 1.23 dB change alongside the prediction's 2.90 dB change. This supports an additional prediction deficit beyond reconstruction degradation, not exclusive attribution to the world model. Differences of aggregate PSNR values also do not give an additive causal decomposition of pixel error.
- **Both, abstract, contributions and Section 4, preserved controls:** The directional scores decline by 0.051, 0.034 and 0.040, and the test checks turn reversal rather than the correctness of all action effects or sustained gameplay. “Keeps playing the game,” “not control,” and A's “What the model keeps is how actions act on the world” exceed that evidence. Describe substantial retained turn sensitivity and label the firing/forward examples qualitative.
- **Both, Section 1, architecture/scale attribution; B, Section 4, “neither ... changes what the shift costs”:** Similar degradation across three backbones does not establish independence from architecture or scale. The drops are not identical, uncertainty is not reported, and architecture, pretraining and latent representation change together. A's Section 4 “looks like a property of the task” is better calibrated than B's categorical wording, but the shared contribution still needs qualification.
- **A, Section 3, “Adaptation,” final sentence:** The definition uses “the training maps' 3.4 dB” without restricting this to the U-Net/PixArt; SD 3.5's reference is approximately 6.5 dB in Table 1. Specify each backbone's own in-distribution gap, as A's Table 2 caption already does. **B, Section 3, adaptation paragraph:** “update counts from 50 to 8k” and the 8k check appear to apply to all models, although the transformer grids stop at 4k and SD 3.5 starts at 250; qualify the grid and one-GPU-hour cost by backbone.
- **A, Section 1, “fine-tuning a separate model per map is prohibitively costly”:** Section 4 reports a full fine-tune taking 0.8 hours per map on the A6000. The memory requirement and cumulative deployment costs may motivate adapters, but the supplied comparison does not substantiate “prohibitively.”
- **A, Section 4, forgetting sentence:** “the training maps' scene PSNR ... falls on all 13 unseen maps” conflates evaluation maps with adapter identities. Say that training-map PSNR falls after each of the 13 map-specific adaptations, and identify which windows the following directional-score increase uses. Keep the stated stock-decoder/decoded-target qualification because this is a different scoring protocol from the principal results.
- **Both, Section 3, data descriptions:** “never trained on” should mean excluded from backbone pretraining, since these maps are subsequently used for adaptation. A's “using 16 for adaptation” conflicts with its later eight-episode description unless this means an available pool; B makes that distinction. A's 2,412 episodes at 150 seconds each imply 100.5 hours and about 12.66 million tics, rather than 96 hours and 12.1 million frames, unless episodes end early or frames are excluded. B instead describes a larger release of 8,000 training-map episodes plus 312 unseen-map episodes; distinguish the experimental subset from the released collection explicitly rather than treating the two totals as interchangeable.
- **B, Section 4, “Parameters matter somewhat more”:** More than which intervention, by which measure? B omits the episode ladder needed to make the implied comparison interpretable. State the concrete full-fine-tuning improvement instead. **B, Section 5:** Failure of two tested predictors does not establish that “a budget has to be measured, not predicted”; restrict the conclusion to those predictors.
- **Both, reported recovery percentages:** These are medians of per-map recovery fractions, not fractions computed from the displayed medians; for example, (0.303 - 0.210)/(0.303 - 0.158) is about 64%, not 72%. This is not itself an arithmetic error, but add the per-map formula and consistently state the aggregation, especially around B's abbreviated transformer percentages.
- **Artifact inconsistencies, excluded from the pick:** A's PDF title is “How Much Adaptation Do World Models Need? A Study of Domain Shifts in Doom,” while its TeX title is “DoomShift: How Much Adaptation Do World Models Need Under Domain Shift?” B's PDF page 4 contains a “pending” placeholder for Figure 3, while A's PDF contains the plot. Both PDFs retain “[tbd]” acknowledgments. These need production fixes; the judgment above concerns the body prose, without using page length or the shared title, contributions and related-work paragraph as differentiators.
