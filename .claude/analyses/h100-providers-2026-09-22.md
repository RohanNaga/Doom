# H100 rental providers — evidence card, read 2026-09-22

Decision: rent one 8×H100 node for ~5 days (Sep 22–27 2026, 120 h = 960 GPU-hours) to run two
diffusion world-model trainings concurrently (SD 1.4 U-Net 0.86 B, batch 32; SD 3.5 Medium 2.25 B,
batch 32 + grad checkpointing), a VAE encode of ~10 M frames at 320×256, and evaluation.
Budget target $2,500–$3,500. Data: public HF dataset, 0.6 TB needed first, 2.75 TB total, must land
on local NVMe. Checkpoints ~0.4 TB. PyTorch 2.x + diffusers, one env, no Slurm.

Every number below carries its source URL. All pages read 2026-09-22 unless noted.
Anything I could not confirm from a primary page is marked **not stated** or **VERIFY**.

## 2. Per-provider detail

### Lambda — [lambda.ai/pricing](https://lambda.ai/pricing), [docs.lambda.ai](https://docs.lambda.ai/public-cloud/on-demand/)

- **Price**: $3.99/GPU-hr for the 8×H100 SXM instance; $4.29 for 1×, $4.19 for 2×, $4.09 for 4×.
  H100 PCIe 1× is $3.29.
- **Node spec (8×)**: 80 GB VRAM/GPU, 208 vCPU, 1,800 GiB RAM, **22 TiB SSD**. That one number
  settles the data problem: the full 2.75 TB dataset plus 0.4 TB of checkpoints fits on the
  instance's own volume with room to spare.
- **1-Click Clusters**: $6.16/GPU-hr at 16 GPUs, minimum term **2 weeks** — wrong shape for a
  5-day run and 54% more per GPU-hour. Ignore it.
- **Minimum commitment**: none stated for on-demand. Billing granularity for instances is **not
  stated** on the docs page I read; filesystems are "billed per GB used per month, in increments
  of one hour".
- **Access**: VM, SSH; the docs reference "the ubuntu user's home directory", so the login user is
  `ubuntu` with sudo, not root. Bare metal is Private Cloud, not on-demand.
- **Persistent storage**: filesystem price is **not stated** on the pricing page. The billing doc
  shows "$0.20 per GiB per month" and explicitly says "The rate above is used for example purposes
  and might not reflect current pricing"
  ([docs.lambda.ai/public-cloud/billing](https://docs.lambda.ai/public-cloud/billing/)). Treat as
  VERIFY in console.
- **Egress**: "There is no minimum storage period, and you are not charged for ingress or egress."
  (same billing doc). Inbound speed from HF: **not stated**.
- **Billing**: card on file, US regions only.
- **Availability risk**: Lambda H100 inventory has historically sold out at peak; third-party
  reviews in 2026 still list "availability constraints during peak demand" and no spot tier
  ([thundercompute.com/blog/lambda-labs-alternatives](https://www.thundercompute.com/blog/lambda-labs-alternatives),
  secondary). Check the console before committing.
- **Academic**: Lambda Research Grant Program, up to **$5,000** in cloud credits, rolling
  competitive applications, expanded at CVPR 2026 to cover research groups (secondary:
  [grantedai.com](https://grantedai.com/grants/lambda-research-grant-program-cloud-gpu-credits-ai-ml-researchers-lambda-a7b8c9d0)).
  No same-day path stated.

### RunPod — [runpod.io/pricing](https://www.runpod.io/pricing)

- **Price**: Secure Cloud H100 SXM $3.49, H100 NVL $3.19, H100 PCIe $2.89. Community Cloud
  $2.69 / $2.59 / $1.99 respectively.
- **Storage**: container disk $0.10/GB/mo; network volume $0.07/GB/mo under 1 TB, $0.05/GB/mo over
  1 TB; high-performance network volume $0.14/GB/mo. 2.75 TB on a standard network volume is about
  **$138/month**, i.e. ~$23 for five days.
- **Egress**: **not stated** on the pricing page. Secondary sources assert RunPod charges nothing
  for ingress or egress, but also warn that on Community Cloud "underlying network usage at some
  community hosts may apply"
  ([northflank.com/blog/runpod-gpu-pricing](https://northflank.com/blog/runpod-gpu-pricing),
  secondary). VERIFY before pushing 2.75 TB through a Community host.
- **Access**: containers, not bare VMs. You get SSH into a pod; you are root inside the container,
  but you do not own the host kernel or the NVMe layout.
- **Minimum commitment**: none; per-second-ish billing on pods.
- **Community Cloud caveat**: machines are third-party hosted. For a 5-day continuous run the
  host can disappear and the 80 GB/GPU claim varies by listing. This is the cheap-but-fragile tier.
- **Billing**: prepaid credits by card.

## 4. Recommendation

**Two best for "start within hours, one 8×H100 node, 5 days, $2,500–$3,500":**

1. **Lambda on-demand 8×H100 SXM — $31.92/hr, $3,830 for 120 h.** Slightly over the top of the
   band, and the best fit on every non-price axis: a single 8-GPU VM with **22 TiB of local SSD**
   (the only option where 2.75 TB of data plus 0.4 TB of checkpoints is a non-issue), ordinary SSH
   with sudo, no ingress/egress charge, no minimum term, card billing, no quota request. Buy 4
   GPUs' worth of run each and both trainings sit on one node. The risk is availability: check the
   console first, and if 8× is gone, 2× or 4× at $4.19/$4.09 is a worse deal per GPU but still
   launches. To land inside budget, run ~100 h instead of 120 h ($3,192) or accept $3,830.
2. **Nebius on-demand — $30.80/hr, $3,696 for 120 h.** Same order of price, free egress and free
   public IP, generous 16 vCPU / 200 GB RAM per GPU, self-serve card signup, managed Slurm free if
   you ever want it. The catch is that local NVMe is a separately-billed block volume at
   $0.065/GiB/mo (~$38 for 3.5 TB over five days, immaterial) and the per-node NVMe spec is not
   published, so confirm the disk attaches at NVMe speed before you start the encode.

**Cheapest that actually meets the need: RunPod Secure Cloud H100 SXM at $27.92/hr, $3,350 for
120 h.** Comfortably inside budget, self-serve, per-second billing, dedicated (not community)
hardware. Add ~$23 for a 2.75 TB network volume. Accept that you are in a container rather than on
a VM, and confirm the pod's local disk is NVMe and large enough before committing the encode to it.

If the budget is the binding constraint rather than the schedule, **SF Compute at ~$2.07/GPU-hr
(~$1,987 for five days)** is the real bargain and its price has been flat within 10 cents all
week — but its `sea-3-h100` SKU carries only **1.5 TiB of storage**, under the 2.75 TB total, so
you would have to stage the dataset in two passes or attach external storage. That is a real
engineering cost against a ~$1,400 saving.

**What to avoid, and why:**

- **Any spot or interruptible tier for a 5-day run.** Nebius preemptible ($2,064), CoreWeave spot
  ($2,365), Azure spot ($2,180) and Vast.ai interruptible (~$960) all look tempting. A preemption
  at hour 90 of a 120-hour training costs more in wall-clock and babysitting than the $1,500 saved,
  unless checkpoint-and-resume is already proven end-to-end. It is not worth it for a run that
  feeds an October paper deadline.
- **AWS p5, GCP A3 and Azure ND H100 v5 on-demand.** At $55–$98/hr per node they are 2–3× the
  budget ($6,605 / $10,619 / $11,798), and all three gate 8×H100 behind a quota increase that
  routinely takes days. Reference only.
- **CoreWeave.** The on-demand node at $49.24/hr is over budget, and signup appears to be
  sales-gated, which kills a same-day start.
- **Lambda 1-Click Clusters.** $6.16/GPU-hr with a two-week minimum — 54% more per GPU-hour than
  Lambda's own on-demand 8× node, for a commitment three times longer than the run.
- **Vast.ai and RunPod Community Cloud.** Cheapest posted numbers in the table, but third-party
  hosts, per-listing storage and bandwidth terms, and no guarantee the machine survives five
  continuous days. Community hosts may also meter the 2.75 TB download.
- **Hyperbolic.** No published price, and the rate it is reported at more than doubled inside ten
  weeks. You cannot plan a budget against it.
- **Prime Intellect and Voltage Park.** Both plausibly cheap (~$1,700–$1,900 for five days) and
  both self-serve, but neither publishes a price you can act on without logging in. Worth five
  minutes in each console before committing to Lambda — if Voltage Park quotes near $1.99/GPU-hr
  bare metal with InfiniBand and no minimum term, it beats everything on this list. Do not plan
  around it sight-unseen.

## 5. Addendum, 2026-09-22 — do we need a full node at all?

The 8-GPU node was a premise of the original brief, not a requirement derived from the workload.
Re-checked against the workload facts, **it is not justified.** Two GPUs clears the job, and the
binding constraint turns out to be local NVMe, not compute.

### 5.1 Memory: one 80 GB card already has 2x the headroom needed

Peak usage measured on an RTX A6000 48 GB was **26 GB** (SD 1.4 U-Net, 0.86 B, batch 32) and
**36 GB** (SD 3.5 Medium, 2.25 B, batch 32 with gradient checkpointing). Neither run needs model
parallelism. Consequences:

- An **H100 80 GB** holds the larger run with 44 GB spare, which is enough to **drop gradient
  checkpointing on the SD 3.5 run entirely**. That is a free speedup on top of the raw hardware
  gain; recomputation typically costs 25-35% of step time.
- An **H200 141 GB buys nothing here.** Same compute generation as H100, more HBM and bandwidth.
  The extra 61 GB is headroom neither run can use. Paying the H200 premium
  (RunPod $4.59 vs $3.49; Nebius $4.50 vs $3.85) is money spent on memory that sits idle.
- Both runs together peak at 62 GB, so they would even co-reside on one 80 GB card, but they would
  then contend for SMs and each would go slower than half speed. One run per card is the right
  split.

### 5.2 Lambda specifics — two traps worth knowing before spending credits

Read from the instances table at [lambda.ai/pricing](https://lambda.ai/pricing), 2026-09-22.

**Trap 1: Lambda does not sell H200 on-demand.** The page title says "rent NVIDIA H100, H200, and
B200", but the instances table offers B200 SXM6, H100 SXM, H100 PCIe, A100, **GH200**, A6000, A10,
Quadro RTX 6000 and V100. No H200. The only "H200" string on the page is inside "GH200". The
1-Click Cluster tiers are B200 and H100 only. So if the credits are on Lambda, H200 is off the
table regardless.

**Trap 2: local SSD scales with GPU count, and the 1-GPU box is too small.**

| Lambda config | $/GPU-hr | vCPU | RAM | Local SSD | Holds 2.75 TB data + 0.4 TB ckpt? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1x H100 PCIe | 3.29 | 26 | 225 GiB | **1 TiB** | no |
| 1x H100 SXM | 4.29 | 26 | 225 GiB | **2.75 TiB** (= 3.02 TB) | **no — 3.15 TB needed** |
| 2x H100 SXM | 4.19 | 52 | 450 GiB | **5.5 TiB** | yes |
| 4x H100 SXM | 4.09 | 104 | 900 GiB | 11 TiB | yes |
| 8x H100 SXM | 3.99 | 208 | 1800 GiB | 22 TiB | yes |
| 1x GH200 96 GB | **2.29** | 64 | 432 GiB | **4 TiB** | yes |

The 1x H100 SXM instance misses by about 130 GB. **2x H100 SXM is the smallest x86 Lambda instance
that holds the dataset and the checkpoints**, which is a storage reason to take two GPUs, not a
compute one. A persistent Lambda filesystem could get round it, but the filesystem rate is
unverified (see section 2) and it is network storage, not the local NVMe the brief requires.

**The GH200 is the interesting row.** $2.29/GPU-hr, 96 GB VRAM, **4 TiB SSD**, 64 vCPU, 432 GiB
RAM. That is H100-class compute with more VRAM and more local disk than the 1x H100 SXM, at **53%
of the price**. Two separate 1x GH200 instances give each training run its own machine, its own
4 TiB disk and zero contention, for $4.58/hr combined.
The catch is **arm64**: Grace is a Neoverse CPU, so every wheel in the environment has to be
aarch64. Lambda publishes a guide for exactly this stack ([docs.lambda.ai](https://docs.lambda.ai/),
"Running Hugging Face Transformers and Diffusers on an NVIDIA GH200 instance"), which says it works
but is not the zero-friction path. Budget half a day for environment setup, or skip it if the
schedule is tight.

### 5.3 Cost of the small configurations, 120 hours

| Config | $/hr | 5 days | Local NVMe | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lambda, two 1x GH200 instances | 4.58 | **$550** | 4 TiB each | arm64 env work |
| RunPod Secure, 2x H100 SXM | 6.98 | **$838** | pod disk + network volume (~$23) | containers |
| Nebius, 2x H100 | 7.70 | **$924** | block volume ~$38 | free egress |
| Lambda, 2x H100 SXM (one instance) | 8.38 | **$1,006** | 5.5 TiB included | x86, simplest |
| Nebius, 2x H200 | 9.00 | $1,080 | | H200 premium wasted |
| RunPod Secure, 2x H200 | 9.18 | $1,102 | | H200 premium wasted |
| Lambda 4x H100 SXM | 16.36 | $1,963 | 11 TiB | |
| Lambda 8x H100 SXM | 31.92 | $3,830 | 22 TiB | the original plan |

**Dropping from 8 GPUs to 2 on Lambda saves $2,825 of the five-day cost**, or $3,280 if the GH200
route works. If the Lambda credits are the Research Grant tier (up to $5,000, see section 2), a
2x H100 SXM run at $1,006 fits inside them with room for a second attempt; the 8-GPU node at
$3,830 would consume almost the whole grant in one go.

### 5.4 Throughput: what 2 GPUs actually delivers

**Estimates, not measurements.** H100 SXM is roughly 2.5-3.5x an A6000 on bf16 diffusion training
in practice. Paper ratios are higher (about 6.4x dense BF16 tensor throughput, about 4.4x memory
bandwidth), and neither is realised in full. The SD 3.5 run gains a further 1.3-1.4x from dropping
gradient checkpointing on an 80 GB card.

| Run | A6000 measured | H100 estimate | Updates/day, 1 GPU |
|---|---|---|---|
| SD 1.4 U-Net, 0.86 B | 1.0 upd/s | 2.5-3.5 upd/s | 216k-302k |
| SD 3.5 Medium, 2.25 B | 0.51 upd/s | 1.7-2.4 upd/s (no ckpt) | 147k-207k |

Over about 4.5 days of training on one GPU each, that is roughly **1.0-1.4 M updates for SD 1.4**
and **0.66-0.93 M for SD 3.5**. Unless the run plan calls for more updates than that, compute is
not the constraint and the extra six GPUs would idle.

**The project's own rule applies here** (CLAUDE.md, "Fill the card"): run a short throughput sweep
before committing to a multi-hour run, and pick the largest micro-batch that fits while keeping the
global batch fixed. The sweep replaces every estimate in this table within about 20 minutes of GPU
time.

### 5.5 The one thing that could still justify more GPUs

**The VAE encode.** It is the only part of the workload that scales close to linearly with GPU
count: 10 M frames at 320x256 through two autoencoders (SD 1.x and SD 3.5), embarrassingly parallel
across episodes, and it must finish before training starts because training consumes the latents.
Training does not scale the same way, because the global batch is fixed.

I do not have a measured encode rate and will not invent one; the `encode-profiler` agent in this
session is producing exactly that number. The decision rule is simple:

- If the encode is **about 40 GPU-hours or less**, two GPUs absorb it in under a day and the 2-GPU
  plan holds.
- If it is **150 GPU-hours or more**, it would eat most of a 5-day window on two GPUs, and the
  right move is a short burst on a bigger box for the encode only, then two GPUs for training, not
  five days of eight idle-ish cards.

Splitting the job that way is cheap on any per-hour provider with no minimum term, which all four
of the recommended options are.

### 5.6 Revised recommendation

1. **Lambda 2x H100 SXM, $8.38/hr, about $1,006 for five days.** If the credits are on Lambda, this
   is the answer. One instance, 5.5 TiB local SSD (the smallest Lambda box that holds the data),
   80 GB per card so SD 3.5 runs without gradient checkpointing, x86 so the existing environment
   works unchanged, no ingress or egress charge, no minimum term. One training run per card.
2. **RunPod Secure 2x H100 SXM, about $838.** Cheapest x86 route if the credits turn out not to
   apply. Costs a network volume for the dataset and puts you in a container rather than a VM.
3. **Lambda, two 1x GH200 instances, about $550.** Best price per unit of work on this list, 96 GB
   and 4 TiB each, but arm64. Take it only if there is slack in the schedule for environment setup.

Do **not** pay for H200 here, and do not book the 8-GPU node unless the encode profile comes back
heavy. Check the encode number first; it is the only input that can overturn this.

**Still unverified:** whether Lambda Research Grant credits carry instance-type or expiry
restrictions. Lambda's public grant description says credits are usable on on-demand instances and
1-Click Clusters across B200, H100, A100 "and more", but I found no primary terms page stating
expiry or eligible SKUs. Check the credit balance page in the console before planning around it.

large-disk SXM listing on the board.

### 6.4 A correction to my own section 5 advice

Running two separate 1-GPU instances means **each instance needs its own copy of the dataset**.
Both training runs consume the same latents, so that is two downloads and, on any provider that
meters storage, two storage bills. This does not change the Lambda 2x H100 SXM recommendation
(one instance, one shared 5.5 TiB disk), but it weakens the "two separate 1x GH200 instances"
option I floated in section 5.6: still $550 of GPU time, but two full dataset pulls and the arm64
work on both machines. A single 2-GPU instance is the cleaner shape wherever it is available.

### 6.5 Verdict on Vast.ai for this job

**Look, but do not book.** The reasoning:

- The saving over RunPod is about $120 on a five-day run. **If the Lambda credits exist, the saving
  over Lambda is negative**, because credits are already paid for.
- Five listings platform-wide meet the disk requirement, so availability is a coin flip on the day,
  with no second choice.
- An unverified host carrying a 120-hour continuous training run is the specific risk the project
  can least afford in the run-up to an October workshop deadline. Vast's own documentation splits
  hosts into verification stages precisely because they are not equivalent, and third-party
  reviews put the effective premium on unverified hosts at 20-40% once restart overhead is counted
  ([lyceum.technology](https://lyceum.technology/magazine/vast-ai-reliability-depends-on-the-host-renting/),
  secondary).

**Where Vast.ai genuinely is the right tool: the VAE encode burst.** If the profiler comes back
heavy (section 5.5, 150+ GPU-hours), the encode is short, restartable and embarrassingly parallel
across episodes, the data does not need to stay resident for days, and an interrupted shard costs
only a re-run. Renting eight cheap Vast GPUs for a few hours to clear the encode, then doing five
days of training on two reliable cards, is a better shape than either extreme. That is the one
scenario where I would spend time in the Vast console.

### 6.6 Name check

There are two different companies here. **Vast.ai** is the GPU marketplace analysed above.
**VAST Data** is an enterprise storage company and is not a GPU rental provider at all. If the
material under discussion is from VAST Data, none of this section applies to it and it is not an
option for renting H100s.

## 7. Addendum, 2026-09-22 — where "5 days" came from, and what the run actually needs

Like the 8-GPU node, the five-day window was a premise of the brief, not a derived number. The
repo settles it. All quotes below are from `RESEARCH_CONTEXT.md` in this worktree.

### 7.1 Five days is a calendar, not a compute estimate

The freeze is the CoRL PhysWM workshop target of **Sep 30**. Renting Sep 22-27 leaves three days
for evaluation and writing. That is where five days comes from. It is the space available, not the
work required.

The compute requirement is a different number, and the repo states it:

> "at batch 32 one pass is about **315k steps**, which the U-Net reaches in about **3.6 days** and
> SD 3.5 does not before the freeze; more data cannot help below one pass"

3.6 days for 315k steps implies **1.0 updates/s**, which matches the SD 1.4 A6000 figure in the
brief exactly. The same entry records SD 3.5 at **0.515 updates/s** on the current hardware, so one
pass for that row is **7.2 days on an A6000** — which is precisely why the repo says it "does not
[finish] before the freeze", and why the entry lists "compute plan for a 300k-step-class dense run
(one A6000: PixArt about 11 days at batch 128; **H100 rental tabled**)" as an open decision for
Rohan. **Making one pass reachable is the entire purpose of the rental.**

### 7.2 On H100s, 48 hours is the right number for training

Applying the section 5.4 speedup range (2.5-3.5x, plus 1.3-1.4x for SD 3.5 from dropping gradient
checkpointing on an 80 GB card) to the repo's 315k-step target:

| Run | A6000 rate | One pass on A6000 | H100 rate (est.) | **One pass on H100** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SD 1.4 U-Net | 1.0 upd/s | 3.6 days | 2.5-3.5 upd/s | **25-35 h** |
| SD 3.5 Medium | 0.515 upd/s | 7.2 days | 1.7-2.4 upd/s | **36-51 h** |

Running one row per card concurrently, **SD 3.5 binds at 36-51 hours.** Rohan's 48-hour instinct
sits squarely inside that band and is better calibrated than the brief's five days. For training
alone, 48 hours is right.

There is also repo evidence that the back half of a run buys little. Comparing a 30k-update grid
cell against the full 90k row: "one third of the budget already matches it on both unseen corpora
and beats it on rollout PSNR and FVD; the extra 60k updates buy only seen-map perceptual quality".
Against that, GameNGen's curves "separate only past 100k steps". So the target is one pass, and
overshooting it is not where the value is.

### 7.3 The catch: the encode does not fit in the same 48 hours

Section 5.5 flagged the VAE encode as the one thing that could overturn the plan, and set a
threshold of "about 40 GPU-hours or less" for the 2-GPU plan to hold. **The repo already contains a
measured rate, and it is above that threshold.**

> "latent encoding (about **4.6 A6000-hours per 1M decision frames** in the 4-channel space) is the
> real cost."

Cross-checked against the other entry — "dense arenas every tic is 467 GiB / **169 card-hours**"
for 40.3M tics, i.e. 4.19 card-hours per 1M — the rate is consistent.

The runs train on ids 0:2000, **10.07M tics**. So:

| Encode | Rate | A6000-hours | H100-hours (est. 2-2.5x) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4-channel (SD 1.x) | 4.6 A6000-h/1M, **measured** | **46** | 18-23 |
| 16-channel (SD 3.5) | **not measured** | ~70-90 est. | 28-45 est. |
| Total | | ~116-136 | **~46-68** |

On two GPUs that is **23-34 hours of wall clock before training starts**. Added to a 36-51 hour
training run, it does not fit in 48 hours, and only barely fits in five days.

The 16-channel rate is the one number still missing. It is a larger autoencoder, so I scaled the
measured 4-channel rate by 1.5-2x; that factor is an assumption, not a measurement, and it is
exactly what `encode-profiler` is producing.

### 7.4 The shape this implies: wide and short, then narrow and long

Because the encode is embarrassingly parallel and the training is not, and because **Lambda bills
in one-minute increments with no minimum period** ("ODC prices instances by hourly usage and bills
in one-minute increments", charges end "the moment you terminate the instance",
[docs.lambda.ai/public-cloud/billing](https://docs.lambda.ai/public-cloud/billing/)), the two
phases should be rented at different widths:

| Phase | Shape | Wall clock | Lambda cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Encode, both latent spaces | 8x H100 | 6-9 h | ~$255 (8 h @ $31.92) |
| Train both rows, one per card | 2x H100 | 48 h | $402 (@ $8.38) |
| **Total** | | **~56 h** | **~$657** |

Against the alternatives: five days on 2 GPUs is **$1,006** and would still be tight once the
encode is counted; five days on the 8-GPU node is **$3,830** with six cards idle for most of it.
The split plan is both the cheapest and the fastest.

**One wrinkle.** Terminating the 8-GPU instance destroys its local NVMe, so the latents (roughly
117 GiB at 4 channels plus about 470 GiB at 16 channels, extrapolating the repo's 467 GiB / 40.3M
tics figure) must be written to a Lambda persistent filesystem before teardown and copied back to
local NVMe on the 2-GPU box. That is about 590 GiB moving twice inside the same datacenter. The
filesystem rate is still unverified (section 2), but at the $0.20/GiB/month figure Lambda's docs
show as an example, holding 590 GiB for two days is about **$8** — immaterial either way.

### 7.5 Revised answer on duration

**Rohan is right that 48 hours covers the training, and the brief's five days was calendar space
rather than a compute requirement.** Three practical consequences:

1. **Nothing is being committed to.** On Lambda, RunPod, Nebius and Voltage Park the rental is
   hourly with no minimum, so the duration only sets the budget line, not a booking. Rent, run to
   one pass or to the freeze, and terminate. The providers where duration *is* a commitment are the
   ones already ruled out: Lambda 1-Click Clusters at two weeks, and SF Compute where you buy a
   block.
2. **Budget the encode separately from the training.** They have different shapes and want
   different GPU counts. Quoting one blended five-day number hides the fact that the encode is the
   part that wants eight cards and the training is the part that wants two.
3. **The number still to confirm is the 16-channel encode rate.** If it comes in near the
   4-channel rate, the whole job compresses toward 48 hours on 2 GPUs and the burst is unnecessary.
   If it is 2x or worse, the burst saves a day of wall clock for about $255.

At 48 hours on two cards the cost is **$402 on Lambda, $335 on RunPod Secure** — small enough that
a Research Grant credit balance, if it exists, covers the run several times over.
