## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

The benchmark is a real contribution and the persistence finding is a real, if small, contribution about evaluation. The headline, as titled ("Pretraining Exposure, Not Architecture, Predicts"), is not supported by the paper's own table. Three rows are finished (one of them run twice), the abstract promises five, and the only finished-row ordering the draft can point to is a held-out v-loss ordering, PixArt 0.2030 < U-Net 0.2043 < DiT 0.2139, which is **not monotone in exposure**: the 25M-image start beats the roughly 2B-image start. The draft's abstract says the ImageNet DiT is "the weakest start on every metric", and the draft's own Table 1 shows it winning rollout PSNR@64 (18.30 vs 16.03), rollout LPIPS@64 (0.553 vs 0.566), unseen-map PSNR (19.52 vs 19.14, intervals disjoint) and seen-to-unseen degradation (1.54 vs 2.23 dB). A reviewer finds that contradiction in the first minute and reads the rest of the paper looking for more. I would score this a weak reject as drafted and a clear accept if the claim is rewritten to what the data show, the UniDiffuser row lands, and one per-row learning-rate robustness check exists.

## 1. Claims as the reviewer reads them, the attack, and what defuses it

### 1.1 Headline: "pretraining exposure in the target latent space, not architecture, predicts small-budget Doom world-model quality"

**As the reviewer reads it.** "Across five warm starts spanning U-Nets and transformers, quality after a fixed 90k-update adaptation is ordered by how much pretraining the checkpoint had in the SD KL-f8 latent space, and the U-Net/transformer distinction does not change that ordering."

**Attack 1: the ordering the paper reports is not the ordering the claim needs.** Exposure by the draft's own Table 2: DiT-XL/2 1.3M images, PixArt-alpha 25M, SD 1.4 "about 2B". Held-out loss: PixArt 0.2030 < U-Net 0.2043 < DiT 0.2139. The start with 80 times PixArt's exposure loses to PixArt. The draft then quietly changes the axis from a count to a binary ("broad data in the target latent space" versus ImageNet), at which point the claim is one-versus-two and "predicts" means "the one class-conditional ImageNet checkpoint was worst". *Defuse:* state the finding ordinally and specifically: "the class-conditional ImageNet DiT is the weakest of three starts on validation loss and seen-map LPIPS; the two starts with web-scale text-conditioned pretraining in the same latent space are within 0.0013 of each other on loss despite an 80x gap in exposure." That is true, interesting, and survives. What PixArt's landed evaluation does to this: if PixArt's seen LPIPS is at or below the U-Net's 0.270, the binary version holds on two metrics; if PixArt's seen LPIPS lands nearer the DiT's 0.311 while its loss is lowest, the loss ordering is shown to be uninformative and the headline has no metric left.

**Attack 2: "exposure" is undefined, and under the other natural definition the DiT was not under-exposed.** The DiT card (`backbones-and-warm-starts.md`) quotes DiT-XL/2-256 as trained for "7M" steps at batch 256, so about 1.8B target presentations in exactly this latent space. SD 1.4's lineage is "about 1.17M steps at batch 2048", about 2.4B presentations. Measured in presentations the DiT and the U-Net had comparable exposure; measured in unique images the DiT had 1,500 times less. The draft uses unique images without saying why. If the useful quantity is diversity or the conditioning signal (class label versus text) rather than "exposure", the title's noun is wrong. *Defuse:* give both counts in Table 2, name the axis "pretraining data diversity in the target latent space", and say that presentations do not separate the rows.

**Attack 3: "not architecture" is confounded with the action-injection path, and Nano World Models already showed that path matters.** DiT: a fresh 29-way table summed into adaLN-Zero. PixArt: two learned 4096-wide tokens through the pretrained caption projection plus adaLN-single for the timestep. U-Net: one cross-attention token plus a class embedding for the bucket. Nano World Models (`dit-world-models-b.md`) compares additive, adaLN, adaLN-fuse, FiLM and cross-attention and finds "Action injection is task-dependent" (FiLM best on RT-1, additive best on PushT) with parameter deltas up to 42.5M. So a reviewer can attribute the whole DiT-versus-PixArt gap to "fresh adaLN table versus pretrained cross-attention projection" and the paper has no control against that. *Defuse:* cite Nano WM, state the confound in the results paragraph (the draft's limitations do), and drop "not architecture" from the title; "architecture is second-order" belongs in the discussion as a reading, not in the title as a result.

**Attack 4: three points on the axis, and two of them are the same skeleton.** The draft counts five rows; on Sep 16 the finished rows are DiT (two seeds), U-Net and PixArt, with PixArt's pixel and rollout metrics landed but not yet in the log. UniDiffuser's build "started Sep 16, train Sep 17"; SkyReels is 10k updates of batch 8 (80k presentations, "36x fewer") in a different VAE and is already declared not "a sixth point on the exposure axis". A study titled around a predictor with three finished points is a preliminary study. *Defuse:* finish UniDiffuser (section 4); until then the abstract must say three rows plus one exploratory.

**Attack 5: the loss gap that carries the ordering is 0.0013 and its uncertainty is not stated.** PixArt 0.2030 vs U-Net 0.2043 on 1,024 validation windows. The DiT seeds ended at 0.213941 and 0.213875 (delta 0.00007), so seed noise on the loss is small for that row, but the two rows being ordered have one seed each and the loss is a uniform-t v-MSE that the paper itself shows does not track pixel metrics (the U-Net leads the DiT by 0.0096 on loss and by 0.041 LPIPS, then loses by 2.28 dB at horizon 64). *Defuse:* never order rows by loss alone; use PixArt's pixel and rollout metrics; report a window-bootstrap interval on the validation loss.

**Attack 6: the recipe was chosen after watching one backbone fail.** Section 7, Sep 10: the DiT at lr 1e-4 had a "45k to 60k excursion" (0.2321 to 0.3047, 20k steps to recover) while "the U-Net at lr 5e-5 was monotone"; the shared 5e-5 / warmup 2000 was then adopted for both. 5e-5 is the SD fine-tuning neighbourhood (GameNGen 2e-5, the arnaudstiegler repro 5e-5); DiT's own recipe is 1e-4, batch 256, no warmup. Astra's round-4 item 5 proposed "controlled forks from one recovery checkpoint at 2.5e-5, 5e-5, 1e-4 ... roughly 500 updates each" and it was not run. A reviewer will say the ordering is a learning-rate artefact until one such fork exists. *Defuse:* section 4, experiment 1b.

**Attack 7: equal updates are not equal compute, and the heavier model won.** Measured throughput on one A6000 at batch 32: DiT 1.26 updates/s, U-Net 0.85, PixArt 0.68 (shared card). The U-Net therefore consumed about 1.5x the DiT's card-hours for the same 90k updates. Astra's round-4 arithmetic: "1.6/1.4 updates/s gives 102,857 DiT updates versus 90k U-Net". The compute-matched runs meant to answer this (`scripts/spiderman/compute_matched.sh`) are 2,500 updates with `--warmup 2000`, so 80% of each run is warmup and the comparison is of models at a fifth of peak learning rate; they will not answer the question. *Defuse:* either fix the short runs (warmup 200, or fork from a mid checkpoint) or report card-hours per row honestly and call the study equal-update rather than equal-compute.

### 1.2 Map transfer: "the transformer gives back less on unseen maps"

**As the reviewer reads it.** "Transformer warm starts generalize to unseen layouts better than the U-Net start."

**Attack 1: n = 2 maps, 20 episodes, and the effect is 0.4 dB.** Unseen PSNR: DiT s0 19.52 [19.32, 19.73], s1 19.59 [19.40, 19.80], U-Net 19.14 [18.90, 19.39]. Disjoint, yes, but the bootstrap resamples episodes within two maps, so it is an interval on "these two maps", not on "unseen maps". Unseen LPIPS is "mixed" (0.450 / 0.442 vs 0.446). The draft's "degrade less (1.54 versus 2.23 dB)" is a difference of differences with no interval anywhere. *Defuse:* the 15-map corpus, with per-map values and a paired episode bootstrap on the seen-minus-unseen difference; until then the word "transfer" should not appear in the abstract.

**Attack 2: the all-live policy removes the U-Net's better weights from exactly the comparison the U-Net loses.** U-Net EMA gains 0.31 dB and 0.020 LPIPS on seen maps; "DiT EMA barely moves". Unseen EMA numbers are not in the log. If the U-Net's EMA gains a similar 0.3 dB on unseen maps, the 0.38 to 0.45 dB transfer gap shrinks to within the DiT's own seed spread. Astra's round-6 rule (all-live or all-EMA, never mixed) is right, but the paper must show the unseen and rollout EMA columns in the appendix so a reviewer can check that the transfer ordering holds under both. *Defuse:* the EMA unseen and rollout columns (already a `\todo` in the draft).

**Attack 3: part of the unseen gap is the decoder, not the world model.** `finetune_decoder.py` fits the decoder on `split["train"]` frames (50k) and validates on `split["val"]`, both from maps 1 to 15. The VAE ceiling is 28.61 / 0.051 on seen maps and 26.36 / 0.070 on unseen maps: the decoder alone loses 2.25 dB on unseen maps. Every reported unseen number passes through that decoder. So "seen-to-unseen degradation" of 1.54 or 2.23 dB is measured against a floor that itself moved by 2.25 dB, and a model that predicts latents equally well on both map sets would still show a degradation. *Defuse:* report ceiling-relative numbers (model minus ceiling) per corpus, or score unseen maps with the frozen decoder as well; state in the benchmark section that the decoder was fit on seen-map frames.

**Attack 4: the 15 further maps are a different level-design family, and the agent is off-distribution on them too.** The corpus in flight is "Freedoom maps 18 to 32" (section 0). The Arnold WAD's 17 maps are deathmatch arenas; Freedoom Phase 2 MAP18 to MAP32 are single-player levels. Arnold was trained on maps 2 to 5 of the deathmatch WAD (survey card). Transfer to maps 16 and 17 is "new arena, same family"; transfer to MAP18 to 32 is "new family, agent behaving differently" (larger layouts, keyed doors, different spawn logic, possibly monsters). The two must be reported separately, and the agent's per-map statistics on the new maps (kills, deaths, cell coverage, action histogram) must be shown to be comparable to the training set's 38% to 89% coverage, or the "transfer" is partly a behaviour shift. *Defuse:* two unseen rows (within-family, cross-family) and a per-map agent-statistics table in the appendix. The curation of the PWAD (which of MAP18 to 32 were kept and why) must be written down before any row is scored, otherwise it is selection on the outcome.

### 1.3 Persistence at long horizons: "every model falls to or below the frozen-seed reference perceptually by horizon 64"

**As the reviewer reads it.** "Long-horizon pixel metrics on Doom rollouts are dominated by persistence; a PSNR lead is not evidence of dynamics."

**Attack 1: this is a known property and prior work already built its evaluation around it.** PlayGen reports every metric by rollout length (1 / 16 / 32 / 64 / 128 / 1024; Doom PSNR 23.81 down to 17.25, LPIPS 0.165 up to 0.472) and introduces ActAcc/ProbDiff precisely "because pixel metrics decay under divergence". MultiGen reports LPIPS by step range (1 to 128 vs 128 to 196). GameNGen's own data show copy-last at 26 dB per tic, which is why this project moved to per-decision frames. Nano World Models writes that long-horizon autoregression "inevitably accumulates perceptual errors". The reviewer's sentence: "known; the authors rediscovered it with weak models". *Defuse:* frame as a quantification with a persistence reference, cite all four, and make the new part explicit: the paired blur control (blur never raises PSNR: U-Net 16.02 / 16.04 / 15.78 at sigma 0 / 0.5 / 4; DiT s0 18.30 to 17.78) and the motion audit, which none of the cited papers ran.

**Attack 2: "every model" means "every model at 2.88M presentations".** The strongest published Doom models do not report copy-seed, so the claim that persistence beats models at horizon 64 could be a statement about this budget. GameNGen reports 19.02 dB after 3 s of autoregression on agent data; PlayGen 20.03 dB at 64 frames. Our DiT is at 18.30 and the U-Net at 16.03. The reviewer will ask whether a model that clears 20 dB at horizon 64 also clears copy-seed on LPIPS. *Defuse:* say "at this budget" in the sentence, and show the copy-seed line at every horizon (8, 16, 32, 64), not only at 64, so the reader sees where each model crosses it.

**Attack 3: inference-time context noise was never tuned, and it is the known lever for exactly this failure.** Training corrupts the context with noise up to 0.7 in 10 buckets; inference feeds a clean context (bucket 0). GameNGen's stated reason for the augmentation is that "without noise augmentation, quality degrades quickly after 10-20 frames". SkyReels' `addnoise_condition` and this repo's own `--ctx-stabilize` in `eval_video.py` implement the inference-side version, and the log records "ctx-stabilize 0 vs the trained 20 for reporting" as an open choice for the video row only. If a non-zero inference bucket lifts every model above copy-seed at horizon 64, the persistence finding becomes "we used the wrong inference setting". *Defuse:* section 4, experiment 2b: sweep the inference bucket on the existing 256 rollouts for all finished rows.

**Attack 4: the late-drop tails are unexplained.** Median horizon-32-to-64 drops are -0.03 / -0.54 / -1.20 dB (DiT s0 / s1 / U-Net) with 25 / 36 / 43% of rollouts losing more than 2 dB and "heavy tails for all". Two DiT seeds differ by 0.62 dB at horizon 64 and 74 FVD32. The paper cannot tell the reader whether the DiT's lead is a mode (it freezes) or a distribution (it drifts less); the motion audit is the only thing that will. *Defuse:* the motion audit (`motion_audit.py`, in flight), with the four measures it already defines (frame-difference energy, PSNR to seed, Farneback flow, fraction of rollouts closer to seed than reality), reported as prediction/real ratios with the cluster-bootstrap CI.

**Attack 5: rollouts exist only on seen maps.** 256 x 64 on the seen corpus; no rollouts on unseen maps. The transfer claim is teacher-forced only; the persistence claim is seen-map only. *Defuse:* one unseen-map rollout set (section 4, experiment 2c).

### 1.4 The open benchmark: "the first open, reproducible Doom world-model benchmark"

**As the reviewer reads it.** "No open Doom world-model data or protocol existed before this."

**Attack 1: "first open" is false as written.** The arnaudstiegler chain is fully open (code, two checkpoints, a fine-tuned VAE, 2,470,229 frames on the Hub, a PPO agent), and the card calls it "the only fully open artifact chain of the five". p-doom/doom-dataset is 10M frames at 60x80. PlayGen released code and a demo. DIAMOND releases playable models and uses a public CS:GO dataset. What is new is the combination: lossless per-tic frames at 320x240, 17 maps, a seeded evaluation corpus with held-out maps, and the references (copy-last, copy-seed, VAE ceiling, blur control, episode bootstrap). *Defuse:* "the first lossless multi-map Doom corpus with a seeded held-out-map evaluation protocol", and cite the three open predecessors in the same sentence.

**Attack 2: nothing is released.** Section 0: "Hugging Face account for the release" is still open for Rohan; "Release: dataset shards and weights under Rohan's Hugging Face account before the final". A benchmark paper without a link at submission is a promise. *Defuse:* upload before the draft is frozen on Sep 26, and put the URL in the abstract.

**Attack 3: one scripted policy, one game mode, Freedoom assets.** All data are the 2017 Arnold checkpoint in deathmatch against 8 bots for 150 game-seconds, 29 actions with a 23.2% majority class and 54.8% IDM macro recall (class imbalance), 62% of decisions off the tic grid because of deaths. GameNGen's data are PPO play on id's levels; MultiGen's are Arnold on 100 generated maps; PlayGen's are a random-plus-agent hybrid with balanced sampling that improved Doom metrics "1.9% to 57.7%". The reviewer: "your benchmark measures how well a model imitates Arnold in deathmatch; generalization to human play or other modes is untested". *Defuse:* say exactly that in the benchmark section; report the action histogram; do not use the words "Doom world model" without "Arnold deathmatch".

**Attack 4: the judge does not discriminate.** Rollout IDM top-1: 0.492 / 0.476 / 0.509 with "CIs all overlap", against a real reference of 0.847 and majority 0.232. Every row lands at the same place. MineWorld validated its IDM metric against humans (Pearson r = 0.56, p = 0.01, 5 players x 20 clips); this judge has no human validation and no per-action-group breakdown in the draft (a `\todo`). The video row's rollouts round-trip through the SD encoder to reach the judge, so its number is on a different footing again. *Defuse:* per-action-group precision/recall as MineWorld and Matrix-Game report it; call the judge "a sanity floor" not a metric until it separates rows.

**Attack 5: reproducibility of the recorder is asserted for one 60 s episode.** "one 60 s episode recorded twice ... gave identical tics, actions, buttons, game variables, and frame hashes (1,980 tics)". That is one episode on one machine with the same ViZDoom build (1.2.4) and torch-patched Arnold weights. *Defuse:* re-record one evaluation episode on the other server and hash it; state the ViZDoom, Arnold-patch and library versions.

## 4. The three experiments that most raise acceptance probability (about 6 A6000-days before Sep 26)

Ranked by how much of the paper's headline they can save or sink. Costs use the measured rates in the log (DiT 1.26 updates/s on a free A6000 at batch 32; U-Net 0.85; sampling about 2 frames/s at 50 DDIM steps on one A6000; teacher-forced evaluation of 2,048 windows in under an hour per corpus per row) and are stated as card-hours; they are estimates, not measurements.

**Experiment 1 (about 2.5 card-days): finish UniDiffuser and run the per-row LR forks.**
- 1a. UniDiffuser U-ViT (952M, LAION-2B, same VAE) to 90k under the shared recipe, then the full evaluation suite. It is the only planned row that separates "transformer" from "ImageNet-only exposure" with a second point. If it lands with the U-Net on loss and seen LPIPS the headline gains its second transformer point; if it lands with the ImageNet DiT the headline is dead and the paper becomes "warm starts are not interchangeable; here is the benchmark", which is still publishable. Cost: throughput is unknown; at 0.7 updates/s, 90k updates is 36 h, plus about 6 h of evaluation: about 1.75 card-days. This is already scheduled for GPU 2 on Sep 17; it is listed here because it is the experiment the headline depends on, not because it is new.
- 1b. LR robustness forks: from each finished row's 45k recovery checkpoint, continue 1,000 updates at 2.5e-5, 5e-5 and 1e-4 with identical batches and corruption seeds (Astra's round-4 item 5, adapted), validate at 500 and 1,000. Three rows x three LRs x 1,000 updates at about 1 update/s is about 2.5 card-hours plus validation; call it 0.3 card-days. Report the paired held-out-loss delta per LR. This is the cheapest thing in the paper that turns "one recipe" into "the ordering does not flip within a 4x LR band", which is what "predicts" needs.

**Experiment 2 (about 1.5 card-days): make the persistence finding a mechanism rather than an observation.**
- 2a. The motion audit on the existing 256 x 64 rollouts of every finished row plus copy-seed (in flight, `motion_audit.py`): decode cost only, under 2 card-hours for four rows. Reports the prediction/real ratio of frame-difference energy and Farneback flow at horizons 8 / 16 / 32 / 64 with an episode cluster bootstrap. This is the experiment Astra ranked first and it decides whether the DiT's 2.28 dB lead at horizon 64 is "moves less" or "drifts less". Keep exactly one of the draft's Version A / Version B sentences.
- 2b. Inference-time context-noise sweep: re-run the same 256 x 64 rollouts with the context fed at buckets 0, 2 and 5 (of 10) for DiT s0, U-Net and PixArt. Cost: 256 rollouts x 64 frames at about 2 frames/s is about 2.3 h per row per setting; two new settings x three rows is about 14 card-hours, 0.6 card-days. If a non-zero bucket lifts any row above copy-seed on LPIPS at horizon 64, the persistence paragraph changes from "every model falls below persistence" to "with the published inference setting every model falls below persistence; the known augmentation lever recovers X". Either outcome is a result; not running it leaves the obvious reviewer question open.
- 2c. Unseen-map rollouts, one setting, 128 x 64 for the three finished rows plus copy-seed: about 1.2 h per row, 0.15 card-days. Puts the transfer claim on the same footing as the persistence claim.

**Experiment 3 (about 1.5 card-days): a second U-Net seed.**
- 90k updates at 0.85 updates/s is about 29 h plus 6 h of evaluation. The U-Net owns every bolded cell in Table 1 (seen PSNR, seen LPIPS, FVD16/32, IDM) on one seed, and the DiT's two seeds show that FVD16 moves by 34 and horizon-64 PSNR by 0.62 dB between seeds. Without a second U-Net seed the bolding must go and the sentence "the U-Net wins perceptual and distributional quality" has to carry "one seed" every time it appears. If the deadline forces a choice between this and 1b, run 1b: it is ten times cheaper and defends the headline; the seed defends the supporting result.

**What to stop:** the compute-matched 2,500-update runs as scripted (warmup 2,000 of 2,500 steps; they will measure warmup); any further SkyReels tuning beyond the one pilot; the human study (no time, and it does not resolve persistence versus motion). The 15-map teacher-forced scoring is cheap (under an hour per row per corpus) and already queued; it is not counted above because it costs nothing that competes with the three.

Total: about 5.5 card-days of the 6 available, in the order 1b, 2a, 2b, 1a (already scheduled), 2c, 3.

## 5. A 150-word abstract that would survive this review

> Small labs building a world model of one environment start from a pretrained diffusion checkpoint, and the published evidence for which one does not transfer across papers. We release a lossless, redistributable Doom benchmark: 850 Arnold-agent deathmatch episodes on 17 Freedoom maps (1.05M verified decision frames), a seeded evaluation corpus with held-out maps, and a protocol with persistence and autoencoder references, a blur control, an inverse-dynamics judge, and episode-level intervals.
