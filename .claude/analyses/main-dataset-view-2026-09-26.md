# Main session's view: the adaptation dataset (2026-09-26, 19:05 EDT)

Written before reading the Astra and Opus views, against `dataset-brief-adaptation-2026-09-26.md`.

**1. Targets: the 13 unseen arenas (1, 6 to 17). Campaign maps stay in the zero-shot table only.** The reason is coverage, not size. Adaptation learns the footage distribution of a map, and on an arena six episodes cover the layout many times over, so adapt and held-out episodes are draws from one distribution; the disjoint-subset check (ii) passes on every arena. On the campaign levels the same check fails on 8 of 13 maps with subsets disagreeing by up to 29 percent, so a campaign curve would measure which rooms the adapt footage covered. The paper keeps the 30-map zero-shot result, presents the arenas as the spine, and uses the campaign rows as the far-shift remark and an appendix table. It says in one sentence why adaptation was not run there.

**2. Episodes: 20 per target arena, same recorder, same conditions.** Arenas 6, 7, 8 already have 20; 16 and 17 have 10; the eight others have 4. Record 16 more on each of the eight and 10 more on 16 and 17: 148 episodes, about 20 minutes of CPU on Spiderman at 32 workers (or 50 minutes at 16 workers at low priority beside the trainers), 37 GB raw, about 2.5 hours of SD 1 encoding on an A6000 (SD 3.5 encoding only if that row joins the study). "Same data quality" means: the same seeded Arnold recorder and corpus id family, 150 game-seconds per episode, 8 bots, the same difficulty setting, seeds disjoint from the existing episodes, every tic recorded, encoded with the pinned encoder, and the same validity rules (one life, one map). Nothing about the agent or the maps is changed.

**3. Split: 6 adapt, 4 held out on every map, fixed once by seed; the data curve takes the first 1, 2, 4, 6, 12 of the adapt episodes; held-out windows are a fresh fixed draw of 256 from the held-out episodes only.** Uniform held-out size keeps the per-map scores comparable; 4 episodes hold about 20,000 candidate windows, and the 256-window draw gives about plus or minus 0.2 dB per map in the zero-shot study, small against the map-to-map spread. The step-0 checkpoint scored on that draw is the zero-shot number on the curve.

**4. Distance range: 0.12 to 0.29 on the arenas, with only arena 7 far. Accept it for the paper and fix it in October with generated arenas.** Thirteen points over a narrower range has less power than 18 over 0.12 to 0.38, and the paper says the relation was tested on same-mode maps. Do not keep campaign maps as a "far arm": a far point measured under a coverage confound is worse than no far point. In October, generate arenas with Obsidian as MultiGen did, choose them by distance before any result (the frozen D is computable on new footage), and fill the far range with same-mode maps.

**5. What a reviewer could use to dismiss it, and the guard for each.** Coverage: the subset check and the uniform split. Selection on outcome: the map set and the pilot maps (8, 17, 6, 7) are fixed by distance rank before any adaptation result, and the record is in `RESEARCH_CONTEXT.md`. Leakage: D was computed on Sep 25 from the existing episodes; the new episodes enter only the adaptation and held-out sets, and the held-out windows never touch training. Agent behaviour: arena footage is fights, so motion is high on every target map; report motion and deaths per map beside the curve.

**Budget:** 148 episodes, 37 GB raw, about 20 to 50 minutes of CPU tonight, about 2.5 GPU-hours of encoding tomorrow behind the PixArt scoring.

**Disconfirming measurement:** after adaptation on the first 6 episodes of an arena, score the adapted model on the 4 held-out episodes and on 4 other adapt-excluded episodes drawn later in the recording; if the two scores differ by more than the per-map bootstrap interval on most arenas, coverage is not solved by six episodes and the split has to grow.
