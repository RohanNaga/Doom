# The distance question, second round: three views and where they land (2026-09-27, 06:00 EDT)

Sources: `main-distance-usefulness-view-2026-09-27.md` (Fable 5.1, main session), `opus-distance-usefulness-view-2026-09-27.md` (Opus 5.5 high, thread in its worktree commit 03aa361), `astra-distance-usefulness-view-2026-09-27.md` (Astra, thread 01a0e233-0874-7e60-93b7-437f9f88ceb8); numbers in `distance-fresh-numbers-2026-09-27.md` and `tools/distance_outcome_corr.py`. All three recomputed the table independently and it reproduces exactly.

## Where the three agree (no decision needed)

1. **No pre-computed distance orders the 13 arenas.** D, directed coverage and the transfer gap G have |rho| <= 0.42 against zero-shot advantage, endpoint, gain and crossing budget; partialling out persistence PSNR or motion changes nothing; leave-one-arena-out fits are no better than predicting the mean; the pre-registered incremental test (does a distance add anything beyond zero-shot error and motion) fails for D, and the small improvements coverage and G show are in opposite directions across twelve tests, so none is a finding.
2. **The zero-shot latent skill orders them** (rho +0.89 with the endpoint, -0.65 with the budget, LOO error halved), and it costs one evaluation pass on footage the adaptation needs anyway. Opus adds the fact that makes this a footage property rather than a U-Net quirk: PixArt's zero-shot advantage ranks the arenas the same way (rho +0.95 with the U-Net's) while D against PixArt is -0.37.
3. **D survives only as the family separator.** Training maps' validation episodes score D 0.026 to 0.069 (frozen table) against every unseen arena's fresh D of 0.118 or more; coverage and G fail that floor (training validation episodes inside the unseen range), so they are worse than D at the one thing D does. Opus's diagnosis: coverage and G mostly track motion (persistence PSNR -0.69 and +0.63), G's neighbour predictor is worse than copy-last even on the training maps, and shuffling the next-frame change moves coverage by 0.004 to 0.010 against a spread of 0.087 between arenas.
4. **The paper on Sep 30** states D as a family separator, states that the ordering criterion set before the runs failed, gives coverage and G one sentence as pre-registered candidates that did not separate the families, and attributes the arena ordering to the model's zero-shot skill. All three withdraw any "distance predicts adaptation cost" claim.
5. **October** is a constructed-shift experiment: retextured and re-laid-out training maps plus generated arenas chosen by distance strata before any outcome is read, two seeds, sealed test maps. Cost estimates range from 40 (main) to 80 (Opus) to about 270 (Astra, 48 arenas) A4000-hours; the design is the same.

## Where they differ (Rohan's call, or resolved below)

- **x axis of the adaptation scatter.** Main and Opus: the zero-shot latent skill S0 itself (decoder-free). Astra: the deficit from home in latent skill (home skill minus S0), same ranking, reads as "how far from home". Resolution proposed: plot S0, label the home skill as a vertical line; the deficit is then visible without a second axis. Learning curves keep updates on x (all three).
- **What "adapting the metric" means.** Main: make it model-in-the-loop (the skill is the distance; score it on the adaptation episodes with three sampler seeds, about 5 A4000-hours). Opus: keep a model-free coverage but per window, stratified by motion and control, tested against per-window skill (one CPU day). Astra: a control-conditioned innovation transfer gap G* with local ridge prediction and calibration on source folds (4 to 16 CPU-hours), frozen before any prospective test. These are three different October projects, not Sep 30 work; none of them belongs in the paper.
- **How to phrase the zero-shot predictor.** Astra: "exploratory, model-specific diagnostic", because A0 enters the crossing threshold and the arenas are now development data. Opus and main: a result, with the PixArt agreement as the evidence it is not circular. Resolution proposed: report it as a result with n = 13 stated and the PixArt cross-check in the same sentence, and say the arenas are development data for any future distance.
- **Astra's audit note on the floor**: the fresh-set file has no training-map validation D, so the family gap there rests on the resampling floor (0.093). The frozen table does have validation D for the training maps (0.026 to 0.069), measured with the same code; the paper cites those. Recomputing them on the fresh pipeline is a 20-minute job if a reviewer would want it in the same file.
