# Main session's view: one cohesive evaluation (2026-09-26, 19:45 EDT)

Written before reading the Astra and Opus views, against `brief-evaluation-cohesion-2026-09-26.md`.

**One-liners.** (1) Primary quantity: the decoder-free latent skill for everything that compares maps or curve points; pixels are the rendering layer, shown beside it, never the comparison. (2) The tuned decoder moves only pixel numbers and the ceiling; it cannot move latent skill; the paper's pixel columns use the tuned decoder (training-map frames only, frozen) as the renderer, with the stock decoder in the appendix, and the tuned ceiling on unseen arenas is the rendering leg. (3) The Sunday rescore saves per-window rows for every quantity with both decoders once, so no table is rerun. (4) Story: the model's advantage over copy-last lives in the latents; it shrinks off the training maps; the decoder adds a fixed rendering floor that raw persistence does not pay. (5) Checks: what `psnr_dec` scores against, whether the decoded comparison flatters the model, and window composition on the fresh set.

## 1. Primary quantity

The model predicts latents. Copy-last in latent space is defined on every map with no decoder and no training. So the primary is the latent skill S = -10 · mean over windows of log10(model latent MSE / copy-last latent MSE), the same form as a PSNR difference (a log-form skill score), aggregated as a mean of logs so static windows do not dominate. It is the primary for (a) the in-domain table (one number per backbone and map), (b) the unseen-arena comparison (S per arena, its drop from the training maps, and the distance analysis), and (c) the adaptation curves (S against updates; the cost target at half the training-map S). Pixel quantities describe how that latent advantage renders; they are shown beside S, with the reconstruction ceiling, and they are not the axis of any comparison across maps.

## 2. What the tuned decoder changes

It changes only what passes through it: raw PSNR and LPIPS against the true frame, the reconstruction ceiling, and the decoded comparison (slightly, because a better decoder shrinks the floor on both sides). It cannot change latent skill, the latent ratio, the directional check, or anything in the rollouts, which never decode. The right use: the paper's pixel columns are rendered by the tuned decoder, trained on training-map frames only and frozen, because that is the decoder a user of the released model would use. The stock-decoder columns go in the appendix as the reproducibility reference. The ceiling under the tuned decoder on the unseen arenas, compared with the training maps, is the rendering leg of the decomposition: if the tuned ceiling rises by 5 dB on training maps and by less on unseen arenas, that gap is a decoder-side domain shift and is reported as such; if it rises equally, rendering is not where the unseen-map loss lives, which the flat stock ceiling already suggests. Nothing waits on the decoder except pixel columns: the adaptation runs and the latent tables proceed on the stock-decoder-free latents.

## 3. What the Sunday rescore computes, per window

For each of the three backbones (U-Net 200k EMA, PixArt 200k EMA, SD 3.5 newest EMA) on the 13 arenas' held-out windows (32 per held-out episode, 256 per map) and the training maps' 512 validation windows: latent MSE of the model and of copy-last (so S and the ratio follow), the paired raw PSNR and LPIPS of the model and of raw persistence against the true frame, the decoded PSNR and LPIPS of the model and of copy-last against the decoded true frame, the reconstruction ceiling PSNR and LPIPS, the HUD variants, the window's episode, start tic, motion (latent change over the context), and phase; all of it twice, once with the stock decoder and once with the tuned decoder of that space. The scorer needs `copy_lpips_dec` added (a one-line addition beside `copy_psnr_dec`) and a `--decoder` path. Aggregation and every table then come from the rows.

## 4. The story and the table

Story: a world model trained on four arenas predicts the next latent better than copying the last one everywhere in the arena WAD, by 3 to 4 dB on its training maps and by 1 to 3 dB on the 13 arenas it never saw; that advantage lives in the latents and shrinks off the training maps. Rendering adds a fixed floor: the decoder reconstructs a true frame at only 23 to 25 dB, so against raw-frame persistence the rendered prediction falls below the reference wherever the footage is static enough for persistence to score above the floor. A decoder tuned on training-map frames raises the floor on every map (numbers Sunday) and does not close the latent gap. Table layout, one per backbone, columns grouped as training maps / unseen arenas: rows reconstruction ceiling, copy-last, model; column groups latent (S, ratio), decoded comparison (tuned decoder; stock in appendix), raw against the true frame with the raw persistence row. Precedents: OccWorld Table 1 and DINO-Foresight Table 2 for ceiling / copy-last / model in one table; PredNet for copy-last on unseen data; GameNGen §4 for the decoder fine-tuning recipe.
