## Question 1. Should the LoRA adaptation to an unseen arena also train the decoder?

Facts: the world model predicts latents; rollouts and the directional check never decode; the decoder (a frozen-encoder VAE decoder, being fine-tuned tonight on training-map frames only, GameNGen recipe) is used only to render pixels for PSNR and LPIPS. The stock decoder's reconstruction ceiling is flat across maps (23.9 dB training maps, 23.7 unseen arenas, full frame, with the HUD carrying 39 to 68 percent of its error); the tuned decoder's ceiling per arena is unknown until it runs. An earlier decision (Sep 26 18:30) dropped decoder adaptation to unseen maps for the paper because the flat stock ceiling bounded its gain at about 0.2 dB and a decoder tuned on an arena's footage could clean up the backbone's errors and blur the decomposition. Rohan now asks whether the per-arena LoRA run should train the decoder too, given that the paper's cost target is now measured in pixels through a decoder.

Answer: for the primary adaptation arm, should the decoder be (a) frozen at the training-map-tuned weights, (b) adapted per arena jointly with the LoRA, or (c) adapted per arena in a separate arm reported as the rendering leg? What does each do to the headline cost (decoded advantage over copy-last, where both sides pass through the same decoder), to the decomposition (control response, latent prediction error, rendering), and to the claim "cheap adaptation of the world model"? What would a per-arena decoder adaptation cost (the tune is about 1 hour per map on an A6000 with the current script) and what would it show?
