## Notes and caveats per system

- **GameNGen's holdout is trajectory-level, not map-level**: the 2048 trajectories come from "5 different levels" that are also the training levels (arXiv 2408.14837). So 29.43 dB is a seen-map, teacher-forced, single-step number. It is not a long-horizon number and not an unseen-map number.
- **GameNGen releases nothing** — no code, no weights, no data (2408.14837). Every quantitative Doom U-Net figure in the literature is therefore unreproducible by construction.
- **MultiGen is the only paper that reports a Doom U-Net baseline under autoregressive rollout**, and its "GameNGen" row is MultiGen's own memory-free reimplementation, not Valevski et al.'s model. MultiGen states no architecture size, no latent space, no resolution and no training hyperparameters at all (2603.06679), so its numbers cannot anchor a compute- or resolution-matched comparison. VERIFY: whether v2 of the paper changed Table 1.
- **PlayGen is a DiT, not a U-Net** (131M, depth 12) and already claims Doom (2412.00887). It is the correct citation for "DiTs have been applied to Doom", and its numbers are at 128x128 with its own VAE, not SD KL-f8.
- **DIAMOND's FPS model is CS:GO, and it is evaluated qualitatively** — no PSNR/LPIPS/FVD for CS:GO (2405.12399). Its quantitative claims are Atari RL returns. It does hold out 0.5M frames / 500 episodes properly.
- **Ha & Schmidhuber report no image-quality metric at all** for VizDoom; the metric is survival time (worldmodels.github.io). It cannot be compared to PSNR/LPIPS rows.
- **Neither open GameNGen reproduction reports a single metric**, and the Taketani README concedes quality is far from the paper's. VERIFY: no reproduction published between mid-2025 and Sep 2026 reports Doom PSNR/LPIPS — searched arXiv and GitHub for "Doom world model", "VizDoom diffusion", "GameNGen reproduction", "open GameNGen"; the only 2026 Doom hits were MultiGen and a non-world-model Doom *control* paper (arXiv 2604.07385).

## How a comparison to our benchmark can be phrased

**Comparable at the protocol level.** (1) Reporting metrics as a function of rollout horizon: PlayGen (1/16/32/64/128/1024) and MultiGen (step ranges) both do it, so our horizon curves sit in an established format. (2) Action-following as a metric that survives divergence: PlayGen's ActAcc/ProbDiff and Matrix-Game's keyboard/mouse accuracy legitimize our IDM row. (3) Held-out episodes from seen maps: GameNGen, PlayGen and DIAMOND all use this, and it is exactly what our 450-episode split gives. (4) Small-budget training on one or two consumer cards: DIAMOND's 2.9-day single-4090 run is the precedent that makes our budget publishable.

**Not comparable.** Resolution and latent space differ everywhere (our 320x240 KL-f8 vs PlayGen's 128x128 own-VAE vs DIAMOND's 56x30 pixels), and PSNR/LPIPS are resolution- and codec-sensitive, so no cross-paper number is a like-for-like. Training corpora differ by two to three orders of magnitude (900M/200M frames vs our ~1.05M) and are private for GameNGen and MultiGen. Compute differs by orders of magnitude (128 TPU-v5e for 700k steps vs our 90k updates on A4000s). MultiGen reports no architecture or hyperparameters, so its rows cannot even be described. Nothing in prior Doom work reports unseen-map transfer.

**What a reviewer would accept as "better than prior Doom U-Nets".** Not a cross-paper number beat: claiming we exceed 29.43 dB is indefensible, and claiming we beat MultiGen's 0.442 LPIPS is meaningless at a different resolution on a different corpus. What survives review is the internal, matched claim: *within one recipe (same data, latents, context, objective, 90k updates, batch 32) our strongest backbone beats the SD 1.4 U-Net arm — the backbone prior Doom work used — on held-out episodes, and we release the dataset, weights and harness so the comparison is reproducible, which no prior Doom result is.* Then a separate, clearly labelled reference table of prior numbers with protocol columns (resolution, corpus size, teacher-forced vs autoregressive, holdout type), stated as context rather than a baseline. Add the persistence reference on every horizon row, since our own audit shows models at or below frozen-seed persistence by horizon 64 and no prior Doom paper reports such a reference — a reviewer who knows that will otherwise assume our gains are copy-frame gains.
