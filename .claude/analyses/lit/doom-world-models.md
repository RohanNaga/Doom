# Doom / game world models — evidence cards

All five build an action-conditioned next-frame predictor that is rolled out autoregressively as an interactive simulator, and four of the five are diffusion models whose only memory is a short window of past frames concatenated on the channel dimension (GameNGen N=64, DIAMOND L=4, MultiGen an unspecified L, gameNgen-repro 9); PlayGen is the exception, replacing the frame stack with a diffusion-forcing RNN hidden state. They diverge on almost everything else. Latent space: GameNGen, MultiGen and the repro use the Stable Diffusion KL-f8 autoencoder (4 channels, 8x), PlayGen trains its own 55M-parameter VAE to a 4x32x32 latent, DIAMOND stays in pixel space at 64x64. Objective: v-prediction for GameNGen, MultiGen, PlayGen and the repro; EDM preconditioning with an L2 target for DIAMOND, which is the one that argues explicitly that the parameterisation (EDM over DDPM) is what buys stability at 3 denoising steps. Initialisation: GameNGen, MultiGen (implicitly) and the repro fine-tune Stable Diffusion 1.4; DIAMOND and PlayGen train small models (4M and 131M) from scratch. Scale spans four orders of magnitude — 900M training frames on 128 TPU-v5e for GameNGen and 200M for PlayGen, against DIAMOND's 100k-frame Atari budget on one RTX 4090 for 2.9 days and the repro's 2.47M frames. Every one of them lands on roughly the same inference point (3-4 denoising steps, ~20 FPS on one accelerator), and every one of them mitigates autoregressive drift by corrupting the context — GameNGen's 10 noise buckets up to level 0.7, copied verbatim by MultiGen and the repro, and reframed by PlayGen as per-frame diffusion-forcing noise levels. Evaluation is the weakest shared axis: GameNGen reports PSNR/LPIPS/FVD plus a 10-rater human study, PlayGen proposes action-accuracy metrics (ActAcc, ProbDiff) because pixel metrics decay under divergence, MultiGen uses SSIM/LPIPS plus a VLM judge for opponent presence, DIAMOND reports RL return, and the repro reports nothing at all.

## GameNGen (arXiv 2408.14837)

- **Task and game**: Interactive real-time simulation of DOOM by a neural network. Formalised as an "Interactive World Simulation": given past observations and actions, predict the distribution over the next frame, then feed it back. Runs "at over 20 frames per second on a single TPU".
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: "We train all simulation models from a pretrained checkpoint of Stable Diffusion 1.4, unfreezing all U-Net parameters." Parameter count: not stated. The text encoder / cross-attention path is repurposed rather than removed (see action conditioning).
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: The Stable Diffusion v1.4 latent auto-encoder, which "compresses 8x8 pixel patches into 4 latent channels". Frames are 320x240 padded to 320x256, so latents are 4x32x40. The decoder is separately fine-tuned: "we train just the decoder of the latent auto-encoder using an MSE loss computed against the target frame pixels" (batch size 2,048, other training parameters identical to the denoiser). The encoder is frozen and autoregression conditions on latents, not pixels, so decoder fine-tuning does not feed back into the rollout. LPIPS for decoder training is explicitly left to future work.
- **Objective**: v-prediction. "We change the diffusion loss parameterization to be v-prediction (Salimans & Ho 2022a)." Noise schedule: "The noise schedule is linear, similarly to Rombach et al. (2022)."
- **Context handling**: Past frames are encoded with the VAE and concatenated in the latent channel dimension to the noised latents. "We use a context length of 64 (i.e. the model is provided its own last 64 predictions as well as the last 64 actions)." Noise augmentation on context: Gaussian noise is added to the encoded context frames at a level sampled uniformly up to a maximum, discretised into buckets with a learned embedding per bucket — "we use a maximal noise level of 0.7, with 10 embedding buckets". Ablation (Table 1, 8912 test-set examples from 5 levels, 200k training steps, frozen decoder): PSNR 20.94 (N=1), 22.03 (2), 22.26 (4), 22.26 (8), 22.28 (16), 22.31 (32), 22.36 (64); LPIPS 0.358, 0.304, 0.298, 0.296, 0.296, 0.296, 0.295. Without noise augmentation, "quality degrades quickly after 10-20 frames"; with it, degradation is prevented.
- **Action conditioning**: "we learn an embedding A_emb from each action into a single token and replace the cross attention from the text into this encoded actions sequence." Action space is "the set of key presses and mouse movements"; exact cardinality not stated. Context-frame conditioning is dropped with probability 0.1 to enable CFG.
- **Data**: A PPO agent (simple CNN feature net producing a 512-dim representation, 2-layer MLP actor and critic heads, 8 games in parallel, replay buffer 512, agent batch 64 for 10 epochs per iteration, agent lr 1e-4) is run for "a total of 10M environment steps" with an environment-specific reward function. "The entirety of the agent's actions and observations corpus during training is maintained and becomes the training dataset." "Overall we generate 900M frames for training." Resolution 320x240 padded to 320x256. Evaluation uses trajectories from 5 different levels. Data release: not stated. Assets/licensing: not stated.
- **Training recipe**: Adafactor without weight decay, gradient clipping 1.0, constant learning rate 2e-5, batch size 128, 700,000 training steps, 128 TPU-v5e devices with data parallelism. Warmup: not stated. EMA: not stated. Precision: not stated. Wall-clock: not stated.
- **Sampler and inference**: DDIM. "Using just 4 denoising steps leads to a total U-Net cost of 40ms (and total inference cost of 50ms, including the auto encoder) or 20 frames per second." A single denoiser step and a single auto-encoder evaluation each take 10ms on a TPU-v5. Classifier-free guidance is applied only to the past-observations condition, with weight 1.5. Sampling-step ablation on 2048 teacher-forced frames of 35FPS data (Table 3): PSNR/LPIPS 25.47/0.255 (1 step), 31.91/0.205 (2), 32.58/0.198 (4), 32.55/0.196 (8), 32.44/0.196 (16), 32.32/0.196 (32), 32.19/0.197 (64); a distilled 1-step model reaches 31.10/0.208 and 50 FPS. They ship the 4-step non-distilled model. Generating 4 samples in parallel and averaging or median-selecting was tried and dropped (needs 4 TPUs, no real gain).
- **Evaluation**: Teacher-forced single-frame prediction over "a random holdout of 2048 trajectories taken in 5 different levels": "PSNR of 29.43 and an LPIPS of 0.249", described as comparable to JPEG at quality 20-30. FVD over "a random holdout of 512 trajectories": 114.02 at 16 frames (0.8s), 186.23 at 32 frames (1.6s). Human study: "10 human raters with 130 random short clips (of lengths 1.6 seconds and 3.2 seconds)" shown side by side with the real game; raters picked the real game "58% or 60% of the time" for 1.6s and 3.2s clips respectively. Data-policy ablation: agent-generated vs random-policy data, both trained 700k steps with decoder, evaluated on 2048 human-play trajectories from 5 levels — single frame 25.06 (agent) vs 24.42 (random); after 3s of autoregression 19.02 vs 16.84; a manual easy/medium/hard split of 456 examples (112/112/232) shows the agent's advantage concentrated in the medium bucket.
- **Reproducibility notes**: Code: not stated. Weights: not stated. Data: not stated. Paper license CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. This is why the third-party repro below matters.
- **Relevance to our setting**: This is the recipe our design follows, not one we reproduce exactly: SD 1.4 init, KL-f8 latents, v-prediction, context concatenated on latent channels, and noise augmentation inspired by its §3.2.1 (its level is "sampled uniformly up to a maximal value"; our variance-preserving blend with 0.7 and 10 buckets is not stated in the paper, so equivalence is not claimed; review 2026-09-22 H5). Their context ablation is our strongest justification for a 32-frame window: 32 vs 64 differs by 0.05 dB PSNR and 0.001 LPIPS, so the channel-count cost of 64 buys nothing measurable. What does not fit in ~4 A6000 card-days is the scale: 700k steps at batch 128 on 128 TPU-v5e over 900M frames is several orders of magnitude beyond our budget, so our numbers will sit far below their 29.43 PSNR and we should frame our contribution as a controlled backbone comparison at fixed budget, not a quality match. Their decoder fine-tune is cheap and separable from the U-Net run, and is the one quality trick here we could afford to add late.

## MultiGen (arXiv 2603.06679)

- **Task and game**: Doom, in two applications. (1) Level design: level-conditioned gameplay generation from a user-authored top-down map of coarse line geometry. (2) Multiplayer: synchronised per-player first-person rollouts in a shared world. The system is factored into Memory (explicit map plus player pose), Observation (next-frame diffusion), and Dynamics (pose update) modules, explicitly positioned against GameNGen's "frames-as-state" design.
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: Observation module is "a standard UNet" denoiser; parameter count not stated, initialisation checkpoint not stated (no mention of Stable Diffusion anywhere in the paper). Dynamics module is "a small transformer encoder" predicting an incremental pose update from action, geometry, and pooled U-Net bottleneck features; layer count and parameters not stated. Memory module is not a network — it is the map M (2D vertices and wall segments) plus pose p_t = (x_t, y_t, theta_t).
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: Not stated. The paper never names a VAE, latent channel count, compression factor, or image resolution.
- **Objective**: "the standard velocity-parameterization objective", loss L_obs = E[|| v_phi(.|o_{t-L+1:t}, r_t, a_t; tau) - v*(o_{t+1}, tau) ||_2^2]. Dynamics is supervised with "an l2 loss on translation and a wrapped-angle error on orientation". Noise schedule: not stated.
- **Context handling**: An L-frame context window concatenated as channels at the U-Net input, alongside the geometric conditioning channels. The value of L is not stated anywhere in the paper, and there is no context-length ablation. Noise augmentation: "we follow prior work [Ho et al.; GameNGen] and corrupt all context frames with Gaussian noise at a randomly sampled noise scale during training". Specific noise levels or bucket counts: not stated. At inference they use history guidance — "the conditional branch receives the clean context frames, while the unconditional branch receives a noised version of the context".
- **Action conditioning**: "We represent the discrete action a_t with a learned embedding and inject it into the observation UNet through cross-attention conditioning tokens." Separately, geometry enters through the convolutional input: the memory module ray-traces a 1D depth vector over the agent's field of view, converts it to disparity (inverse depth), maps that 1D signal to a spatial tensor at U-Net input resolution, and concatenates it as additional channels.
- **Data**: Level design — "100 procedurally generated maps" from the Obsidian map generator, explored by "a pre-trained Doom agent", yielding "over 10 million gameplay frames" paired with actions and player poses. Multiplayer — ViZDoom deathmatch, "one pre-trained agent playing against four identical agents", again "over 10 million gameplay frames", each sequence recording the ego action, all active player poses, and the map layout; trained and evaluated on a single map. Resolution, frame rate, episode counts, hours, holdout size, release status, assets/licensing: all not stated.
- **Training recipe**: Not stated. No optimizer, learning rate, warmup, batch size, step count, GPU type or count for training, wall-clock, EMA, or precision appears anywhere in the paper.
- **Sampler and inference**: Number of denoising steps not stated; sampler not stated; no distillation mentioned. History guidance is used to stabilise long rollouts. "the full system runs at approximately 20 FPS using a single NVIDIA A100 per player" — each player runs their own Observation/Dynamics instance against shared memory, so adding players adds GPUs rather than latency. Multiplayer network latency: not stated.
- **Evaluation**: Level design (Table 1, reported over rollout step ranges All / 1-128 / 128-196) — SSIM: GameNGen baseline 0.457 / 0.476 / 0.418, Ours 0.469 / 0.484 / 0.438. LPIPS: GameNGen 0.442 / 0.389 / 0.549, Ours 0.416 / 0.375 / 0.496. No PSNR, no FVD. The only baseline is a GameNGen-like memory-free model. Multiplayer (Table 2) — opponent-presence detection judged by a pre-trained VLM against the same VLM's labels on ground-truth frames: split-screen no-memory baseline 65.31% accuracy / 86.86% precision / 44.59% recall; ours 75.38% / 88.12% / 65.07%. Held-out protocol sizes: not stated. Human study: none.
- **Reproducibility notes**: Project page https://ryanpo.com/multigen. No statement about code, weights, or data release.
- **Relevance to our setting**: This is the closest thing to a 2026 state-of-the-art framing of the GameNGen recipe, and it reuses our exact ingredients — U-Net denoiser, velocity objective, channel-concatenated context, GameNGen-style noised-context training. It is also a warning about reporting standards: it states no architecture size, no latent space, and no training hyperparameters at all, which means it cannot anchor any compute or quality comparison for us. The transferable idea inside our budget is the cheap auxiliary conditioning channel: their 1D ray-traced disparity map broadcast to a spatial tensor and concatenated at the input costs almost nothing and is the mechanism that fixes long-horizon layout drift. Their SSIM/LPIPS-by-step-range protocol (early vs late rollout windows) is a better drift metric than a single averaged number and is free for us to adopt.

## DIAMOND (arXiv 2405.12399)

- **Task and game**: Two settings. (1) RL in imagination on the Atari 100k benchmark, 26 games, where "an agent is only allowed to take 100k actions in the environment, which is roughly equivalent to 2 hours of human gameplay". (2) The diffusion world model standing alone as an interactive neural game engine for Counter-Strike: Global Offensive on the map Dust II, with no RL agent involved.
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: "a standard U-Net 2D" for the vector field. 4M parameters for the Atari models; 381M for the CS:GO model, "including 51M for the upsampler". "We trained DIAMOND from scratch for 5 random seeds on each game" — no pretrained checkpoint anywhere. A separate CNN-LSTM reward/termination model and a CNN-LSTM actor-critic complete the agent.
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: None. The model operates directly in pixel space at 64x64x3 for Atari. For CS:GO, "we reduce the resolution from (280x150) to (56x30) for world modeling" and add a second, smaller diffusion model as an upsampler back to the original resolution. The paper's whole premise is that discretising into latent tokens loses visual details that matter for control.
- **Objective**: EDM (Karras et al. 2022) preconditioning with an L2 target. The network F_theta is trained against (1/c_out)(x_clean - c_skip * x_noised), with c_skip = sigma_data^2/(sigma_data^2 + sigma^2(tau)), c_in and c_out chosen to keep input and output at unit variance, c_noise an empirical transform of the noise level, and sigma_data = 0.5. Noise levels are sampled from EDM's empirically chosen log-normal distribution to concentrate training on medium noise. The DDPM-vs-EDM comparison is the paper's central ablation: DDPM in the few-step regime "leads to severe compounding error", EDM "appears much more stable over long time horizons, even for a single denoising step".
- **Context handling**: Frame stacking. "we keep a buffer of L past observations and actions that we use to condition the model. We concatenate these past observations to the next noisy observation channel-wise". L = 4 (Table 3: "number of conditioning observations and actions" = 4). Burn-in length 4, training sequence length 19. No noise augmentation on the context — stability comes from the EDM parameterisation instead. The limitations section calls frame stacking "a minimal mechanism", and an initial cross-attention architecture in Appendix M was found less effective than frame stacking.
- **Action conditioning**: "we input actions through adaptive group normalization layers in the residual blocks of the U-Net", together with the diffusion time. Action space "Discrete (up to 18 actions)"; frameskip 4.
- **Data**: Atari — 100k environment actions per game, collected online by the agent itself (100 environment steps per epoch, epsilon-greedy 0.01), observations 64x64x3, termination on life loss, max 30 noops. CS:GO — the Online dataset of Pearce and Zhu (2022), "5.5M frames (95 hours) of online human gameplay captured at 16Hz from the map Dust II"; "We randomly hold out 0.5M frames (corresponding to 500 episodes, or 8 hours) for testing, and use the remaining 5M frames (87 hours) for training." The CS:GO dataset is third-party and public; DIAMOND itself releases code, agents, videos and playable world models. Assets/licensing: not stated.
- **Training recipe**: AdamW, learning rate 1e-4, epsilon 1e-8, weight decay 1e-2 on the world model, batch size 32, 1000 epochs x 400 training steps per epoch. "Each run utilized around 12GB of VRAM and took approximately 2.9 days on a single Nvidia RTX 4090 (1.03 GPU years in total)" for the Atari suite (26 games x 5 seeds). CS:GO: "The combined model was trained for 12 days on an RTX 4090." EMA: not stated. Precision: not stated. Warmup: not stated.
- **Sampler and inference**: Euler's method, 3 denoising steps ("3 NFE per frame compared to 16 NFE per frame for IRIS"). Higher-order samplers and stochastic sampling were available but not used for the dynamics model. For CS:GO they add stochastic sampling and raise the upsampler to 10 steps while keeping the dynamics model at 3, "with the model running at 10Hz on an RTX 3090".
- **Evaluation**: Atari 100k, 5 seeds, mean and interquartile mean of human-normalised scores. Mean: Random 0.000, Human 1.000, SimPLe 0.332, TWM 0.956, IRIS 1.046, DreamerV3 1.097, STORM 1.266, DIAMOND 1.459. IQM: 0.000, 1.000, 0.130, 0.459, 0.501, 0.497, 0.636, DIAMOND 0.641. Superhuman on 11 of 26 games. Stratified bootstrap confidence intervals are reported rather than point estimates alone. CS:GO is evaluated qualitatively (videos, human play) — no FVD/PSNR headline. No human rater study.
- **Reproducibility notes**: "we release our code, agents, videos and playable world models at https://diamond-wm.github.io". The CS:GO training data is a public third-party dataset.
- **Relevance to our setting**: DIAMOND is the existence proof that a serious diffusion world model fits on one consumer GPU — 2.9 days on a single RTX 4090 per Atari run is roughly our 4 A6000 card-day budget, and the 12-day CS:GO run is only about 3x it. It differs from us on every axis we hold fixed: pixel space instead of KL-f8 latents, EDM instead of velocity, 4 frames of context instead of 32, adaptive group norm instead of cross-attention for actions, and training from scratch instead of SD 1.4 init. The one result we should take seriously before committing to our recipe is the DDPM-vs-EDM ablation: it claims the parameterisation, not context noise augmentation, is what keeps few-step rollouts from compounding error, which is a directly testable alternative to the GameNGen noise-bucket trick and would cost us one extra run. Their 4M-parameter Atari U-Net also shows how much of the quality gap is resolution and data rather than capacity.

## PlayGen / Playable Game Generation (arXiv 2412.00887)

- **Task and game**: Playable game generation, validated on the 2D game Super Mario Bros (Mario-AI-Framework) and the 3D game Doom (ViZDoom). Three claimed criteria: real-time interaction, sufficient visual quality, and accurate simulation of interactive mechanics, "sustained even after over 1000 frames of gameplay on an NVIDIA RTX 2060 GPU".
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: A custom VAE (55M parameters) plus a latent diffusion model (131M parameters) whose backbone is N DiT blocks — depth 12, hidden size 384, patch size 2, 6 heads, time embedding dim 192, action embedding dim 192. A CNN head p_phi predicts the clean latent from the hidden state. Initialisation from a pretrained checkpoint is never mentioned; the paper describes training the VAE first, then freezing it and training the LDM, which reads as from scratch, but the paper does not say so explicitly — **not stated**.
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: Own VAE, not Stable Diffusion's. Channels 128, channel multiplier [1, 2, 4], 2 ResNet blocks, latent shape 4x32x32 from 128x128 input (4 channels, 4x spatial compression). Trained with "the weighted sum of reconstruction loss, perceptual loss with LPIPS and KL-penalty", KL loss weight 1e-6, perceptual loss weight 1.
- **Objective**: "we freeze the VAE and train the LDM with the objective of velocity parameterization (Salimans & Ho, 2022)". Diffusion steps 1000, noise schedule sigmoid. The architecture is diffusion forcing (Chen et al. 2024): each frame carries its own noise level k.
- **Context handling**: Not a frame stack. "we employ an autoregressive RNN-like model structure for LDM" — a hidden state z_t (z-shape 32x32x32) carries the influence of the current latent and all past latents and actions, and the model denoises x_{t+1} conditioned on z_t and a_t. "we concatenate z_t with noisy latent observation x_{t+1}^{k_{t+1}} as the input of LDM". Training sequence length 36. There is no separate noise augmentation on a context window; the per-frame noise level k of diffusion forcing plays that role.
- **Action conditioning**: "we concatenate embeddings of action a_t and noisy level k_{t+1} as the conditions used for cross attention mechanism". Action embedding dimension 192.
- **Data**: Doom — "we collect 900M frames of transitions based on the ViZDoom, and then sample 200M balanced transitions for training". Super Mario Bros — 236M frames collected, 50M balanced transitions for training. "All frames (during training and testing) are at a resolution of 128x128." Collection uses a hybrid of a random agent and other agents (engine-provided or RL), where at each step the agent takes a random action with probability p and follows the other agent otherwise, with actions repeated to look more human; 200 timesteps per sample, agent spawned at a random map position each sample, and in Doom a per-episode "preferred action" is sampled and given higher probability. Cluster-based balanced sampling and a self-supervised long-tailed transition queue then reshape the distribution. Hours, episode counts, map/level identity, and dataset release: not stated. Assets/licensing: not stated.
- **Training recipe**: VAE — batch size 32, learning rate 4.5e-6. LDM — batch size 8, learning rate 1e-4. Optimizer, warmup, number of steps, hardware for training, wall-clock, EMA, and precision are all **not stated**. The only hardware named anywhere is the RTX 2060 used for inference timing.
- **Sampler and inference**: DDIM. "With only 4 DDIM sampling timesteps, our game-generative model is able to simulate both video games Super Mario Bros and Doom at 20 FPS on NVIDIA RTX 2060". Table 2 measures the tradeoff: 4 steps = 20 FPS, 8 steps = 10 FPS, 16 steps = 5 FPS. No distillation. Guidance: not stated.
- **Evaluation**: Table 1, by prediction length (1 / 16 / 32 / 64 / 128 / 1024). **Doom** — LPIPS 0.165 / 0.253 / 0.285 / 0.311 / 0.346 / 0.472; PSNR 23.81 / 21.28 / 20.41 / 20.03 / 19.24 / 17.25; FID 76.48 / 50.37 / 58.75 / 60.86 / 72.44 / 136.40; FVD - / 390.30 / 622.51 / 711.09 / 730.29 / 2176.94; ActAcc - / - / 0.858 / 0.851 / 0.848 / 0.822; ProbDiff - / - / 0.019 / 0.020 / 0.022 / 0.028. **Super Mario Bros** — LPIPS 0.022 / 0.043 / 0.062 / 0.083 / 0.122 / 0.222; PSNR 33.81 / 28.06 / 26.18 / 24.56 / 22.59 / 18.19; FID 15.24 / 7.09 / 6.12 / 7.52 / 12.85 / 50.91; FVD - / 195.36 / 105.44 / 109.31 / 123.49 / 173.06; ActAcc - / - / 0.803 / 0.801 / 0.790 / 0.789; ProbDiff - / - / 0.056 / 0.059 / 0.063 / 0.065. The two proposed metrics come from a Valid Action Model (VAM) trained on the same balanced dataset, input sequence length 32 with 8 preceding and 8 succeeding context frames, so the metrics only apply to sequences longer than 17 frames: ActAcc is the accuracy of the VAM's recovered action against the executed action, and ProbDiff measures the probability gap between actions so that action-invariant moments are not penalised. Balanced sampling ablation (Table 3) improves Doom metrics by "1.9% to 57.7%" over the raw 900M-frame dataset. Denoise-step sensitivity on Doom: at 4 steps FVD degrades to 1156.66 versus 622.51 at 8, while ActAcc only drops 0.858 to 0.834. Human study: none — the paper's stated contribution is replacing human evaluation with automated playability metrics.
- **Reproducibility notes**: "Our code is publicly available: https://github.com/GreatX3/Playable-Game-Generation. Our playable demo generated by AI is: http://124.156.151.207/". Weights and data release: not stated.
- **Relevance to our setting**: PlayGen is our nearest neighbour in architecture — a DiT operating on VAE latents with velocity parameterisation — but it uses its own small VAE (4x32x32 from 128x128) rather than SD 1.4's KL-f8, trains at 131M parameters rather than DiT-XL/2's ~675M, and replaces the frame stack with a diffusion-forcing recurrent state, so it is a different point in design space than our channel-stacked 32-frame context. Its Doom numbers are the right comparison target for our absolute quality (PSNR 20.41 and LPIPS 0.285 at 32-frame prediction length, on 128x128), and unlike GameNGen they are reported as a function of rollout length, which is the protocol we should copy. The genuinely portable, budget-free contribution is ActAcc/ProbDiff: a small action-classifier trained on our own 1.05M frames gives us a controllability metric that does not collapse when the rollout diverges, which pixel metrics do. Their 200M-frame training set is out of reach, but their balanced-sampling ablation suggests distribution coverage may matter more than raw count at our scale.

## gameNgen-repro by Arnaud Stiegler (https://github.com/arnaudstiegler/gameNgen-repro)

- **Task and game**: Open-source reproduction of GameNGen on ViZDoom. README: "This repo contains the code to reproduce the results of the gameNgen paper. The model / inference has not been optimized enough to get to 20 FPS as reported in the paper."
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: Stable Diffusion 1.4 — `PRETRAINED_MODEL_NAME_OR_PATH = "CompVis/stable-diffusion-v1-4"`, loading `UNet2DConditionModel.from_pretrained(..., subfolder="unet")` with `unet.requires_grad_(True)` (full fine-tune), plus the frozen CLIP tokenizer and text encoder. Parameter count: not stated in the repo. The input conv is replaced to take the frame stack: `new_in_channels = 4 * (BUFFER_SIZE + 1)` = 40 channels, a fresh 40 -> 320 3x3 conv with Xavier-uniform weights and zero bias. A second modification adds noise-bucket class embeddings: `unet.register_to_config(num_class_embeds=NUM_BUCKETS)` with `unet.class_embedding = nn.Embedding(10, time_embed_dim)`.
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: SD 1.4's KL-f8 autoencoder, frozen (`vae.requires_grad_(False)`), but loaded from a fine-tuned-decoder checkpoint: `AutoencoderKL.from_pretrained("arnaudstiegler/game-n-gen-vae-finetuned")`, with the comment "Based on the original GameNGen code, the vae decoder is finetuned on images from the training set to improve the quality of the images." `finetune_autoencoder.py` performs that fine-tune. Frames are resized to HEIGHT=240, WIDTH=320 and normalised to [-1, 1]; unlike the paper there is no padding to 320x256, so latents are 4x30x40.
- **Objective**: v-prediction on a DDIM scheduler — `noise_scheduler = DDIMScheduler.from_pretrained(...)` then `noise_scheduler.register_to_config(prediction_type="v_prediction")`, commented "This is what the paper uses". Load-time assertions enforce both `prediction_type == "v_prediction"` and `num_class_embeds == 10`. Noise schedule: SD 1.4's own (scaled linear), unchanged.
- **Context handling**: `BUFFER_SIZE = 9` past frames ("Number of past frames/actions we provide"), encoded to latents and concatenated on the channel dimension with the noised target latent, giving the 40-channel input conv. This is far short of the paper's 64. Short episodes are left-padded with black frames and zero actions. Noise augmentation copies the paper's constants: `MAX_NOISE_LEVEL = 0.7`, `NUM_BUCKETS = 10`, with `get_conditioning_noise` sampling `rand() * 0.7` per example, discretising into one of 10 buckets, and the bucket index entering through the U-Net class embedding. **Deviation worth noting**: `add_conditioning_noise` implements `frames + randn_like(frames) * noise_level` — plain additive noise with no `sqrt(1 - level)` rescaling of the signal, so it is not the diffusion forward process the paper's formulation implies.
- **Action conditioning**: `nn.Embedding(action_dim + 1, 768)` initialised N(0, 0.02), replacing the CLIP text embeddings as the cross-attention sequence, matching the paper's "replace the cross attention from the text into this encoded actions sequence". The action dimension is read from the dataset (`max(action)`), plus one for a do-nothing action. Conditioning frames are zeroed with probability `ZERO_OUT_ACTION_CONDITIONING_PROB = 0.1` ("Given by the paper") to enable CFG.
- **Data**: ViZDoom, scenario `deathmatch_simple`, level 5, frameskip 4, generated by a PPO agent trained in-repo (`train_ppo_parallel.py`, best checkpoint at `ViZDoomPPO/logs/models/deathmatch_simple/best_model.zip`) and dumped with `load_model_generate_dataset.py`. Two datasets are public on the Hub: `arnaudstiegler/vizdoom-5-episodes-skipframe-4-lvl5` (**10,212 rows / frames**, 187 MB) and `arnaudstiegler/vizdoom-500-episodes-skipframe-4-lvl5` (**2,470,229 rows / frames**, 48.1 GB) — both counts read from the HF datasets-server size API, not from the README. Hours and stored frame resolution: not stated in the README. Assets/licensing: repo carries a LICENSE file; generated model cards are published under `creativeml-openrail-m` (inherited from SD 1.4). One map/scenario only.
- **Training recipe**: The README's full-training command is `--dataset_name arnaudstiegler/vizdoom-500-episodes-skipframe-4-lvl5 --gradient_checkpointing --learning_rate 5e-5 --train_batch_size 12 --dataloader_num_workers 18 --num_train_epochs 3 --validation_steps 1000 --use_cfg --lr_scheduler cosine --push_to_hub`. Script defaults not overridden by that command: `torch.optim.AdamW` (or `bnb.optim.AdamW8bit` with `--use_8bit_adam`), betas 0.9/0.999, weight decay 1e-2, eps 1e-8, `max_grad_norm` 1.0, `gradient_accumulation_steps` 1, `lr_warmup_steps` 500, `mixed_precision` default None (fp32; fp16/bf16 available via accelerate). No EMA anywhere in the training script. Hardware, wall-clock, and cost: **not stated**. Published checkpoints correspond to 20k and 60k steps.
- **Sampler and inference**: DDIM, `DEFAULT_NUM_INFERENCE_STEPS = 10` (not the paper's 4), `CFG_GUIDANCE_SCALE = 1.5` matching the paper. `run_inference.py` generates a single frame; `run_autoregressive.py` rolls out, filling the buffer from the small dataset and replaying the dataset's actions. No distillation. Explicitly does not reach the paper's 20 FPS.
- **Evaluation**: None. No PSNR, LPIPS, FVD, or human study is reported; the only evidence is two rollout GIFs in the README.
- **Reproducibility notes**: Code on GitHub; two checkpoints on the Hub (`arnaudstiegler/gameNgen-baseline-20ksteps`, `arnaudstiegler/sd-model-gameNgen-60ksteps`) plus the fine-tuned VAE (`arnaudstiegler/game-n-gen-vae-finetuned`); two datasets on the Hub; the PPO agent checkpoint in-repo. This is the only fully open artifact chain of the five.
- **Relevance to our setting**: This is the single most reusable source for us — it is our exact SD 1.4 U-Net arm, already wired for KL-f8 latents, v-prediction, channel-concatenated context, 10 noise buckets at max level 0.7, and action-token cross-attention, and its 2.47M-frame ViZDoom dataset is roughly twice our 1.05M and directly downloadable. Two concrete things to borrow: the `conv_in` surgery (`4 * (context + 1)` channels, Xavier init, zero bias) generalises straight to our 32-frame stack at 132 channels, and the noise-bucket class-embedding trick is a five-line change to any U-Net. Two things to fix before trusting it: the additive `add_conditioning_noise` is not the forward process, and there is no EMA, no evaluation harness, and no reported hardware or wall-clock, so it gives us no baseline number — only code. At batch 12 with fp32 and gradient checkpointing this recipe is sized for a single consumer GPU, consistent with our one-A6000-per-run constraint, though we should raise the batch and move to bf16.
