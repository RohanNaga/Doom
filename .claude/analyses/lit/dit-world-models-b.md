# Transformer interactive world models — evidence cards (batch B)

*Compiled 2026-09-14. Every number below is quoted from the source. "not stated" means the source does not say it. "not retrieved" means the fetch of that page truncated before the section and I could not confirm either way — treat it as unknown, not as absent.*

**What they have in common, and what they hide.** Every large system here is a fine-tune of an existing video foundation checkpoint, not a from-scratch world model: Hunyuan-GameCraft from HunyuanVideo, Yume 1.0 from SkyReels-V2-14B-540P, Yume 1.5 from Wan2.2-5B, Cosmos-Video2World from its own Text2World predecessor. All of them put frames in a causal or spatio-temporal 3D-VAE latent space with 16 channels and 8x spatial / 4x temporal compression (Wan-VAE, Cosmos-CV8x8x8), and all of them treat frames as tokens under factorised spatio-temporal attention rather than stacking a frame history on the channel axis — the only channel-style trick that survives is Yume's FramePack-derived hierarchical compression of older history at (1,2,2)/(1,4,4)/(1,8,8). The objective has converged on rectified flow / flow matching with v-prediction (Hunyuan, both Yumes) with Cosmos the EDM holdout and MineWorld the discrete-token autoregressive holdout; interactivity comes not from the base objective but from a second stage — history-conditioned autoregressive rollout plus a step-reduction distillation (PCM in Hunyuan, adversarial distillation in Yume 1.0, Self-Forcing/DMD in Yume 1.5). Action conditioning is consistently cheap: a small convolutional Plucker encoder added by token addition (Hunyuan), pure text tokens with zero new parameters (both Yumes), 11 discrete tokens per action in the sequence (MineWorld). What they do not disclose, nearly uniformly: parameter counts of the added conditioning modules, wall-clock or GPU-hours, EMA and precision settings, per-stage resolution schedules, and the training data itself (Hunyuan's 1M AAA clips and Cosmos's 20M hours are both unreleased; only Sekai and VPT are public). Genie 3 discloses essentially nothing beyond 720p/24fps and a one-minute memory. The single most useful source for our scale is Nano World Models, which is the only one that ablates the choices we actually have to make (prediction target, action injection mechanism, latent space, model size) under a fixed 8-GPU recipe.

## Hunyuan-GameCraft (arXiv 2506.17201)

- **Task and domain**: "High-dynamic Interactive Game Video Generation" — action-controlled game video continuation from a single image, across "over 100 AAA games". Interactive, autoregressive long-horizon rollout.
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: MM-DiT (Multimodal Diffusion Transformer), built on the **HunyuanVideo** text-to-video foundation model. **Parameter count not stated** anywhere in the paper — neither for the base model nor for Hunyuan-GameCraft (the widely-cited 13B for HunyuanVideo is *not* written in this paper; do not cite it from here).
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: a "causal VAE" (HunyuanVideo's). **Compression ratios, channel count, and whether it is a 3D causal VAE are not stated in the paper text.** Output video is 720p, generated in **33-frame clips**.
- **Objective**: "flow matching to progressively denoise". The prediction target and the full flow-matching loss equation are **not stated**; the only loss written out (Eq. 2) is the classifier-free-guidance distillation loss.
- **Context and temporal modelling**: frames as tokens (MM-DiT), autoregressive chunk-by-chunk extension. **Hybrid history condition**: a binary mask "assigns value 1 to head latent regions and 0 to chunk segments", i.e. history latents are concatenated with the noisy chunk and disambiguated by a mask channel. Three history variants are mixed during training with sampling ratios **"0.7 for single historical clip, 0.05 for multiple historical clips, and 0.25 for single frame"**. KV cache: **not stated**. Noise added to history: **not stated**.
- **Action conditioning**: keyboard and mouse inputs are unified into a **shared continuous camera representation** `a = (d_trans, d_rot, alpha, beta)` — translation and rotation directions on the 2-sphere with velocity bounds. Camera trajectories are turned into **Plucker embeddings**, encoded by a lightweight module of "convolutional layers for spatial downsampling and pooling layers for temporal downsampling", with a "learnable scaling coefficient", and injected by a **"token addition strategy"** (added to video tokens, not cross-attended). **Parameter count of this encoder is not stated.**
- **Data**: "over one million gameplay recordings across over 100 AAA games", source **1080p**, segmented into **6-second coherent segments**; curation is scene partition, filtering, 6-DoF camera annotation via **Monst3R**, and VLM captioning. Plus "about 3,000 high-quality motion sequences" of synthetic rendered data for fine-tuning. Total duration in hours: **not stated**. fps of the data: **not stated** (the model runs at "25 fps" nominal video rate). **Dataset not released.**
- **Training recipe**: two phases — **30k iterations at lr 3e-5**, then **20,000 iterations at lr 1e-5**. **Batch size 48**, on **192 NVIDIA H20 GPUs**. Resolution 720p. Wall time / GPU-hours, EMA, and precision: **not stated**.
- **Sampler and inference**: base model inference speed **0.25 FPS**. Accelerated with **Phased Consistency Model (PCM)** distillation, an **8-step** consistency model, giving **"10–20x acceleration"** and **6.6 frames per second**. The inference GPU type is **not stated**.
- **Evaluation**: FVD **1554.2**; Image Quality **0.69**; Dynamic Average **67.2**; Temporal Consistency **0.95**; relative pose error **RPE trans 0.08**, **RPE rot 0.20**; inference speed 0.25 FPS (6.6 with PCM). Controllability is measured as relative pose error between the commanded camera trajectory and poses re-estimated from the generated video. User study: "Ours" scored **4.42–4.61** across five dimensions versus competitors' **1.63–4.44**.
- **Reproducibility notes**: the paper itself only points at a project website, but that site links released **code** (github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan-GameCraft-1.0) and **weights** (huggingface.co/tencent/Hunyuan-GameCraft-1.0). **Training data not released.**
- **What of this is reproducible at our scale**: The hybrid history-condition recipe is the transferable part and costs nothing — sample the conditioning context stochastically (mostly one previous clip, sometimes many, sometimes a single frame) so one model handles both cold start and long rollout. Their action pathway is also cheap enough to copy directly: encode the action as a small dense embedding and *add* it to video tokens rather than building a cross-attention branch. Their 192xH20 / 50k-iteration budget is not reproducible, and neither is the 1M-clip AAA corpus; the 0.25 FPS base speed tells us step-reduction distillation is a separate project, not a free add-on. Their RPE-based controllability metric is directly borrowable for Doom if we can estimate camera pose from generated frames.

## Yume 1.0 (arXiv 2507.17744)

- **Task and domain**: interactive world generation / exploration of real-world (mostly urban walking) scenes from an input image, driven by keyboard camera commands. "a preview version" of the method.
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: initialised from **"SkyReels-V2-14B-540P as the pre-trained model"** (14B). Architecture is a **Masked Video Diffusion Transformer (MVDT)** with an asymmetric **encoder–interpolator–decoder** design, trained with **masking ratio rho = 0.3**. Memory module is a modified **FramePack**. Parameter count of the added MVDT/memory machinery: **not stated**.
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: **Wan-VAE**, spatio-temporal, latents of shape **[1 + T/4, H/8, W/8]** with **C = 16** channels; **4x temporal, 8x spatial** compression.
- **Objective**: **Rectified Flow** with **v-prediction**; loss is **MSE(z1 − z0, v_theta(z_t, c, t))** in latent space.
- **Context and temporal modelling**: frames as tokens; history is **frame-wise concatenated** with the segment being generated, after **hierarchical FramePack compression** — recent frames at **(1,2,2)**, mid-range at **(1,4,4)**, older at **(1,8,8)**. Autoregressive extension with stochastic horizon sampling: **"0.3 probability ... 33–400 frame videos; 0.7 probability ... 400–800 frame videos"**. Causal attention and KV cache: **not stated** (conditioning uses standard cross-attention). Noise on history: **not stated**.
- **Action conditioning**: camera motion is **quantized** — relative SE(3) transforms are matched to **8 discrete actions** (forward, backward, left, right, turn left, turn right, tilt up, tilt down), plus speed described by three parsed quantities (translational displacement, directional change angle, rotational dynamics). Actions are injected **as text**, through the existing text pathway: **"text injection without additional learnable parameters"** — zero added module size.
- **Data**: **Sekai-Real-HQ**. Collected "10471 hours of walking videos ... 628 hours of drone videos"; downloaded "8409 hours of walking videos and 214 hours of drone videos"; after preprocessing "6620 hours of video clips as Sekai-Real"; final curated subset **"400 hours of video clips as Sekai-Real-HQ"**, yielding **"139019 video clips"** after quantization (minimum 33 frames each). Source at least 1080P and 30–60 FPS; training at **544x960** and **16 FPS**. Dataset pipeline **released** (sekai-codebase).
- **Training recipe**: a single end-to-end stage (with 30% static-image conditioning), **7,000 iterations**, **batch size 40**, **lr 1e-5**, Adam, on **NVIDIA A800 GPUs** (**GPU count not stated**). Wall time / GPU-hours, EMA, and precision: **not stated** (bf16 is used for cached residuals at inference).
- **Sampler and inference**: baseline **50 inference steps**. Training-free **Anti-Artifact Mechanism (AAM)**: two passes of 30 steps (first without CFG, then with CFG=90) for **90 NFE**, "achieving 10% NFE reduction". **TTS-SDE** (Time Travel Sampling via SDE) with interval s=5, depth l=5. **Adversarial distillation** cuts steps from **"50 to 14"**, and caching (bf16 residual caching on the 10 lowest-scoring blocks) brings generation from **583.1s to 158.8s**. **Real-time fps and inference hardware are not reported.**
- **Evaluation**: **Yume-Bench**, "70 videos or images". Metrics: instruction following (human-judged), subject consistency, background consistency, motion smoothness, aesthetic quality, imaging quality. Headline controllability: instruction following **0.657** vs Wan-2.1 **0.057** and MatrixGame **0.271**. Long-video decay over the rollout: subject consistency **0.934 -> 0.930**, background consistency **0.947 -> 0.941**. Motion correctness was assessed **manually**, explicitly because camera pose estimation was too inaccurate on their videos.
- **Reproducibility notes**: code at github.com/stdstu12/YUME, weights at huggingface.co/stdstu123/Yume-I2V-540P, data pipeline at github.com/Lixsp11/sekai-codebase. CC BY 4.0.
- **What of this is reproducible at our scale**: The most striking number is **7,000 iterations at batch 40** — the whole interactive behaviour is a short fine-tune on top of a strong video prior, which is the shape our DiT-XL/2 vs SD-1.4-UNet comparison should take if we ever initialise from a video checkpoint. Their action conditioning is free to copy: quantize Doom's action set to a small discrete vocabulary and route it through the existing conditioning embedding rather than adding parameters, which keeps the two backbones strictly comparable. The FramePack-style hierarchical history compression is directly relevant to our 32-frame channel-stacked context — it is the alternative that keeps a long horizon at a fraction of the token cost, and it is implementable in a day. Their admission that camera pose estimation was too noisy to score controllability is a warning: for Doom we should build an inverse dynamics model (MineWorld's route) rather than a pose-based metric.

## Yume 1.5 (arXiv 2512.22096)

*Confirmed: the id resolves to "Yume1.5: A Text-Controlled Interactive World Generation Model", Xiaofeng Mao, Zhen Li, Chuanhao Li, Xiaojie Xu, Kaining Ying, Tong He, Jiangmiao Pang, Yu Qiao, Kaipeng Zhang (Shanghai AI Laboratory, Fudan University).*

- **Task and domain**: text-controlled interactive world generation, both T2V and I2V, with keyboard camera control plus text-described events and human movement.
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: initialised from **Wan2.2-5B** (5B). DiT with a new **Joint Temporal-Spatial-Channel Modeling (TSCM)** module. Added-module parameter count: **not stated**.
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: latent diffusion inheriting the **Wan2.2 VAE**; the paper does not restate the compression ratios or channel count — **not stated** in this paper.
- **Objective**: **Rectified Flow loss**. Second stage is **Self-Forcing** with **distribution matching distillation**, i.e. the model is trained on its own rollouts to close the train/test gap, then distilled to few steps.
- **Context and temporal modelling**: **TSCM** — historical frames are compressed at **(1,2,2), (1,4,4), (1,8,8)** by temporal distance (as in Yume 1.0), plus a **channel compression to 96 dimensions**, and **linear attention replaces standard attention for the historical tokens**. This is the one place in this batch where history is compressed on the channel axis as well as the token axis. Explicit KV cache and per-frame noise on history: **not stated**.
- **Action conditioning**: a discrete keyboard vocabulary of **8 camera actions + 9 human movement actions**. The caption is decomposed into an **Event Description** and an **Action Description**, each encoded separately by **T5**; action-description embeddings are **precomputed and cached**, so interactive control costs no text-encoder forward pass. No new learnable action parameters stated.
- **Data**: real — the **Sekai-Real-HQ** walking subset. Synthetic — **"80,000 diverse captions"** from OpenVid, synthesised with **Wan 2.1 14B**, filtered by VBench to the **"top 50,000 videos"**. Events — **"10,000 first-person perspective images"** synthesised to 10,000 videos, manually screened down to **"4,000 videos"**. Training at **704x1280**, evaluation at **544x960**, **16 FPS**.
- **Training recipe**: foundation stage **10,000 iterations**, **batch size 40**, **lr 1e-5**, Adam, on **NVIDIA A100 GPUs** (**count not stated**); then **Self-Forcing with TSCM for 600 iterations** at identical hyperparameters. Alternating T2V/I2V dataset sampling. Wall time / GPU-hours, EMA, precision: **not stated**.
- **Sampler and inference**: **4 inference steps** after distillation; **"average generation speed of 12 fps at 540p resolution using only a single A100 GPU"**; total inference time **8 seconds** versus baselines' **611–971 seconds** (baselines used **50 sampling steps**).
- **Evaluation**: **Yume-Bench** with six metrics (IF, SC, BC, MS, AQ, IQ). Headline: **IF 0.836**, vs Wan-2.1 **0.057**, MatrixGame **0.271**, Yume 1.0 **0.657**. Instruction following is human-judged. Full table of the other five metrics: not captured beyond the above.
- **Reproducibility notes**: "codebase in the supplementary material"; "model weights and full codebase **will be made public**" (future tense at time of writing). Repo github.com/stdstu12/YUME, project page stdstu12.github.io/YUME-Project, data pipeline via sekai-codebase.
- **What of this is reproducible at our scale**: This is the clearest demonstration that **real-time interactivity is a post-hoc distillation problem, not an architecture problem** — 600 Self-Forcing iterations on top of a 10,000-iteration fine-tune buys 4-step sampling at 12 fps on one A100. That is the single most relevant fact for a Doom model on one A6000: train the flow model normally, then spend a short Self-Forcing/DMD stage. Caching the action-conditioning embedding so interactive control never re-runs the encoder is a free trick we should copy. The TSCM channel compression to 96 dims is a concrete middle ground between our 32-frame channel stack and full frames-as-tokens, and is worth an ablation row.

## Cosmos World Foundation Model Platform (arXiv 2501.03575) — diffusion WFMs and Video2World

*Caveat: the HTML rendering truncated before Sections 5.3 (3D consistency, physics alignment) and 6.2/6.3 (robotic manipulation and driving post-training) across every fetch route I tried, including ar5iv. Those items are marked "not retrieved", which is not the same as "not stated".*

- **Task and domain**: general-purpose world foundation models for Physical AI. **Video2World** = future-video prediction conditioned on past observation(s) plus a text prompt; Text2World is the predecessor stage.
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: diffusion DiT with per-block self-attention, cross-attention, and feed-forward. **7B-Video2World: 28 layers, model dimension 4,096, 32 attention heads**. **14B-Video2World: 36 layers, model dimension 5,120, 40 attention heads**. Video2World models are **fine-tuned from the corresponding Text2World checkpoints**, not trained from scratch. **AdaLN-LoRA** gives a "36% reduction in parameter count (from 11B to 7B parameters)".
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: **Cosmos-1.0-Tokenizer-CV8x8x8**, a continuous video tokenizer, **spatial compression 8x8, temporal compression 8x** (corrected Sep 14 night: the CV8x8x8 name is temporal x height x width, so 8x temporal; the reader's first draft said 4x), latent channels **C = 16**, with a **temporally causal** design "ensuring that each stage processes only current and past frames".
- **Objective**: **EDM** denoising score matching, `L(D_theta, sigma) = E[|| D_theta(x0 + n; sigma) − x0 ||_2^2]`, with **ln(sigma) ~ N(P_mean, P_std^2)** and learned uncertainty weighting u(sigma). This is the only x0-prediction / EDM system in this batch.
- **Context and temporal modelling**: frames as tokens with **3D-factorised RoPE** over temporal, height and width axes, **FPS-aware** rescaling. Conditional frames are **"concatenated with generated frames along temporal dimension"** plus a **binary mask that distinguishes conditional from generated frames**. **"The model can flexibly operate with either a single conditional frame (image) or multiple previous frames as input"** — an exact maximum count is not stated in what I could retrieve. **Noise is added to the conditioning frames**: "augmented noise to the conditional frames" with **P_mean = −3.0, P_std = 2.0** — this is the explicit history-noising recipe, and it exists specifically to make autoregressive rollout robust. Context length at the high-res stage: **56,320 tokens** = 1280x704 / (8·2)^2 x 15 latent frames (121 video frames, patch pt=1, ph=pw=2).
- **Action conditioning**: **not retrieved.** Section 6.2 (robotic manipulation post-training) states only "We fine-tune our WFMs on various robotic tasks, which consist of video-action sequences"; the mechanism, dataset size, and results were not retrievable. The pre-trained diffusion WFMs themselves are text- and frame-conditioned, **not** action-conditioned.
- **Data**: **"20M hours of raw videos"**, resolutions "from 720p to 4k", processed into **"~100M video clips ranging from 2 to 60 seconds"**. Category mix: nature 20%, manipulation 16%, spatial 16%, driving 11%, human motion 10%, egocentric 8%, camera 8%, synthetic 4%, other 7%. VLM-generated captions averaging **"559 characters or 97 words"**. **Data not released.**
- **Training recipe**: staged — Stage 1 low-res **512p, 57 frames, 10,240 context tokens**; Stage 2 high-res **720p (1280x704), 121 frames, 56,320 context tokens**; Stage 3 fine-tune **"~10k iterations"** on a high-quality subset. Learning rate **2^-15** (7B-Text2World) and **2^-16** (14B-Text2World), **linear scheduler with 2,500 iterations** warmup. Precision: **BF16 forward/backward with FP32 weight updates**, loss "scaled by factor of 10". Hardware: **"cluster of 10,000 NVIDIA H100 GPUs ... three months"**; GPU-hours not broken out. Batch size **not disclosed**; video batches scaled by **sqrt(frame_count)** with domain-specific normalization for joint image/video training. EMA: not stated in what I retrieved. Video2World-specific iterations/lr/batch: **not retrieved**.
- **Sampler and inference**: classifier-free guidance, with "higher guidance values required for video-generation tasks". **Sampling step count not retrieved**; **no distillation mentioned**; **wall-clock generation time not retrieved**. A separate **diffusion decoder** exists as a quality enhancement, not an accelerator.
- **Evaluation**: metric categories named are **3D consistency** (Sampson error, camera pose estimation success rate) and **physics alignment** (PSNR, SSIM, IoU), Sections 5.3.1/5.3.2 — **the numeric tables were not retrieved**. Human evaluation numbers: **not retrieved**.
- **Reproducibility notes**: models and tokenizers **open-weight** under the **NVIDIA Open Model License**, code **CC BY 4.0**, at github.com/NVIDIA/Cosmos; "pre-training script and post-training script will be available at NVIDIA Nemo Framework". **Training data not released.**
- **What of this is reproducible at our scale**: Three things transfer directly and cheaply. First, **noise-augmented conditioning frames** (sigma drawn with P_mean=−3.0, P_std=2.0) is a two-line change that specifically targets the drift our 32-frame context will suffer during rollout — we should ablate it on both backbones. Second, the **binary conditional/generated mask concatenated with the latents** is a cleaner way to express a variable-length context than a fixed channel stack, and it lets one model serve both single-frame init and full-context rollout. Third, their **low-res-then-high-res staging** is the right way to spend a single A6000: most steps at low resolution, a short high-res finish. The 10,000-H100-for-three-months pre-training and the 20M-hour corpus are irrelevant to us, and the CV8x8x8 causal tokenizer is an open-weight alternative to SD 1.4 KL-f8 if we ever want temporal compression — but swapping it would break the like-for-like VAE control in our DiT-vs-UNet comparison.

## Genie 3 (https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/)

- **Task and domain**: general-purpose interactive world model — "generate dynamic worlds that you can navigate in real time".
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: **not disclosed.** The announcement says nothing about architecture family, layer counts, or parameters.
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: **not disclosed.**
- **Objective**: **not disclosed.** No mention of diffusion, autoregression, flow matching, or any loss.
- **Context and temporal modelling**: the only quantitative statement is memory — **"visual memory extending as far back as one minute ago"**, and "Limited interaction duration. The model can currently support a few minutes of continuous interaction" / "retaining consistency for a few minutes". No context length, attention scheme, or caching is disclosed.
- **Action conditioning**: navigation actions plus **"promptable world events"**, i.e. text that can "change the generated world, like altering weather conditions or introducing new objects and characters". A stated limitation: "Limited action space. Although promptable world events allow for a wide range of environmental interventions, they are not necessarily performed by the agent itself". No mechanism, no module sizes.
- **Data**: **not disclosed** — no hours, sources, curation, or licensing.
- **Training recipe**: **not disclosed** — no steps, batch, hardware, or compute.
- **Sampler and inference**: **"real time at 24 frames per second"** at **720p**. Inference hardware, step count, and any distillation: **not disclosed**.
- **Evaluation**: **not disclosed.** No benchmark, metric, or human study numbers are given.
- **Reproducibility notes**: "limited research preview". **No code, no weights, no data, no paper.**
- **What of this is reproducible at our scale**: Nothing technical — this is a capability announcement, not a method. Its only legitimate use in our paper is as a citation for the target operating point (720p, 24 fps, minute-scale consistency) and to motivate why open, small-scale replications matter. We should not infer its architecture, and we should say plainly in the related-work section that no training details are public.

## MineWorld (arXiv 2504.08388)

- **Task and domain**: "a real-time interactive world model on Minecraft, an open-ended sandbox game", modelling p(x_{i+1} | x_{<i}, a_i) over visual game states and user actions.
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: **autoregressive Transformer decoder** following the **LLaMA** design ("root mean square layer normalization and Rotary Embeddings"). Three sizes: **300M, 700M and 1.2B parameters**. The **visual tokenizer** is initialised from a public pre-trained checkpoint and fine-tuned on Minecraft; the **Transformer itself is trained from scratch**. This is the non-diffusion contrast point in the batch.
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: **VQ-VAE**, **8k codebook**, **16x compression on both height and width**, giving **14x24 patches = 336 discrete tokens per frame**. Plus **11 tokens per action**.
- **Objective**: plain **next-token prediction** cross-entropy over the interleaved sequence, "treating tokens of game states and actions equivalently".
- **Context and temporal modelling**: a single causal token stream interleaving frame tokens and action tokens: `(t_{i*c+1},...,t_{(i+1)*c}, [aBOS], t_1^{a_i},...,t_9^{a_i}, [aEOS])`. Context is **16 frames = 5.5k tokens** ("maximum context length ... 16 pairs"). **Causal attention**, with a **"parallel attention mask"** variant fine-tuned in for fast inference. KV cache is implicit to AR decoding. No noise on history (not a diffusion model).
- **Action conditioning**: **7 exclusive discrete action classes** plus camera angles quantized into **11 bins**; each action is serialised into **11 tokens** including BOS/EOS markers. Actions are tokens in the same vocabulary — **no separate conditioning module at all**.
- **Data**: the **VPT** (Video PreTraining) dataset. **10M video clips (160M frames)** for training, **0.5k validation clips, 1k test clips**; **55B tokens in the training set**. Frames resized **360x640 -> 224x384**. Curation: filtered out frames with no recorded action and with the GUI open; split into 16-frame clips. VPT is public; MineWorld's code and weights are released.
- **Training recipe**: **200k steps**, lr **3e-4** with **cosine decay** and **10k warm-up steps**, **AdamW betas (0.9, 0.95), weight decay 0.1**, on **32 NVIDIA 40G A100 GPUs**. Batch size, wall time, EMA and precision: **not stated**.
- **Sampler and inference**: **diagonal decoding** — "tokens in adjacent rows and columns generated simultaneously" — giving a theoretical speedup ratio **r = (h x w)/(h + w − 1)** and a measured **3x speedup** over vanilla AR. Throughput: **300M: 5.91 FPS; 700M: 3.18 FPS; 1.2B: 3.01 FPS** (inference hardware for these numbers **not stated**). The model is fine-tuned with the parallel attention mask after pre-training so decoding and training match.
- **Evaluation**: video quality via **FVD, PSNR, LPIPS, SSIM**. **Controllability via an Inverse Dynamics Model** that predicts the action from generated consecutive frames, scored as **precision / recall / F1 over the 7 discrete action classes** plus an **L1 loss on camera angle bins**. That automatic metric was validated against humans: **Pearson r = 0.56 (p = 0.01)** with a human study of **5 players rating 20 clips on a 1–5 scale**. Headline (700M vs Oasis 500M): **F1 0.71 vs 0.41**, **FVD 231 vs 377**, **FPS 3.18 vs 2.58**.
- **Reproducibility notes**: **code and weights released** at aka.ms/mineworld; VPT data is public; full hyperparameters in Appendix B.
- **What of this is reproducible at our scale**: This is the closest match to our compute envelope — **32 A100s, 200k steps, 700M parameters**, and it is the one paper whose whole pipeline we could actually re-run. The **IDM-based controllability metric is the single most valuable import for our Doom paper**: train a small inverse dynamics model on real Doom frame pairs, run it on generated rollouts, and report per-action precision/recall/F1, with their human-correlation check (r = 0.56) as precedent for calling it valid. Their 16-frame / 5.5k-token context is close to our 32-frame stack, which makes their context length a fair reference point in the related-work table. Their 3.01–5.91 FPS also sets the honest expectation: "real time" is not free even at 700M, and our DiT-XL/2 will not be interactive without a distillation stage.

## Nano World Models (arXiv 2605.23993)

*The id resolves. Title: "Nano World Models: A Minimalist Implementation of Future Video Prediction", Siqiao Huang, Partha Kaushik, Michael Chen, Hengkai Pan, Omar Chehab, Fernando Moreno-Pino, Max Simchowitz (an additional author, Kaiwen Geng, appears on the abstract listing).*

- **Task and domain**: a minimal, controlled codebase for action-conditioned future video prediction. Environments: **D4RL and DeepMind Control Suite**, maze navigation (**Maze, Wall**), tabletop pushing (**PushT**), deformable manipulation with an XArm (**Rope, Granular**), game simulation on the **CS:GO** dataset, and robot manipulation on **RT-1**.
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: transformer over latent video tokens with **interleaved spatial-temporal attention**. Four scales: **NanoWM-S (40M), NanoWM-B (160M), NanoWM-L (600M), NanoWM-XL (830M)**; the precise per-variant counts given are **NanoWM-S/2: 39.8M, NanoWM-B/2: 158.6M, NanoWM-L/2: ~460M**. Initialisation from a pretrained checkpoint is **not stated** (trained on the listed datasets).
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: **Stable Diffusion VAE (`stabilityai/sd-vae-ft-mse`)** giving **SD-VAE latents of shape [4, 32, 32]**, compared against semantic encoders **Web-DINO** and **V-JEPA 2.1** with **dense latents of shape [1024, 16, 16]**. This is the same KL-f8 family we use.
- **Objective**: **Diffusion Forcing** as the unifying interface — independent per-frame noise levels indexed by a noise set K. Prediction targets compared head-to-head: **x-prediction, eps-prediction, v-prediction**, plus **flow-matching** objectives. Noise schedule: **squared-cosine with zero-terminal SNR (ZTSNR)**. Explicit loss equation: not reproduced here.
- **Context and temporal modelling**: **frames as tokens** (no channel stacking), with **causal masking** used in the PushT experiments. Context lengths: conditions on **the first frame and generates the remaining three** in the standard eval; **four frames out of a 16-frame window** for CS:GO. Because it is diffusion forcing, **history carries its own noise level** rather than being clean — the context/future split is a choice of noise indices, not of architecture. KV cache: not stated.
- **Action conditioning**: five mechanisms compared directly — **element-wise addition (additive), adaLN, adaLN fused with timestep injection (adaLN-fuse), FiLM, and cross-attention**. Parameter overhead measured on PushT: **additive 0, FiLM ~14.4M, cross-attention ~28.3M, adaLN ~42.5M**. This is the only source in the batch that prices action conditioning.
- **Data**: D4RL, DeepMind Control Suite, CS:GO, RT-1. **256-resolution** models are standard; RT-1 at native resolution. fps: **not stated**. Pretrained checkpoints for supported environments are released.
- **Training recipe**: **8 GPUs with per-GPU batch size 8, effective batch size 64** (GPU type **not stated**). Steps vary by experiment: **30K (PushT)**, **50K (RT-1 ablations)**, **100K (latent space study)**, **300K (RT-1 final)**. **lr 1e-4**, **AdamW weight decay 0.01**. EMA, precision, wall time and GPU-hours: **not stated**.
- **Sampler and inference**: **250 DDIM sampling steps** unless otherwise stated. Rollout is a **sliding window**: generated frames become the context for the next window; long-horizon evaluation generates **50-frame videos** from a **four-frame context window**. fps: **not stated**; no distillation.
- **Evaluation**: **PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID** for fidelity, **FVD** for longer videos, **Success Rate** for planning. Fixed-seed validation (seed 42) on **256 fixed validation clips**. Headline prediction-target ablation on RT-1 at 50K steps: **v-prediction PSNR 23.07 / SSIM 0.760 / LPIPS 0.207 / FID 42.27**; **x-prediction PSNR 23.37 / SSIM 0.783 / LPIPS 0.184 / FID 42.99**; **eps-prediction PSNR 21.89 / SSIM 0.739 / LPIPS 0.225 / FID 48.86**. Findings: "eps-prediction underperforms x and v-prediction"; "Larger Models give Better Performance" monotonically S->B->L; "Action injection is task-dependent" (FiLM best on RT-1, additive best on PushT); semantic latents "do not automatically yield better world models for planning" — Web-DINO and V-JEPA "fail completely" on PushT goal-reaching (**0% success vs 25% for the VAE**); long-horizon autoregression "inevitably accumulates perceptual errors".
- **Reproducibility notes**: code at github.com/simchowitzlabpublic/nano-world-model, weights at huggingface.co/collections/knightnemo/nano-world-model, blog at simchowitzlabpublic.github.io/nano-world-model, Hydra configs released.
- **What of this is reproducible at our scale**: All of it — this is our scale, and it is the strongest external support for our design choices. Their eps < x ≈ v result on RT-1 is direct evidence for our **velocity loss**, and we can cite it rather than re-deriving it. Their **action-injection ablation with parameter costs** (additive 0 params, FiLM 14.4M, cross-attn 28.3M, adaLN 42.5M) is the template for how we should report Doom action conditioning, and their "task-dependent" conclusion means we must ablate on Doom rather than assume adaLN. Their **diffusion-forcing context handling with per-frame noise levels** is the principled alternative to our 32-frame channel stack, and given that they also use an SD KL-f8 VAE at 256 resolution, an apples-to-apples comparison against our setup is realistic on a single A6000. Their sliding-window 50-frame rollout protocol with fixed seeds and 256 validation clips is a ready-made evaluation harness we should mirror.
