# DiT-based interactive world models — evidence cards (batch A)

*Sources read in full: Oasis blog + `etched-ai/open-oasis` source; arXiv 2508.13009v1; arXiv 2506.18701v1; arXiv 2411.00769v3; arXiv 2412.03568v1. Numbers are quoted from the sources; "not stated" means the source is silent. Compiled 2026-09-14.*

## Summary: what these five share, and what they hide

Four of the five do not train a world model from scratch — they fine-tune a public text-to-video or image-to-video diffusion transformer and then bolt action conditioning onto it. Matrix-Game 1.0 initialises from HunyuanVideo I2V (17B), Matrix-Game 2.0 from SkyReels-V2-I2V-1.3B (Wan 2.1 architecture, grown to 1.8B by the action modules), The Matrix from a public 2.3B video DiT (Open-Sora, reference [47]) plus a 0.4B Interactive Module, and GameGen-X trains a 28-layer MSDiT (width 1152, 16 heads) whose initialisation is never stated but whose 3D-VAE is explicitly the Stable Diffusion 2D VAE extended with temporal layers. Only Oasis is architecturally independent, and it is also the only one small enough to be a real reference point: DiT-S/2 at 1024 width and depth 16, ~500M parameters, on a ViT-L/20 *image* VAE with no temporal compression. Data scale is the other commonality and the main gap to a small lab: 800–1200 hours (Matrix-Game 2.0), 2700 hours unlabelled plus ~1200 hours labelled (Matrix-Game 1.0), 4000 hours / 1M clips (GameGen-X), 750k labelled plus 1.2M unlabelled 6-second clips (The Matrix); Oasis discloses no data at all. Temporal modelling splits cleanly into two camps: frames as a real time axis with causal or block-causal attention plus a KV cache and per-frame noise levels (Oasis diffusion forcing, Matrix-Game 2.0 self-forcing with a 6-latent rolling cache, The Matrix's Swin-DPM token queue), versus fixed-length bidirectional chunks with a short motion-context window concatenated on channels (Matrix-Game 1.0, k=5 motion frames; GameGen-X, masked context frames inside a 102-frame clip). Actions almost always enter the same way — continuous camera/mouse concatenated to the latent then MLP + temporal self-attention, discrete keys via cross-attention — a design that traces back to GameFactory and is copied by both Matrix-Games; The Matrix instead verbalises keys into English and encodes them with T5. What is systematically *not* disclosed: training GPU count and type (only GameGen-X states it: 24× H800 80GB, 25 days base + 7 days InstructNet; The Matrix says only "8 GPUs" with no model; both Matrix-Games say nothing), wall-clock or GPU-hours, EMA (mentioned by none), optimizer beyond GameGen-X's Adam, per-stage step counts for Matrix-Game 1.0, any parameter count for GameGen-X, and any training detail whatsoever for Oasis. Three of five also give no reproducible evaluation against a shared baseline; the GameWorld Score from Matrix-Game 1.0 is the only controllability metric here with a stated, checkable definition (IDM-inferred actions vs. ground truth).

## Oasis (https://oasis-model.github.io/ and https://github.com/etched-ai/open-oasis)

- **Task and domain**: Real-time, keyboard-conditioned open-world gameplay generation in a Minecraft-like domain. Blog: "the first experiential, realtime, open-world AI model"; README: "Oasis takes in user keyboard input and generates gameplay in an autoregressive manner." The 25 action keys in `utils.py` (`inventory`, `ESC`, `hotbar.1`–`hotbar.9`, `forward`, `back`, `left`, `right`, `cameraX`, `cameraY`, `jump`, `sneak`, `sprint`, `swapHands`, `attack`, `use`, `pickItem`, `drop`) are the MineRL/VPT action space, confirming Minecraft.
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: "two parts: a spatial autoencoder, and a latent diffusion backbone. Both are Transformer-based… the autoencoder is based on ViT, and the backbone is based on DiT." Released checkpoint is "Oasis 500M, a downscaled version of the model". The released config is `DiT_models["DiT-S/2"]` = `DiT(patch_size=2, hidden_size=1024, depth=16, num_heads=16)`, with defaults `in_channels=16`, `input_h=18`, `input_w=32`, `mlp_ratio=4.0`, `external_cond_dim=25`, `max_frames=32`. Initialisation: no foundation checkpoint is named anywhere — weights are randomly initialised in `initialize_weights()` (Xavier, with adaLN and final layers zeroed), so the released model is effectively from scratch. The larger model behind the live demo has no stated size.
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: A **spatial-only ViT VAE**, not a video VAE. `VAE_models["vit-l-20-shallow-encoder"]` = `AutoencoderKL(latent_dim=16, patch_size=20, enc_dim=1024, enc_depth=6, enc_heads=16, dec_dim=1024, dec_depth=12, dec_heads=16, input_height=360, input_width=640)`. So 360×640 RGB → an 18×32 grid of 16-channel latents (20× spatial downsample, **no temporal compression**; each frame is encoded independently). Latent scaling factor `0.07843137255` (= 1/12.75). DiT patch size 2 over the 18×32 grid → 9×16 = 144 tokens per frame.
- **Objective**: "Trained using Diffusion Forcing, which denoises with independent per-token noise levels." The released sampler is **v-prediction**: `x_start = alphas_cumprod[t].sqrt() * x_curr - (1 - alphas_cumprod[t]).sqrt() * v`, over a `sigmoid_beta_schedule` with `max_noise_level = 1000`. The exact training loss weighting, and whether EMA was used, are not stated.
- **Context and temporal modelling**: Frames are a genuine time axis. Each `SpatioTemporalDiTBlock` runs `SpatialAxialAttention` (with pixel-frequency RoPE) then `TemporalAxialAttention` with `is_causal=True` (temporal RoPE), each with its own adaLN modulation and MLP. Context length `max_frames=32`, enforced at inference by a sliding window `start_frame = max(0, i + 1 - model.max_frames)`. **Noise on history is the core trick**: context frames are held at `stabilization_level = 15` (of 1000) rather than 0 while the newest frame is denoised — the blog's "dynamic noising, which adjusts inference-time noise on a schedule, injecting noise in the first diffusion forward passes to reduce error accumulation, and gradually removing noise in the later passes." No KV cache in the released code; the window is recomputed each step. Noise is clamped to `noise_abs_max = 20`.
- **Action conditioning**: A single `nn.Linear(external_cond_dim=25, hidden_size=1024)` — roughly 26k added parameters — whose output is **added to the timestep embedding** and then drives the per-block adaLN modulation (`c = t_emb + action_emb`). So actions enter through adaLN, per-frame, not cross-attention. Camera is continuous, bucketed and rescaled to [−1, 1] (`max_val = 20`, `bin_size = 0.5`); the other 23 keys are binary.
- **Data**: **Not stated.** Neither the blog nor the repo gives hours, clips, frames, source, or curation. Resolution is 360×640 and output fps 20 (`--fps` default). Data not released (only two sample frames and one action tensor in `sample_data/`).
- **Training recipe**: **Not stated.** No stages, steps, lr, batch size, hardware, wall time, EMA, or precision anywhere in the blog or repo. Inference runs under `autocast("cuda", dtype=torch.half)`.
- **Sampler and inference**: DDIM with `--ddim-steps` default **10** over a 1000-level schedule; `--num-frames` default 32; `--n-prompt-frames` default 1 (single image prompt). Blog: "generates real-time output in 20 frames per second… must generate a new frame every 0.04 seconds", "over 100x faster" than Sora/Mochi-1/Runway, "Optimized for Sohu, the Transformer ASIC built by Etched." The GPU behind the 20 fps demo figure is not stated, and no distillation is described.
- **Evaluation**: **No quantitative evaluation is published** — no FVD, FID, or controllability numbers in the blog or repo. Stated limitations only: "fuzzy video in the distance, temporal consistency of uncertain objects, domain generalization, precise control over inventories, and difficulties over long contexts." (Third-party numbers exist: Matrix-Game 1.0 scores Oasis at Image Quality 0.65, Keyboard Acc. 0.77, Mouse Acc. 0.56; Matrix-Game 2.0 scores it at Image Quality 0.27, Keyboard Acc. 0.73, Mouse Acc. 0.56 — the two disagree sharply, so treat both as baseline-reimplementation numbers, not Oasis's own.)
- **Reproducibility notes**: MIT license. Released: inference code (`generate.py`, `dit.py`, `vae.py`, `utils.py`, `attention.py`), `Etched/oasis-500m` on HF with `oasis500m.safetensors` (DiT) and `vit-l-20.safetensors` (ViT VAE). **Not** released: training code, hyperparameters, dataset, and the larger demo checkpoint.
- **What of this is reproducible at our scale**: Almost all of it, and this is the closest analogue to our setup — a ~500M DiT at 1024 width / depth 16 on 144 tokens per frame, trained from scratch on a single-game corpus, is an A6000-sized run. Two design choices transfer directly and are cheap to test against our current recipe: replacing our channel-stacked 32-frame context with a causal temporal-attention axis over the same 32 frames (Oasis alternates spatial and causal-temporal axial attention inside each block, which keeps the per-frame token count at 144 instead of inflating the channel dimension), and adopting per-frame independent noise levels with context frames pinned at a low non-zero level (`stabilization_level = 15/1000`) at sampling time — that is a pure inference-side change to our v-prediction loop and is the single cheapest experiment in this batch. Our SD 1.4 KL-f8 VAE is a reasonable stand-in for their ViT-L/20 since both are per-frame image autoencoders with no temporal compression, though theirs gives 16 latent channels at 20× downsample versus our 4 at 8×. What we cannot copy is the hardware story: their 20 fps claim rests on an ASIC, so we should report steps-per-frame and latency on the A6000, not chase real time.

## Matrix-Game 2.0 (arXiv 2508.13009)

- **Task and domain**: Real-time streaming interactive world model from a single reference image plus frame-level mouse and keyboard input. Domains: Minecraft, Unreal Engine scenes, GTA5 driving, Temple Run, and real-world "wild" scenes (Sekai). "generates high-quality minute-level videos across diverse scenes at an ultra-fast speed of 25 FPS."
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: "we initialize our model with SkyReels-V2-I2V-1.3B [8], which follows Wan 2.1 [44] architecture." "We remove the text injection modules from the released checkpoint." "action modules are added into each DiT block, leading to the total model size as 1.8B." So 1.3B base → 1.8B total, i.e. ~0.5B of added action modules. "The foundation model is derived from the Wan [44] I2V design. By removing the text branch and adding action modules as in Matrix-Game [57], the model predicts next frames only from visual contents and corresponding actions." Image input is encoded by both the 3D VAE encoder and a CLIP image encoder as condition.
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: "A 3D Causal VAE [55, 20] is first employed to compress raw video data along both spatial and temporal dimensions — by a factor of 8×8 in space and 4 in time." (Wan2.1-VAE lineage.) Latent channel count not stated. Video is 352×640, 57 frames per training clip.
- **Objective**: Two-phase distillation onto a causal student. "The distillation process comprises two key phases: student initialization and DMD-based [52] Self-Forcing training." Student init is a regression to the teacher's ODE trajectory: `L_student = E_{x,t^i} ‖G_φ({x_{t^i}^i}_{i=1}^L, {c^i}_{i=1}^L, {t^i}_{i=1}^L) − {x_0^i}_{i=1}^L‖²`, with `t` "sampled from 3 steps subset of [0, T]". Then DMD aligns `p_{θ,t}(x_t^{1:N})` with the teacher's `p_{real,t}(x_t^{1:N})` under Self-Forcing, where "the generator samples previous frames from its own distribution rather than the ground-truth training data". Explicitly contrasted with Diffusion Forcing and Teacher Forcing as exposure-bias fixes. The teacher/foundation objective is the inherited Wan/SkyReels flow-matching loss; the paper does not restate its form. Self-forcing is noted as "a data-free training method".
- **Context and temporal modelling**: Frames as tokens with **block-wise causal attention**: "block-wise causal masks are applied to keys and values in each attention layer". Latents are split into chunks: "The chunk size of latent frames and attention local size are set as 3 and 6, respectively." KV cache: "The KV-caching mechanism enables efficient sequential generation by maintaining a fixed-length cache of recent latents and action embeddings. Our rolling cache implementation automatically manages memory by evicting oldest tokens when exceeding capacity, supporting infinite-length generation." The cache window is deliberately constrained: "we constrain the KV-cache window size. This forces the model to rely more on its learned priors" — an ablation on KV-cache local size is reported, with 6-frame caches preferred. RoPE replaces the sin-cos embeddings used in Matrix-Game 1.0 "to facilitate long video generation". History carries no added noise in the Oasis sense; the self-forcing rollout plays that role.
- **Action conditioning**: "continuous mouse actions are directly concatenated to the input latent representations, forwarded through an MLP layer, and then passed through a temporal self-attention layer. Furthermore, keyboard actions are queried by the fused features through a cross-attention layer, leading to precise controllability for interactions." Frame-level granularity. Added modules sit "into each DiT block"; their parameter count is the 1.3B → 1.8B delta, ~0.5B, not itemised further.
- **Data**: "~1200 hours" headline. Breakdown: "about 800-hour action-annotated video at 360p resolution in total. The data includes 153-hour Minecraft video data and 615-hour Unreal Engine data, arranged into 57 frames for each video clip"; plus "the open-source Sekai dataset [24], obtaining an additional 85 hours of training data after data curation" (frame-resampled to match Unreal navigation speed); plus "574-hour GTA-driver data and 560-hour Temple Run game data… for additional fine-tuning." "All the videos are resized to 352×640 resolution." Curation: "We collected over 1.2 million video clips through our data curation pipeline… The overall accuracy of the data exceeded 99%, and the system achieved a 50-fold improvement in the precision of the camera rotation… the pipeline supported dual concurrent data streams per GPU." Recording uses Unreal navigation-mesh path planning and a GTA5 Script Hook V plugin with frame-accurate input capture; quaternion double-precision fixed "a critical error rate of 0.2% in camera rotation calculations". fps of the source data is not stated. The dataset itself is not released.
- **Training recipe**: Three phases. (1) "To stabilize the whole training process, we firstly fine-tune the model for 5k steps" (before action modules are added). (2) "We train the foundation model for 120k steps with learning rate=2e-5, batch size=256." (3) "For distillation, we firstly collect 40k ODE pairs and fine-tune the causal student model for 6k steps, with subsequent 4k training steps via DMD-based Self-Forcing. The learning rate is 6e-6." **GPU count and type for training are not stated** (the only H100 mention is inference); wall time, GPU-hours, optimizer, precision, and EMA are all **not stated**.
- **Sampler and inference**: "few-step auto-regressive diffusion" — **3 steps**, matching the "3 steps subset of [0, T]" used for the ODE trajectories. "achieving 25 FPS generation on a single H100 GPU while maintaining minute-long temporal consistency". Output 352×640. Distillation is the whole point (Self-Forcing + DMD); an ablation compares acceleration techniques.
- **Evaluation**: GameWorld Score benchmark (borrowed from Matrix-Game 1.0). Minecraft, vs Oasis: Image Quality 0.61 vs 0.27; Aesthetic 0.50 vs 0.27; Temporal Cons. 0.94 vs 0.82; Motion smooth. 0.98 vs 0.99; **Keyboard Acc. 0.91 vs 0.73; Mouse Acc. 0.95 vs 0.56**; Obj. Cons. 0.64 vs 0.18; Scenario Cons. 0.80 vs 0.84. Wild scenes, vs YUME: Image Quality 0.67 vs 0.65; Aesthetic 0.51 vs 0.48; Temporal Cons. 0.86 vs 0.85; Motion smooth. 0.98 vs 0.99; Obj. Cons. 0.71 vs 0.77; Scenario Cons. 0.76 vs 0.80. Controllability is computed by the GameWorld IDM-precision protocol and is Minecraft-only: "Since the action controllability assessment in GameWorld Score Benchmark is designed specifically for Minecraft evaluation, it cannot be directly applied to wild scenes." No human study.
- **Reproducibility notes**: "We open-source our model weights and codebase"; "we will release the code and weights of our pre-trained models." Paper license CC BY 4.0. No repository URL or HF link appears in the v1 HTML, and the dataset is not offered. Stated limitations: OOD degradation, 352×640 is below SOTA resolution, and "lack of explicit memory" over long generations.
- **What of this is reproducible at our scale**: The recipe shape is reproducible even though the scale is not — initialise from a small public video DiT, add per-block action modules, train a bidirectional teacher, then distill a causal student. The two pieces worth lifting are the action-injection split (continuous look/turn concatenated to the latent through an MLP then temporal self-attention; discrete key presses via cross-attention) which we could test directly against our single-most-recent-action adaLN scheme, and the KV-cache local size ablation, which is exactly the question of how much context a Doom model actually needs — their answer of 6 latent frames at 4× temporal compression is ~24 raw frames, suspiciously close to our 32. Self-Forcing/DMD distillation is out of reach on one A6000 for the full pipeline, but the student-initialisation phase alone (regress a causal student onto 40k teacher ODE trajectories, 6k steps) is a small enough job to be a stretch goal after the main comparison lands. Their 3-step inference is the number to cite when arguing that step count, not parameter count, is what buys interactivity.

## Matrix-Game 1.0 (arXiv 2506.18701)

- **Task and domain**: Controllable image-to-world generation for Minecraft (plus procedural Unreal Engine data). "conditioned on a reference image, motion context, and user actions." Deliberately text-free: text "often imposes semantic biases, constrains spatial interpretation, and reduces the model's ability to ground understanding purely in visual and physical cues."
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: Multi-modal diffusion transformer (MMDiT) "following Flux [30] and HunyuanVideo [29]". "the model is initialized with pretrained weights from the HunyuanVideo image-to-video model [29]. To shift from text-driven generation to image-conditioned world modeling, we replace the original text branch in the multi-modal diffusion transformer with an image branch." Size: "With over 17 billion parameters"; "The final model, Matrix-Game, contains 17 billion parameters." Also adopts "the token replacement trick in HunyuanVideo I2V".
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: "a 3D Causal VAE [28, 68], which reduces the spatial and temporal resolution of video sequences by factors of 8 and 4, respectively." Latent channel count not stated. Reference image processed "by either a visual encoder or a multi-modal backbone".
- **Objective**: "Matrix-Game leverages the flow matching paradigm [11], which outperforms traditional denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPM) [23] in both convergence and sampling efficiency. Based on this paradigm, we adopt the **rectified flow loss** [36] for model training." Flow-matching shift parameter 15 at inference.
- **Context and temporal modelling**: Fixed-length bidirectional segments, extended autoregressively by a short motion window: "last k=5 frames from each generated segment" are kept as motion conditions; "the latent of these motion frames are concatenated with the noisy latent along the **channel dimension**, and a binary mask is also concatenated to indicate which frames contain valid motion information." Clip lengths are 17, 33, and 65 frames in stage 1 (aspect ratios 16:9, 4:3, 21:9), 33 frames then 65 frames in stage 2; 16 training fps. **Noise on history is explicit**: "inspired by the Open-Sora plan [33], we introduce Gaussian noise to the motion frames and reference images with a probability of 0.2 during training. In addition, we apply classifier-free guidance (CFG) to motion frames during training: the latents of motion frames are replaced with unconditioned signals (i.e., zero latents) with a probability of 0.25." No KV cache (full-sequence diffusion; Matrix-Game 2.0 calls it "limited to fixed-length generation").
- **Action conditioning**: "Inspired by Genie 2 [41], we employ frame-level control signals… following the action control design introduced in GameFactory [67]." Keyboard is discrete ("up," "down," "left," "right," "jump," "attack"); mouse is "continuous scalar values… defined as changes in pitch angle". "To align these action signals with the compressed latent tokens produced by the 3D Causal VAE, we adopt the group operation trick [67], which accounts for the temporal compression ratio." Mechanism: "The continuous mouse action is concatenated with the input latent and processed through an MLP followed by temporal self-attention, while the discrete keyboard actions are integrated via cross-attention to guide the diffusion process." Action CFG dropout probability 0.1. **Parameter count of the added control module is not stated.**
- **Data (Matrix-Game-MC)**: "over 2,700 hours of unlabeled gameplay video clips and over 1,000 hours of high-quality labeled clips with fine-grained keyboard and mouse action annotations." Stage 1 uses "2,700-hour unlabeled Minecraft videos at 720p resolution", then "a subset of 870-hour high-quality" clips for refinement. Labelled: "over 1,026 hours of video clips for 33-frame training" and "more than 1,200 hours of video for 65-frame training"; "approximately half of this balanced set is sourced from MineRL-based scenarios spanning 14 distinct Minecraft biomes". Sources: MineDojo, a MineRL exploration agent, and procedural Unreal Engine video. 720p, 16 fps action sampling. Curation is a three-stage hierarchical filter: DOVER (video quality, genre-specific thresholds), LAION aesthetic predictor (genre-aware thresholds), IDM (menu/idle-state detection), CRAFT (subtitle detection), DeepFace (faces), GMFlow (motion, bidirectional), IDM again (camera yaw/pitch filtering). Dataset release is not promised — only weights and the benchmark.
- **Training recipe**: Two stages. Stage 1, unlabelled pretraining for world understanding (2,700 h at mixed frame counts and aspect ratios, then 870 h refinement), action module excluded. Stage 2, action-labelled training (1,200 h of 720p 33-frame clips, "fixed 720p resolution and a 33-frame setting in the early training phase", then a rebalanced set for 65-frame training). Hyperparameters: "a per-GPU batch size of 1. We employed **bf16** mixed precision and the **Fully Sharded Data Parallel (FSDP)** strategy… The learning rate was set to 5×10⁻⁵, with 16 training FPS and 5 motion frames." **GPU count and type, total steps per stage, wall time, GPU-hours, optimizer, and EMA are all not stated.**
- **Sampler and inference**: "The CFG scale was set to 6, with **50 sampling steps** using Flow Matching. The flow matching shift parameter was set to 15." CFG is applied to the reference image, motion frames, and action signals. **Inference speed is not reported anywhere** — no fps, no latency, no hardware. No distillation.
- **Evaluation**: GameWorld Score (introduced here), 8 dimensions. Table 2 — Matrix-Game / MineWorld / OASIS: Image Quality 0.72 / 0.69 / 0.65; Aesthetic 0.49 / 0.47 / 0.48; Temporal Cons. 0.97 / 0.95 / 0.94; Motion smooth. 0.98 / 0.98 / 0.98; **Keyboard Acc. 0.95 / 0.86 / 0.77; Mouse Acc. 0.95 / 0.64 / 0.56**; Obj. Cons. 0.76 / 0.51 / 0.56; Scenario Cons. 0.93 / 0.92 / 0.86. **Controllability metric, stated in full**: an Inverse Dynamics Model infers actions from the generated video and they are compared to the ground-truth inputs; "IDM, trained on 1,962 hours of Minecraft gameplay, achieves 90.6% accuracy on keyboard prediction and an R² score of 0.97 for mouse movement regression, making it a reliable proxy". Keyboard accuracy is precision over four mutually-exclusive groups: (forward, back, empty), (left, right, empty), (attack, empty), (jump, empty). Scenario consistency is an out-and-back test: MSE between paired frames after a camera excursion large enough to move the scene out of view, "allowing up to 4-pixel shifts to handle minor misalignments". Human study: two independent double-blind annotator groups, win rate = "the proportion of scenario-metric pairs in which each method is rated as the best"; Overall Quality **96.30%**, Controllability **93.76%**, Visual Quality 98.23%, Temporal Consistency 89.56%.
- **Reproducibility notes**: "we will open-source the Matrix-Game model weights and the GameWorld Score benchmark at https://github.com/SkyworkAI/Matrix-Game". Training code and Matrix-Game-MC are not offered. Baselines OASIS and MineWorld are both public, which is why this is the one paper here with a like-for-like comparison.
- **What of this is reproducible at our scale**: The 17B model is not, but three methodological pieces are, and they are the most directly useful things in this batch. First, the GameWorld controllability protocol — train an IDM on our Doom action labels, run it over generated rollouts, and report per-action-group precision — gives us the controllability number our paper currently lacks, and it is cheap because we already have paired frame/action data. Second, their motion-context design is exactly our channel-stacking choice (k=5 motion frames concatenated on the channel dimension plus a validity mask), which means our 32-frame channel stack is the same family, just longer; their history-noise probabilities (Gaussian noise on motion frames with p=0.2, zeroed motion latents with p=0.25) are two hyperparameters we can adopt verbatim to fight autoregressive drift. Third, bf16 + FSDP at per-GPU batch size 1 with lr 5e-5 is the concrete answer to "what learning rate does a flow-matching video DiT want", and 5e-5 is a sane anchor for our runs. Their 50 sampling steps and silence on inference speed is also a useful contrast to cite: full-sequence flow matching at 720p simply is not interactive, which is the gap Matrix-Game 2.0 then closes.

## GameGen-X (arXiv 2411.00769)

- **Task and domain**: Open-world game video generation *and* interactive control. "the first diffusion transformer model specifically designed for both generating and interactively controlling open-world game videos." Control signals are structured text instructions plus keyboard inputs, not a full action space; it is closer to a directable video generator than a frame-level world model.
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: "Masked Spatial-Temporal Diffusion Transformer (MSDiT)", "composed of stacked Spatial Transformer Blocks and Temporal Transformer Blocks, along with an initial embedding layer and a final layer… our MSDiT consists of **28 layers**, with each layer containing both a spatial and temporal transformer block". "the latent channel dimension is set to **1152**. For the transformer blocks, we use **16 attention heads** and apply several techniques such as query-key normalization (QK norm) and rotary position embeddings (RoPE)." The embedding layer does "a 2x downsampling along the height and width dimensions". **Parameter count is never stated**, and **initialisation is never stated** — the paper says only that the *dataset* was built "from scratch"; Open-Sora is cited as related work, not as an initialisation.
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: "a 3D Spatio-Temporal Variational Autoencoder (3D-VAE) to compress video clips into latent representations", built as "We extended the 2D VAE architecture from **Stable Diffusion** by incorporating additional temporal layers… Multiple layers of Causal [convolutions]… reducing **spatial dimensions by a factor of 8 and temporal dimensions by a factor of 4**." Latent channel count not stated. Text encoder: "the T5 model with a maximum sequence length of 300 tokens." Metadata (aspect ratio, frame count, timestep, fps) is MLP-projected and added to the latent.
- **Objective**: Diffusion with rectified flow and classifier-free guidance — "we incorporated the bucket training, classifier-free diffusion guidance and rectified flow for better generation performance"; "Rectified flow was used to accelerate training and enhance generation accuracy." Text dropped with 25% probability. The masking scheme is the interesting part: "a masking mechanism that excludes certain frames from noise addition and denoising", with `M(i)=1 for i>x, M(i)=0 for i≤x`; "we unmask the frames that the model should condition on during video extension tasks. In the forward pass of the base model, **unmasked frames are assigned a timestep value of 0**, while the remaining frames retain their original timesteps." Task mix: "we allocated 75% of the training probability to text-to-video generation tasks and 25% to video extension tasks."
- **Context and temporal modelling**: Frames as tokens with paired spatial and temporal attention plus cross-attention to T5 features — "we adopt the design of stacking paired spatial and temporal blocks, where each block is equipped with cross-attention and one of spatial or temporal attention." Context is the unmasked prefix inside a clip; headline clip length is **102 frames at 720p** (~4 s at 24 fps). Bucket sampling spans "a range of resolutions (480p, 512×512, 720p, and 1024×1024) and durations (from single frames to 480 frames at 24 fps)"; e.g. 1024×1024/102 frames at 8.00% probability, 480p/408 frames at 18.00%. "during video extension tasks, we retain the first frame of latent as a reference." No KV cache; attention is bidirectional within the clip. No added noise on context frames (they are set to timestep 0, the opposite of Oasis's stabilisation trick).
- **Action conditioning (InstructNet)**: A frozen-backbone side network. "we keep the parameters of the pre-trained foundation model frozen, which preserves its inherent generation and continuation abilities. Meanwhile, the additional trainable InstructNet is introduced to handle control signals." Built from "N InstructNet blocks, each utilizing Operation Fusion Expert Layer and Instruct Fusion Expert Layer". Keyboard path: keys "are projected into one-hot encodings, and then transformed through an MLP to match the latent feature dimensionality. The resulting keyboard features are processed through an additional MLP to predict affine transformation parameters, which are subsequently applied to modify the latent features" — i.e. FiLM-style modulation, `ẑ = γ(f_O) ⊙ (z − μ)/σ + β(f_O)`. Text instructions enter by cross-attention; optional video prompts (canny edges, motion vectors, pose) go through the 3D-VAE encoder and are added to `z`. **N and the InstructNet parameter count are not stated.**
- **Data (OGameData)**: "OGameData comprises 1 million high-resolution video clips" — Table 1 lists it as Game domain, 1000k clips, 607 words/min caption density, GPT-4o captions, 720P-4k, "Generation & Control", **4000h**. From "over 150 games". Curation: manual filtering for metadata (game name, genre, perspective), then "PyScene and TransNetV2 to detect scene changes, discarding clips shorter than 4 seconds and splitting longer clips into 16-second segments", yielding "around 1,000,000 video clips, each containing 4-16 seconds of content at **24 frames per second**"; then CLIP-AVA (aesthetic), UniMatch (motion), VideoCLIP (content similarity), CoTrackerV2 (camera motion). The control subset is separate and self-recorded: games purchased on Steam, in-game weather/lighting events randomised "every 5-10 seconds", "we developed a virtual keyboard that randomly controls the character's movements", captured with Game Bar, "resulting in nearly **100 hours** of recorded data" with frame-aligned keyboard signals. Split into OGameData-GEN and OGameData-INS (the latter with deliberately shorter captions). Release: video IDs and metadata only — CSVs of 50K/100K/250K/860K samples on GitHub, CC BY 4.0, research-only.
- **Training recipe**: Two phases. "The **Adam** optimizer with a fixed **learning rate of 5e-4** was applied for **20 epochs**" with 25% text dropout, rectified flow and bucket sampling. Hardware and wall time, stated in full: "our training infrastructure consisted of **24 NVIDIA H800 GPUs** distributed across three servers, with each server hosting 8 GPUs equipped with 80GB of memory per unit. We implemented distributed training across both machines and GPUs, leveraging **Zero-2** optimization… The training process was structured into two phases: the base model training, which took approximately **25 days**, and the InstructNet training phase, completed in **7 days**. For storage, we utilized approximately **50TB**." (That works out to roughly 18,000 H800-GPU-hours, though the paper never states a GPU-hour figure itself.) Ablations: 20k GEN + 10k INS samples, 3 epochs generation + 2 epochs control, "All experiments are conducted on 8 H800 GPUs". Batch size, EMA, and precision are **not stated**.
- **Sampler and inference**: Sampling steps are a swept variable, not a fixed recipe. Table 10, time per 102-frame sample: 320×256 @ 10 steps — 7.5 s (13.6 fps) on A800, 5.1 s (20.0 fps) on H800; 848×480 @ 10 steps — 60 s (1.7 fps) / 20.1 s (5.07 fps); @ 30 steps — 136 s (0.75) / 44.1 s (2.31); @ 50 steps — 196 s (0.52) / 69.3 s (1.47); 1280×720 @ 10 steps — 160 s (0.64) / 38.3 s (2.66); @ 30 steps — 315 s (0.32) / 57.5 s (1.77); @ 50 steps — 435 s (0.23) / 160.1 s (0.64). "At this resolution and number of sampling steps [320×256, 10 steps], the model can achieve 20 FPS on a single H800 GPU." Quality vs steps (Table 11, Overall): 848×480 rises 0.737 → 0.800 from 10 to 50 steps; 1280×720 rises 0.655 → 0.812. No distillation.
- **Evaluation**: Generation (Table 2), all at the model's own resolution/frames — GameGen-X 720p/102: **FID 252.1, FVD 759.8, TVA 0.87, UP 0.82**, MS 0.99, DD 0.80, SC 0.94, IQ 0.50. Baselines: OpenSora1.2 (720p/102) FID 318.1, FVD 1016.3, TVA 0.50, UP 0.37; CogVideoX-5B (480p/49) FID 316.9, FVD 1310.2; OpenSora-Plan1.2 (720p/102) FID 407.0, FVD 1940.9; Mira (480p/60) FID 360.9, FVD 2254.2. Control (Table 3): GameGen-X **SR-C 63.0%, SR-E 56.8%**, UP 0.71, vs OpenSora-Plan1.2 26.6%/31.7%, CogVideoX-5B 23.0%/30.3%, OpenSora1.2 21.6%/14.2%. **Controllability is human-judged, not model-judged**: SR-C is the success rate for character actions and SR-E for environment events, scored by people. Human study: "We recruited 10 volunteers through an online application process, specifically selecting individuals with both gaming domain expertise and AIGC community experience… a blind evaluation protocol where videos and corresponding texts were presented without model attribution", covering user preference, video-text alignment, and control success rate.
- **Reproducibility notes**: Paper CC BY 4.0. The GitHub repo releases **dataset metadata only** (YouTube IDs and per-clip metadata in four CSVs); no checkpoints, no training code, no inference code, and the self-recorded ~100 h keyboard-labelled control data is not offered. Architecture is specified well enough to rebuild (28 layers, width 1152, 16 heads, QK norm, RoPE, SD-derived 3D VAE at 8×/4×) but the parameter count and initialisation are missing.
- **What of this is reproducible at our scale**: The architecture is the one in this batch we could actually clone — 28 layers at width 1152 with 16 heads is roughly DiT-XL's shape, so their MSDiT is a near-neighbour of our DiT-XL/2 arm, and their explicit choices (QK norm, RoPE, metadata added to the latent) are drop-in improvements we can test. Their frame-masking trick is the cleanest alternative to our channel stacking: keep the context frames in the sequence but assign them timestep 0 and exclude them from the loss, which lets one model do both unconditional generation and continuation without a separate architecture — worth one ablation against our channel-stacked baseline. The InstructNet pattern (freeze the foundation model, train only a side network that FiLM-modulates the latent from one-hot keys) is directly applicable and cheap: it would let us reuse a single pretrained Doom backbone across action-conditioning variants without retraining the trunk. Their compute (24× H800 for 25 days) and 4000-hour dataset are the honest reason our numbers will be lower, and their per-resolution speed table is the right citation for why we should report at low resolution: at 320×256 / 10 steps they hit 20 fps, and at 720p / 50 steps they hit 0.64 fps, on the same GPU.

## The Matrix (arXiv 2412.03568)

- **Task and domain**: Infinite-horizon, real-time, keyboard-controlled generation of realistic driving and walking worlds. "the first foundational realistic world simulator capable of generating infinitely long 720p high-fidelity real-scene video streams with real-time, responsive control in both first- and third-person perspectives." Domains: Forza Horizon 5 (driving), Cyberpunk 2077 (city walking), the DROID robot dataset (embodied arm control), and unlabelled real-world footage (Tokyo streets).
- **Backbone, parameter count, initialisation**: "the video DiT backbone, adapted from the publicly available DiT models [47]" — reference [47] is Zheng et al., **Open-Sora**. "using a pre-trained 2.3B parameter DiT model as the backbone". "The backbone consists of **32 attention blocks**, followed by a linear output head with LayerNorm. Each attention block includes a self-attention layer… a cross-attention layer… and a[n MLP]." Total after the added module: "The model is light and consists of **2.7B parameters**" (2.3B backbone + 0.4B Interactive Module). Token feature size 2048 (video embedding via 3D convolution; caption embedding is a two-layer FFN on T5 output, hidden and output size 2048).
- **Latent space / tokenizer**: "It employs a 3D Variational Auto-Encoder (VAE) to encode T×p video frames into T video tokens." Concretely the rate is given as "4 video tokens per second, each decoded into 4 frames by the VAE decoder", i.e. p=4 frames per token at a 16 fps working rate. Which VAE, its channel count, and its spatial compression factor are **not stated** (the citation is to Kingma's VAE paper, not a specific checkpoint).
- **Objective**: Standard DiT diffusion, reorganised into a streaming schedule — the **Shift-Window Denoising Process Model (Swin-DPM)**. "within each window, a queue of video tokens undergoes denoising at various noise levels. After k denoising steps (where k×T is the number of diffusion solver steps), the leftmost, lowest-noisy token is dequeued into a cache. To maintain the queue length, a new token with Gaussian noise will be then added to the rightmost position. Each cached token is re-appended to the window's token queue at noise level 0 until the next token is cached." Token stride s=1. The loss form itself is not restated. Final stage is **Consistency Model distillation**: a Stream Consistency Model "distills the original diffusion process and its class-free guidance into a four-step consistency model while incorporating the denoising window design from Swin-DPM… This integration results in a 10-20× acceleration in inference speed."
- **Context and temporal modelling**: A sliding token window with per-token noise levels — the same idea family as diffusion forcing, applied to a token queue. "During training, we sample 2w video tokens, where w is the window size. We usually set w=T. The first w tokens are used solely for warming up Swin-DPM and do not participate in backpropagation; loss is computed only on the last w tokens. At inference time, we follow the same setup: the first w tokens are for warmup and are discarded." Dequeued tokens are held in a cache and keep participating in attention (a hand-rolled KV cache in all but name). Each token covers 4 frames at 16 fps, so the window is short in wall-clock terms; w and T are not given a numeric value.
- **Action conditioning (Interactive Module)**: Keyboard presses are first **verbalised**: keys are translated into natural-language descriptions, "processed by a T5 encoder and transformed into a vector embedding through two linear layers and a SiLU layer between them. This vector embedding is then concatenated with its corresponding video token and the next ω video tokens, where ω is a pre-defined causal relation range, typically set to **ω=4**." Placement: "After every two DiT blocks, the module merges the keyboard inputs into the video token feature through a **Causal Cross-Attention Layer**, where each keyboard input is limited to influence only the current and subsequent ω tokens." With 32 blocks that means "**16 Interactive Module[s]**", totalling **0.4B added parameters**. Robustness trick: a default caption "The camera is moving in an unknown way." replaces labelled inputs "with probability q=0.1".
- **Data (the Source dataset)**: "Synthesized Observations of Unreal Rendered Contextual Environments (Source)". Raw collection: "For Forza Horizon 5, we collected approximately **1,200,000** pairs of video and control signals, while for Cyberpunk 2077, we gathered around **1,000,000** such pairs. All collected videos have a duration of approximately **6 seconds, recorded at 60 FPS**." After segmentation and GPT-4o captioning: "**750k labeled samples and 1.2 million unlabeled samples**, all with 60 FPS." Plus real-world unlabelled footage (Tokyo streets) and, for the embodied experiment, "50,000 6-second clips from the DROID dataset, along with per-frame action labels of joint angles for seven joints." Preprocessing: "all video inputs were resized to a resolution of **1280×720** pixels and set to **16 FPS**." Collection pipeline is a purpose-built "GameData Platform" on open-source tools: Cheat Engine (in-game world status — character (x,y,z) and camera movement — used for stuck detection and sample filtering), the Reshade plugin (removes game UI/HUD "to ensure a more consistent data distribution"), OBS (recording), and a Key Extractor aligning keypresses to frames; for Forza a telemetry socket gives XYZ, velocities and accelerations. Dataset not currently released.
- **Training recipe**: Four stages, all with the same common settings. "All training procedures were executed with an overall **batch size of 32** and a **learning rate of 1×10⁻⁵**. Mixed-precision training was employed using **bfloat16**… For sequences exceeding 25,200 frames in length, we used the **Deepspeed Ulysses** sequence parallelism strategy, distributing the sequence across **8 GPUs**." Stages: (1) LoRA warm-up on unlabelled Source — "we fine-tuned all linear layers of the base DiT model using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)… The **LoRA rank was set to 128**, and the model was trained for **20,000 steps**"; (2) Interactive Module training on labelled Source, "**20,000 steps** with the same batch size, introducing another 0.4B parameter"; (3) "we fine-tune The Matrix model using **Swin-DPM over 60,000 steps**"; (4) "For the final Consistency Model distillation, we use the Swin-DPM checkpoints as a teacher model and train the student network for **10,000 steps**". **GPU model is never named** (only "8 GPUs"); wall time, GPU-hours, optimizer, and EMA are **not stated**.
- **Sampler and inference**: Four-step consistency model after distillation. Speed, per Table 2: the 2.3B backbone does "48 frames / 34 Seconds"; adding the Interactive Module slows it to "48 frames / 55 Seconds"; Swin-DPM alone runs at **0.8 FPS**; "+ SCM" reaches **8–16 FPS** — "significantly improving rendering speed from 0.8 FPS to 8 - 16 FPS". Output 1280×720. **Inference hardware is never named.** For context the paper notes "state-of-the-art 2D platformer game generator Genie runs as slow as 1 FPS."
- **Evaluation**: Table 2 is a cumulative ablation (component / scene / #params / speed / FVD↓ / FID↓ / CLIP↑ / Move-LPIPS↓ / Move-PSNR↑). DiT Backbone (2.3B): FVD 1016.30, FID 318.10, CLIP 0.30. + Warmup: Cyberpunk 2077 FVD 1429.45, FID 183.24, CLIP 0.28, Move-LPIPS 0.125, Move-PSNR 27.80; DROID 1133.16 / 224.98 / 0.29 / 0.191 / 27.72; Forza Horizon 5 1891.67 / 141.11 / 0.31 / 0.128 / 26.89. + Interactive Module (2.7B, 48 frames / 55 s): Cyberpunk 1112.49 / 173.31 / 0.28 / 0.129 / 28.24; DROID 1200.82 / 237.66 / 0.30 / 0.180 / 27.90; Forza 1211.30 / 119.20 / 0.27 / 0.125 / 28.98. + Swin-DPM (Forza, 0.8 FPS): 1651.50 / 163.27 / 0.24 / 0.113 / **29.90**. + SCM (Forza, 8–16 FPS): **1936.79 / 153.80 / 0.23 / 0.109 / 29.73**. The controllability metrics are **Move-LPIPS and Move-PSNR**, "computed between the generated videos and test videos with ground truth movements" — i.e. pixel/perceptual agreement with a held-out rollout that executed the same action sequence, no IDM and no human raters. No human study. Generalisation is shown qualitatively (a BMW X3 driving through an office).
- **Reproducibility notes**: "All the codes, data, and model checkpoints in this paper will be open sourced." No repository URL appears in the paper (project page https://thematrix1999.github.io/); at time of reading nothing is confirmed released. Paper CC BY 4.0. The GameData Platform is described in enough detail to rebuild (Cheat Engine + Reshade + OBS + a key extractor, plus a public Forza telemetry listener they link).
- **What of this is reproducible at our scale**: Move-PSNR and Move-LPIPS are the single most portable idea here for us — hold out a Doom rollout, replay its exact action sequence through the model, and measure PSNR/LPIPS against the real frames. That is precisely the paired-rollout metric our PSNR/LPIPS harness already wants to be, it needs no IDM, and it gives a controllability number for both the DiT-XL/2 and the U-Net arm under one recipe. Their staged training order is also worth copying at our budget: adapt the backbone to the domain first (they use LoRA rank 128 for 20k steps), then train only the action-conditioning module, rather than learning visuals and control at once. Their Interactive Module placement — action cross-attention after every second block, with each action limited to the current and next ω=4 tokens — is a concrete, cheap alternative to our current single-most-recent-action adaLN scheme, and the causal window is the knob that decides how long an action keeps influencing the future. The parts we should not attempt are Swin-DPM plus consistency distillation on one A6000, and we should note in the paper that their own table shows quality *degrading* as they buy speed (FVD 1211 → 1651 → 1937 across Swin-DPM and SCM on Forza) — a useful caution against treating real-time numbers as free.
