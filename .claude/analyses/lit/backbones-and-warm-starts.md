# Backbones and warm starts — evidence cards

Scope: candidate pretrained transformers to warm-start an action-conditioned Doom world model whose latent space is the SD 1.x KL-f8 autoencoder (4 channels, 8x spatial downsample, scaling factor 0.18215). Target input is a 132-channel 32x40 latent (33 frames x 4 channels, channel-stacked), conditioned on a 29-way action id and a 10-way noise bucket.

All numbers below are quoted from the sources fetched. "not stated" means the source did not say it; nothing here is inferred unless explicitly labelled as such.

## DiT — Scalable Diffusion Models with Transformers (arXiv 2212.09748, https://github.com/facebookresearch/DiT)

- **What it is** (one line): Class-conditional latent diffusion transformer on ImageNet; the architecture our model already is.
- **Transformer / model size** (parameters of the diffusion network alone): Config table quoted as "DiT-S/12 layers/384 hidden/6 heads/1.4 Gflops, DiT-B/12/768/12/5.6, DiT-L/24/1024/16/19.7, DiT-XL/28/1152/16/29.1". Explicit parameter counts are not stated in the DiT paper text retrieved; the SiT paper, which states it uses identical configurations, gives SiT-S 33M, SiT-B 130M, SiT-L 458M, SiT-XL 675M.
- **Latent space / VAE** (exact identity): **Yes**, our space. Paper: "Off-the-shelf pre-trained variational autoencoder from Stable Diffusion with encoder downsample factor of 8", latents of "shape 32×32×4" at 256x256 and 64×64×4 at 512x512. Repo code confirms the checkpoint and the scale: `vae = AutoencoderKL.from_pretrained(f"stabilityai/sd-vae-ft-{args.vae}")` with `args.vae` defaulting to `mse`, and `samples = vae.decode(samples / 0.18215).sample`. The README's evaluation note says FID is "computed with 250 DDPM sampling steps, with the `mse` VAE decoder and without guidance (`cfg-scale=1`)".
- **Pretraining data**: ImageNet, 1000 classes, at 256×256 and 512×512.
- **Conditioning mechanism**: adaLN-Zero. "Rather than directly learn dimension-wise scale and shift parameters γ and β, we regress them from the sum of the embedding vectors of t and c", plus "dimension-wise scaling parameters α that are applied immediately prior to any residual connections". Classifier-free guidance trained with "10% dropout" of the label to a null embedding.
- **Parameterisation and schedule**: "Predict noise instead of pixels" with "learned reverse process covariance Σθ"; "Linear variance schedule ranging from 1×10⁻⁴ to 2×10⁻²" with "tmax=1000".
- **Training recipe**: "AdamW optimizer, constant learning rate of 1×10⁻⁴, no weight decay", "no learning rate warmup nor regularization". Batch size 256. Hardware "TPU-v3 pods". EMA decay 0.9999. Augmentation: "horizontal flips" only. "Models trained for 7M and 400K steps respectively" (7M for the headline XL/2-256 result). Precision not stated in the retrieved text.
- **Released checkpoints**: Exactly two, both DiT-XL/2: `https://dl.fbaipublicfiles.com/DiT/models/DiT-XL-2-256x256.pt` (FID-50K 2.27, IS 278.24) and `https://dl.fbaipublicfiles.com/DiT/models/DiT-XL-2-512x512.pt` (FID-50K 3.04, IS 240.82). File sizes not stated.
- **Adaptation notes for our task**: This is the incumbent warm start and the one currently losing to the SD 1.4 U-Net. Its `x_embedder` is a Conv2d(4 → 1152, kernel 2, stride 2), so a 132-channel input needs a new patch embedding — replicating the pretrained 4-channel kernel across all 33 frame slots and dividing by 33 preserves the pretrained mapping for a static clip, which is the cheapest principled init. Positional embeddings are fixed 2D sin-cos, so regenerating them for a 16×20 token grid (32×40 latent, patch 2) costs nothing, unlike a learned table. The 1000-way class embedding table is dead weight: replace it with a 29-way action embedding plus a 10-way noise-bucket embedding summed into the same adaLN conditioning vector, and reuse the existing label-dropout path for cfg.

## PixArt-alpha (arXiv 2310.00426, https://github.com/PixArt-alpha/PixArt-alpha, https://huggingface.co/PixArt-alpha/PixArt-XL-2-512x512)

- **What it is** (one line): A 0.6B DiT-XL/2-shaped text-to-image transformer trained cheaply by inserting cross-attention to a frozen T5 and using adaLN-single.
- **Transformer / model size**: "0.6B" (model card: "0.6B params"). Architecture: "Diffusion Transformer (DiT-XL/2) with 28 Transformer blocks, patch size 2×".
- **Latent space / VAE**: **Yes**, our space. The repo names the VAE explicitly as **sd-vae-ft-ema** (80M parameters), hosted at `PixArt-alpha/PixArt-alpha/tree/main/sd-vae-ft-ema`, frozen. That is the same KL-f8 4-channel encoder as `sd-vae-ft-mse` (the two fine-tunes share the encoder; only the decoder differs), so encoded latents are numerically interchangeable with ours. The scaling factor 0.18215 is not stated in the sources fetched, but follows from the SD 1.x VAE config. The HF model card only says "one latent feature encoder (VAE)" without naming the variant.
- **Pretraining data**: Three stages. "Stage 1 (Pixel dependency): 1M ImageNet images at 256×256"; "Stage 2 (Text-image alignment): 10M SAM dataset images at 256×256"; "Stage 3 (Aesthetics): 14M high-quality images (4M JourneyDB + 10M internal) across 256×256, 512×512, 1024×1024". Captions on SAM auto-labelled with "LLaVA". The model card summarises training volume as "0.025B" images.
- **Conditioning mechanism**: "Cross-attention modules" inserted into each DiT block for text injection, with cross-attention output projections zero-initialised for identity mapping at init; "adaLN-single" replaces per-block adaLN regression with a shared global time embedding plus layer-specific trainable embeddings, "reducing parameters by 26% versus standard adaLN". Text encoder "Flan-T5-XXL" (4.3B), token length "120" (up from 77).
- **Parameterisation and schedule**: Not stated explicitly in the retrieved text. Epsilon prediction is implied; inference uses "DPM-Solver with 20 inference steps".
- **Training recipe**: "AdamW" with weight decay 0.03, constant learning rate "2e-5". Hardware "64 V100 GPUs for ~26 days"; the paper text gives "753 A100 GPU days" while the abstract gives "675 vs. 6,250 A100 GPU days" against Stable Diffusion v1.5; estimated cost "$28,400". Batch sizes "varied per stage (128-178 effective)". EMA not stated. Initialisation: "pre-trained ImageNet initialization used with re-parameterization technique to load original weights" — i.e. it warm-starts from DiT-XL/2 ImageNet weights and re-parameterises them into adaLN-single. The GitHub README does not mention the DiT init.
- **Released checkpoints**: PixArt-α-SAM-256 (0.6B), PixArt-α-256 (0.6B), PixArt-α-256-MSCOCO-FID7.32 (0.6B), PixArt-α-512 (0.6B), PixArt-α-1024 (0.6B), plus PixArt-δ variants: δ-1024-LCM (0.6B), δ-512-ControlNet (0.9B), δ-1024-ControlNet (0.9B). Sizes not stated. Model card loads via `PixArtAlphaPipeline`; license "CreativeML Open RAIL++-M License". Inference "requires at least 23GB of GPU memory", with diffusers configurations down to 8-11GB.
- **Adaptation notes for our task**: Same latent space and the same 28×1152 DiT-XL/2 skeleton as our model, but with far more visual diversity behind it than ImageNet-only DiT, so it is the most direct upgrade path. Two structural mismatches: the conditioning is adaLN-single (one shared six-vector modulation plus small per-layer deltas) rather than per-block adaLN-Zero regression, so our action and noise-bucket embeddings must be injected into that shared modulation instead of DiT's per-block MLPs; and roughly a third of each block is cross-attention to 120 T5 tokens, which has no counterpart here. Cheapest use of the cross-attention is to keep it and feed a short learned token sequence (action embedding + noise-bucket embedding) rather than deleting the layers and throwing away pretrained mass. Patch embedding and positional embedding need the same surgery as DiT (132-channel conv, 16×20 grid).

## PixArt-Sigma (arXiv 2403.04692)

- **What it is** (one line): PixArt-alpha continued weak-to-strong into 4K generation with a new VAE, longer text tokens, and KV token compression.
- **Transformer / model size**: "0.6B parameters".
- **Latent space / VAE**: **No.** The paper states it uses "a pre-trained and frozen VAE from SDXL", replacing PixArt-alpha's VAE via a rapid adaptation fine-tune of "2K steps". Channel count and downsample factor are not stated in the retrieved text. The SDXL VAE is a different KL-f8 autoencoder with its own weights and its own scaling factor, so PixArt-Sigma weights do **not** live in our latent space even though the tensor shape matches.
- **Pretraining data**: "Internal-Σ dataset: 33M high-resolution images (all exceeding 1K resolution)"; "4K subset: 2.3M images at ~4K resolution after aesthetic filtering". Captions from Share-Captioner, "average length of 180 words (mix of 60% generated + 40% raw captions)".
- **Conditioning mechanism**: Cross-attention to Flan-T5-XXL with text token length extended "from 120 to 300"; adaLN-single inherited from PixArt-alpha. KV token compression "reduces attention complexity from O(N²) to O(N²/R²) through group convolutions with a stride of 2 for local aggregation of keys and values", ~34% train/inference speedup.
- **Parameterisation and schedule**: Not stated in the retrieved text.
- **Training recipe**: Optimizer "CAME with weight decay=0, learning rate=2e-5". Hardware "32 V100 GPUs for 1K resolution; 16 A100 for 2K/4K". "Approximately 170 [GPU] days total (VAE adaptation through 4K compression stages)". Fine-tuned from the PixArt-α checkpoint (weak-to-strong). EMA and batch size not stated.
- **Released checkpoints**: 256px, 512px, 1K, 2K and 4K models produced through staged weak-to-strong training. Exact names/sizes not stated in the retrieved text.
- **Adaptation notes for our task**: The VAE swap is disqualifying unless we re-encode the whole Doom dataset with the SDXL VAE and change the decode path, which means every latent, every cached feature, and every metric baseline is regenerated. The upside (more and higher-resolution pretraining data than PixArt-alpha) does not pay for that at our resolution, since our latents are 32×40 and we gain nothing from 4K-capable weights. Note the paper's own VAE-adaptation trick is reusable evidence in the other direction: 2K steps was enough to move a transformer from one VAE to another, so "wrong VAE" is a medium blocker rather than an absolute one.

## Open-Sora 1.0 and 1.1 (report_01.md, report_02.md) and the Open-Sora technical report (arXiv 2412.20404)

- **What it is** (one line): An open reproduction of a Sora-style video latent diffusion transformer (STDiT) that warm-starts from PixArt weights and adds temporal attention.
- **Transformer / model size**: 1.0 — "Parameter count increased from 580M to 724M after adding temporal components". 1.1 — "700M parameters" (STDiT-2). 1.2 (covered by the arXiv report) — "Base (from PixArt-α): 580M parameters; With temporal attention added: 1.1B parameters".
- **Latent space / VAE**: **Yes for 1.0 and 1.1; no for 1.2.** Report 01: "2D VAE (from Stability-AI)", specifically the "sd-vae-ft-mse-original" model. The arXiv report confirms for both: versions 1.0 and 1.1 "Utilized Stability-AI's 2D VAE (84M parameters), which compresses video spatially by a factor of 8x8", with temporal reduction done by frame extraction. Report 02 does not restate the VAE. Version 1.2 replaces it with a custom 3D VAE: "first compresses spatially by 8x8 and subsequently temporally by 4x", 384M parameters total (300M 3D + 84M 2D) — that is **not** our space. Scaling factor not stated anywhere in the fetched sources.
- **Pretraining data**: 1.0 — "366K video clips from HD-VG-130M" then "20k relatively high quality videos from Pexels"; captions from "LLaVA, an image captioning model, with three frames and a designed prompt". 1.1 — "Pretraining: 9.7M videos + 2.6M images; Fine-tuning: 560k videos + 1.6M images", sources including Panda-70M. 1.2 (arXiv report) — "approximately 30M video clips totaling 80k hours" from Webvid-10M, Panda-70M, HD-VG-130M plus high-quality and free-licensed subsets.
- **Conditioning mechanism**: T5 cross-attention inherited from PixArt-alpha, with "temporal attention right after each spatial attention" (STDiT). 1.1 adds RoPE for temporal attention, QK-normalization with RMSNorm, and dynamic input size support; "T5 tokens extended from 120 to 200".
- **Parameterisation and schedule**: 1.0 and 1.1 are DDPM/eps (inherited from PixArt). The arXiv report states the model "transitioned from DDPM (discrete-time) to rectified flow (continuous-time)" at 1.2.
- **Training recipe**: 1.0 — learning rate "2e-5" (reduced from 1e-4), "bf16 for training on 64x512x512" with fp16 "deemed unstable"; progressive stages "16x256x256 on 366K pretraining datasets, and then 16x256x256, 16x512x512, and 64x512x512 on 20K datasets"; batch size, optimizer, GPU count and duration not stated. 1.1 — three stages over "~9 days" on "64 H800 GPUs"; stage 1 "81k steps (24k + 40k + 17k)" mostly 144p, stage 2 "22k steps" 240p–480p, stage 3 "4k steps" 480p–720p; lr and per-stage batch sizes not stated; initialised by fine-tuning "from PixArt-alpha-1024 checkpoints for 6k steps" before the main pretrain. 1.2 (arXiv report) — "12×8 H100 GPU setup", "~35,000 H100 GPU hours; approximately 70k steps over two weeks", "AdamW with epsilon 1×10⁻¹⁵", lr "5×10⁻⁵ (flow matching)", stages of "30k + 23k + 15k steps", initialised from the "PixArt-Σ 2K checkpoint". EMA not stated.
- **Released checkpoints**: 1.0 — not stated in report_01. 1.1 — "STDiT-2 checkpoints available", specific names not stated in report_02. The arXiv report says all "model weights" and "training/inference/data preparation codes" are "publicly available" at the GitHub repository.
- **Adaptation notes for our task**: Open-Sora 1.1 (STDiT-2, 700M, sd-vae-ft-mse) is the only candidate that is simultaneously in our latent space, DiT-XL-shaped, and trained on real video dynamics rather than still images — exactly the prior our channel-stacked model lacks. But its temporal structure is orthogonal to ours: it treats time as a separate token axis with its own attention and RoPE, whereas we stack 33 frames into 132 channels and have no temporal axis at all. Transplanting it means either keeping only the spatial blocks and dropping every temporal attention block — which discards most of what makes it a video model and leaves something close to PixArt-alpha with extra drift — or restructuring to a token-per-frame layout, which is a rewrite rather than a warm start. Versions 1.2 and later are off the table on VAE grounds.

## Latte (arXiv 2401.03048, https://github.com/Vchitect/Latte)

- **What it is** (one line): A latent video diffusion transformer that factorises spatial and temporal attention over DiT blocks, trained on small video benchmarks and extended to T2V.
- **Transformer / model size**: "the Latte-XL configuration containing 673.68M parameters". Latte-1 (T2V) is listed on Hugging Face as "1B params".
- **Latent space / VAE**: **Yes.** Paper: "Leverages a pre-trained variational autoencoder from Stable Diffusion 1.4". The released Latte-1 VAE config confirms `"_class_name": "AutoencoderKL"`, `latent_channels: 4`, `block_out_channels: [128, 256, 512, 512]`, `scaling_factor: 0.18215` — identical geometry and scale to our space. The repo also offers "Using temporal decoder of VAE" at sampling, which changes only the decoder, not the encoder.
- **Pretraining data**: "FaceForensics, SkyTimelapse, UCF101, Taichi-HD", using "16-frame video clips...with each frame resized to 256×256 resolution". Latte-1's own training data is not stated on its model card.
- **Conditioning mechanism**: "S-AdaLN" (scalable adaptive layer norm) for timestep and class injection, with learned scaling parameters — i.e. DiT-style adaLN, not cross-attention, for the class-conditional models. The T2V variant "extends to T2V using text embeddings via pre-trained PixArt-α", so Latte-1 carries PixArt-style text cross-attention. The paper compares four spatio-temporal factorisations; variant 1 (alternating spatial and temporal blocks) performs best.
- **Parameterisation and schedule**: "Predicts noise (epsilon prediction)" with "learned reverse process covariance"; both simple MSE and variational lower bound objectives. Schedule not stated.
- **Training recipe**: "AdamW with learning rate 1×10⁻⁴", EMA decay 0.9999, "8 NVIDIA A100 (80G) GPUs", horizontal flipping only. The UCF-101 training config gives `Latte-XL/2`, `image_size: 256`, `num_frames: 16`, `local_batch_size: 5`, `learning_rate: 1e-4`, `ckpt_every: 10000`. Total steps and global batch size not stated.
- **Released checkpoints**: "All checkpoints (including FaceForensics, SkyTimelapse, UCF101, and Taichi-HD) can be found [at] https://huggingface.co/maxin-cn/Latte/tree/main". Latte-1: "Pre-trained model can be downloaded [at] https://huggingface.co/maxin-cn/Latte-1/tree/main/transformer. We support both T2V and T2I." Released 23 May 2024, safetensors. Individual file sizes and resolutions are not enumerated in the README or the model card.
- **Adaptation notes for our task**: The class-conditional Latte checkpoints are the closest structural analogue to what we want — DiT-XL/2 shape, our VAE and scale factor, adaLN-only conditioning (so our 29-way action id and 10-way noise bucket drop straight into the S-AdaLN embedding path with no cross-attention left stranded), and trained on video rather than stills. The problem is the data: FaceForensics, SkyTimelapse, UCF101 and Taichi-HD are small, narrow domains with almost nothing in common with first-person 3D rendering, so the visual prior may well be worse than ImageNet's. Same patch-embed and positional-grid surgery as DiT, plus the temporal blocks would have to be deleted or repurposed since our 33 frames live in the channel dimension. Realistically a cheap ablation, not a strong warm start.

## UniDiffuser (arXiv 2303.06555)

- **What it is** (one line): A ~1B U-ViT transformer that fits all joint and conditional distributions over image and text by perturbing each modality with its own timestep.
- **Transformer / model size**: "~952M" parameters; "Depth: 31 layers, Hidden size: 1536, Attention heads: 24, MLP size: 6144, Patch size: 2".
- **Latent space / VAE**: **Yes (encoder), with caveats.** The image encoder is "Stable Diffusion's image autoencoder" and the decoder is "Stable Diffusion's image decoder". Which SD VAE fine-tune and the scaling factor are **not stated** in the source. Images are handled at 512×512 in the later stages, so latents are 64×64×4. Note CLIP ViT-B/32 image embeddings (512-dim) are a *second*, parallel image representation in the token stream, not a replacement for the VAE latent.
- **Pretraining data**: "laion2B-en: ~2B image-text pairs", "laion-high-resolution: ~170M pairs (≥1024px)", "laion-aesthetics v2 5+: ~193M pairs (≥512px, filtered)". Text encoder is the CLIP text encoder (77 vectors × 768-dim) with a linear reduction 768 → 64; text decoder is a fine-tuned GPT-2 (124M).
- **Conditioning mechanism**: No adaLN and no cross-attention — all modality tokens are concatenated into a single sequence and the task is specified by two independent timesteps: conditioning is "different for different modalities via independent timesteps (t^x, t^y) where zero indicates conditioning and maximum timestep indicates marginalization".
- **Parameterisation and schedule**: Noise prediction. Schedule not stated. Sampling with "DPM-Solver with 50 steps".
- **Training recipe**: "AdamW (lr: 2e-4, weight decay: 0.03, β₁/β₂: 0.9/0.9)". Stage 1 "250K steps at 256×256 (batch: 11264)"; stage 2 "200K steps at 512×512 (batch: 2112)"; stage 3 "220K steps at 512×512 (batch: 2112)". Hardware "88 A100 (80GB) GPUs" for approximately 28 days. EMA not stated.
- **Released checkpoints**: Not enumerated in the retrieved text.
- **Adaptation notes for our task**: Same latent space and by far the largest visual pretraining corpus of any transformer here, but the architecture is a U-ViT with long skip connections and no adaLN conditioning path at all, so there is nowhere natural to put a 29-way action id or a 10-way noise bucket short of adding new conditioning machinery (extra tokens, or bolting adaLN onto a model that never had it). Depth and width (31×1536) do not match DiT-XL/2 (28×1152), so this is not a drop-in warm start — it would be a different model, not a re-initialised one. The one idea worth stealing regardless is the per-modality independent timestep: our 10-way noise bucket is structurally the same device, and UniDiffuser is evidence that one transformer can carry several independent noise levels in a single forward pass.

## SiT (arXiv 2401.08740) and REPA (arXiv 2410.06940)

- **What it is** (one line): SiT keeps the DiT architecture exactly and replaces the diffusion formulation with a continuous-time stochastic interpolant; REPA adds a representation-alignment loss to DINOv2 features that cuts training time by more than an order of magnitude.
- **Transformer / model size**: SiT follows DiT configurations identically: "SiT-S: 33M, SiT-B: 130M, SiT-L: 458M, SiT-XL: 675M". REPA reports the same sizes: "B/2 (130M), L/2 (458M), XL/2 (675M parameters)".
- **Latent space / VAE**: **Yes.** SiT uses "the same pre-trained VAE encoder and decoder models originally used in Stable Diffusion", input shape "32×32×4" for 256×256×3 images with patch size 2. REPA: "Stable Diffusion VAE encoding images to 32×32×4 latent representations". Neither source names ft-mse vs ft-ema explicitly or restates 0.18215, but both build on the DiT codebase, which defaults to `stabilityai/sd-vae-ft-mse` and `/ 0.18215`.
- **Pretraining data**: ImageNet at 256×256 (SiT also 512×512). REPA: ImageNet 256×256; the alignment target is DINOv2 (primarily DINOv2-g), whose own pretraining corpus is not stated in the fetched text.
- **Conditioning mechanism**: "Class conditioning leverages AdaLN-Zero block", classifier-free guidance at sampling — identical to DiT.
- **Parameterisation and schedule**: SiT uses a linear interpolant "(αₜ = 1-t, σₜ = t)" with "velocity field prediction rather than noise", and supports "both ODE (deterministic) and SDE (stochastic) pathways, with tunable diffusion coefficients post-training", in continuous time. REPA: "Standard velocity/denoising loss + λ × alignment loss", where the alignment "aligns hϕ(𝐡t) with 𝐲∗, where hϕ(𝐡t) is a projection of diffusion transformer encoder output", applied to early layers (optimal at layer 8), λ in 0.25–1.0 and saturating at 0.5, patch-wise NT-Xent or cosine similarity.
- **Training recipe**: Both: "AdamW with learning rate 1e-4", batch size 256, EMA 0.9999 (SiT), "400K steps (standard), 7M steps (extended XL)" for SiT and "400K for comparisons; extends to 4M for final results" for REPA. Hardware and warmup not stated in the retrieved text.
- **Released checkpoints**: SiT — "SiT-XL checkpoints trained on both 256×256 and 512×512 resolutions achieving FID-50K scores of 2.06 and 2.62 respectively under cfg=1.5". REPA — "SiT-XL/2 trained checkpoint with REPA available"; authors "promise implementation and model checkpoints". Speedup claim: "17.5× faster convergence than vanilla model" for SiT-XL/2; SiT-XL/2 + REPA at 400K steps reaches FID 7.9 versus vanilla SiT-XL/2 FID 8.3 at 7M steps; with cfg at 800 epochs, FID 1.80 (1.42 with guidance interval).
- **Adaptation notes for our task**: SiT-XL/2 is architecturally byte-compatible with our DiT-XL/2 — same 28×1152 blocks, same adaLN-Zero conditioning, same SD latent space — so the checkpoint loads with only the patch-embed, positional-grid and label-embedding surgery already described for DiT. The catch is the objective: SiT predicts velocity under a linear interpolant, so adopting its weights means adopting flow matching, and our loss, sampler, and the definition of the 10-way noise bucket all have to move to continuous time. REPA is the more interesting item on this line: it is an auxiliary loss rather than a checkpoint, and it is orthogonal to whichever warm start we pick — aligning layer-8 features to a frozen DINOv2 on Doom frames could buy convergence speed without touching the latent space or the conditioning at all. That is a cheap, self-contained ablation on exactly our architecture.

## Stable Diffusion v1-4 (https://huggingface.co/CompVis/stable-diffusion-v1-4)

- **What it is** (one line): The U-Net latent diffusion model whose warm start is currently beating our DiT-XL/2 — the reference point for what "a lot of in-our-latent-space pretraining" means.
- **Transformer / model size**: Parameter count of the U-Net is **not stated** in the model card.
- **Latent space / VAE**: **Yes — this defines our space.** "an encoder with relative downsampling factor of 8" producing latents of "H/8 × W/8 × 4", so "512×512 images become 64×64×4".
- **Pretraining data**: Checkpoint lineage quoted verbatim: v1-1 — "237k steps at 256×256 on laion2B-en, then 194k steps at 512×512 on laion-high-resolution"; v1-2 — resumed from v1-1, "515k steps at 512×512 on laion-improved-aesthetics"; v1-3 — resumed from v1-2, "195k steps at 512×512", adding "10% dropping of the text-conditioning to improve classifier-free guidance sampling"; v1-4 — resumed from v1-2, "225,000 steps at resolution 512x512 on laion-aesthetics v2 5+" plus the same text-conditioning dropout.
- **Conditioning mechanism**: Cross-attention to "CLIP ViT-L/14" text embeddings.
- **Parameterisation and schedule**: eps prediction under DDPM; not restated in the model card excerpt.
- **Training recipe**: Hardware "32 x 8 x A100 GPUs". Optimizer AdamW. Batch size 2048 ("32 × 8 × 2 × 4"). Learning rate: "warmup to 0.0001 for 10,000 steps and then kept constant". "Total GPU Hours [150,000] & Emissions [11250 kg CO2 eq.]".
- **Released checkpoints**: `CompVis/stable-diffusion-v1-4` plus the v1-1/v1-2/v1-3 lineage. Sizes not stated in the excerpt.
- **Adaptation notes for our task**: Nothing to adapt — this is the incumbent winner, and the card explains why. Roughly 1.17M optimizer steps at batch 2048 over LAION-scale data, versus DiT-XL/2's 7M steps at batch 256 over 1.28M ImageNet images, means the U-Net has seen far more image-tokens and vastly more visual diversity in *our exact latent space*. Any transformer warm start should be judged on that axis: not "is this a transformer trained on SD latents" but "has it seen LAION-scale diversity in SD latents". On that test PixArt-alpha (0.025B images) and Open-Sora 1.1 are the only serious contenders, and UniDiffuser is the only one trained at genuine LAION scale.

## VPT — Video PreTraining (arXiv 2206.11795)

- **What it is** (one line): Not a diffusion backbone — the canonical recipe for turning unlabelled gameplay video into action-labelled data with an inverse dynamics model.
- **Transformer / model size**: IDM "approximately 0.5 billion trainable weights"; VPT foundation model "0.5 billion parameter" behavioural-cloning policy.
- **Latent space / VAE**: Not applicable — the IDM operates on pixels, "128 consecutive image frames" at "128×128×3".
- **Pretraining data**: IDM trained on "1962 hours of data" of contractor-labelled gameplay. Pseudo-labelling corpus: "approximately 270,000 hours of raw video, reducing it to ∼70k hours" of clean labelled data. Abstract: "we are the first to report computer agents that can craft diamond tools, which can take proficient humans upwards of 20 minutes (24,000 environment actions) of gameplay to accomplish."
- **Conditioning mechanism**: Not applicable. IDM architecture: non-causal (sees past *and* future frames), "Initial 3D convolution layer (128 filters, temporal kernel width of 5)", a ResNet image network, then "residual unmasked attention layers".
- **Parameterisation and schedule**: Not applicable — the IDM is a classifier over actions; the foundation model is behavioural cloning.
- **Training recipe**: Foundation model minimises "the negative log-likelihood of actions predicted by the IDM on clean data", trained "for 30 epochs using 720 V100 GPUs over approximately 9 days". IDM accuracy: "90.6% keypress accuracy and a 0.97 R² for mouse movements". IDM optimizer, lr and batch not stated in the retrieved text.
- **Released checkpoints**: Not stated in the retrieved text.
- **Adaptation notes for our task**: Irrelevant as a warm start, relevant as a data argument. If the bottleneck is the quantity of action-labelled gameplay rather than the quality of the initialisation, VPT is the precedent for the cheap fix: train a small non-causal inverse dynamics model on our existing action-labelled segments, then pseudo-label a much larger pile of unlabelled Doom video with 29-way actions. Their accuracy bar (90.6% keypress on a far larger action space, from a 1962-hour label budget) suggests a Doom IDM over 29 discrete actions would be considerably easier. Orthogonal to the backbone choice, and worth a related-work paragraph either way.

## Diffusion Forcing (arXiv 2407.01392)

- **What it is** (one line): A training regime, not a checkpoint: give every token in a sequence its own independent noise level so next-token prediction and full-sequence diffusion become the same objective.
- **Transformer / model size**: Not a released backbone. The implementation uses "a vanilla Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)" that "maintains latents 𝐳ₜ capturing the influence of past tokens"; video experiments use "a convolutional RNN implementation". Parameter counts not stated.
- **Latent space / VAE**: Not stated in the retrieved text.
- **Pretraining data**: Minecraft and DMLab navigation datasets.
- **Conditioning mechanism**: Causal latent state, with the per-token noise level kₜ itself an input; "𝐳ₜ ∼ pθ(𝐳ₜ|𝐳ₜ₋₁, 𝐱ᵗᵏᵗ, kₜ)".
- **Parameterisation and schedule**: "each token is associated with a random, independent noise level"; training samples independent kₜ uniformly from {0,1,...,K} and optimises "𝔼 ∑[‖ϵₜ−ϵθ(𝐳ₜ₋₁,𝐱ᵗᵏᵗ,kₜ)‖²]".
- **Training recipe**: Not stated in the retrieved text.
- **Released checkpoints**: Not stated in the retrieved text.
- **Regime in four sentences**: Train a causal sequence model to denoise a sequence in which every frame carries its own independently sampled noise level, rather than a single shared level. Because the noise level is per-token, the model learns both "predict the next frame from clean history" (history at k=0, future at k=K) and "denoise the whole sequence jointly" (all tokens at the same k) as special cases of one objective. At sampling time you can then pick any schedule over the noise-level grid, which is what gives variable horizons, generation past the training length, and reward-guided planning from one set of weights. The reported evidence is that it "succeeds at stably rolling out even far beyond its training horizon (e.g. 1000 frames)" on Minecraft and DMLab while "teacher forcing and full-sequence diffusion baselines diverge quickly".
- **Adaptation notes for our task**: We already have the key ingredient in embryonic form — a 10-way noise bucket conditioning input — but it is a *single* bucket for the whole channel-stacked clip, whereas Diffusion Forcing needs one level per frame. Applying it to a 132-channel 33-frame stack means sampling 33 independent noise levels, noising each 4-channel slice with its own level, and feeding all 33 levels into conditioning; since the adaLN path only absorbs a fixed-size vector, the cleanest version is a small MLP over the 33 embedded levels, which is a handful of new parameters and no architectural rewrite. The real cost is that channel stacking gives the model no way to attend per-frame, so it has to route 33 noise levels through channel mixing alone. That is precisely the argument for moving to a token-per-frame layout before adopting the regime.

## Self-Forcing (arXiv 2506.08009)

- **What it is** (one line): A training regime that closes the train-test gap in autoregressive video diffusion by rolling out the model's own predictions during training.
- **Transformer / model size**: Base model "Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B (Flow Matching-based)".
- **Latent space / VAE**: Not stated in the retrieved text. Wan2.1 uses its own 3D VAE, so almost certainly **not** our space — flagged as unconfirmed rather than asserted.
- **Pretraining data**: "250k filtered/expanded prompts from VidProM"; "70k generated videos for GAN training".
- **Conditioning mechanism**: Text-to-video via the Wan2.1 base; not restated in the source.
- **Parameterisation and schedule**: Flow matching, distilled to "a 4-step diffusion backbone".
- **Training recipe**: "Approximately 1.5 hours on 64 NVIDIA H100 GPUs for DMD training", with a "stochastic gradient truncation strategy, effectively balancing computational cost and performance", and "KV caching during training" plus a rolling KV cache for long video.
- **Released checkpoints**: Not stated in the retrieved text. Reported throughput "17 FPS (chunk-wise), 8.9 FPS (frame-wise)", latency "0.69 seconds (chunk-wise), 0.45 seconds (frame-wise)", VBench "84.31 (chunk-wise), 84.26 (frame-wise)".
- **Regime in four sentences**: Instead of training each frame's denoiser on ground-truth history, "Self Forcing...performs autoregressive self-rollout during training, denoising the next frame based on previously self-generated frames rather than ground-truth ones". Supervision is not per-frame reconstruction but "a holistic loss at the video level that directly evaluates the quality of the entire generated sequence", implemented with DMD, SiD, or a GAN objective. Rolling out inside the training loop is only affordable because the backbone is distilled to four denoising steps and uses KV caching plus stochastic gradient truncation. The target failure is exposure bias: the mismatch where "models trained on ground-truth context must generate sequences conditioned on their own imperfect outputs during inference".
- **Adaptation notes for our task**: The most expensive item on this list and the least compatible with our current design. It requires an autoregressive frame-by-frame generator so there is something to roll out (our channel-stacked 33-frame model is not one), a few-step sampler so a rollout fits in a training step (which means distillation on top of whatever we train), and a distribution-matching critic — a second network held in memory alongside the generator and the KV cache, hard to reconcile with 16 GB A4000s. If the paper's story is about long-horizon drift in Doom rollouts, the honest framing is that Diffusion Forcing is the tractable version of this idea for us and Self-Forcing is future work.

## What the evidence says about the warm-start choice

Three candidates are in our latent space *and* share the DiT-XL/2 skeleton: **PixArt-alpha XL/2** (sd-vae-ft-ema, 0.6B, 25M images, adaLN-single + T5 cross-attention), **SiT-XL/2** (identical config to our model, but a velocity/interpolant objective), and **Latte-XL/2** (identical config and adaLN-only conditioning, but trained on four narrow video datasets). **Open-Sora 1.1** is in our space with genuine video dynamics but a temporal-token layout incompatible with channel stacking. **PixArt-Sigma** and **Open-Sora 1.2+** are out on VAE grounds (SDXL VAE and a custom 3D VAE respectively).

The uncomfortable result is that none of them approaches the LAION-scale, our-latent-space exposure that makes SD 1.4's U-Net a strong warm start — PixArt-alpha's entire training run is 0.025B images against SD 1.4's lineage of over a million steps at batch 2048. That points at a pretraining-volume gap rather than an architecture gap, and suggests a checkpoint swap alone may not close it. The two interventions with better expected value per GPU-day are REPA's DINOv2 alignment loss (orthogonal to the warm start, cheap, 17.5x reported convergence speedup on exactly our architecture and latent space) and per-frame noise levels in the Diffusion Forcing style.
