# Brief for Astra: the distance metric does not order the arenas; rethink it (2026-09-26, 18:45 EDT)

You are a second research engineer. Propose your own analysis and metric design first; the main session's ideas are withheld from this round and will be compared in a second round.

## The problem

The paper measures a frozen distance D from each evaluation map's footage to the training footage and asks whether D predicts how much a world model trained on four Doom arenas loses on that map. Over 30 maps the partial Spearman of the model's PSNR gain over copy-last persistence on D (controlling for persistence PSNR) is -0.73. But that relation lives entirely in two contrasts: training maps against unseen maps, and deathmatch arenas against single-player campaign maps. **Within the 13 unseen arenas, D predicts nothing**: Spearman of gain on D is about 0.0 (partial about -0.1). What the within-arena gain does track is the persistence PSNR of the map, at -0.78: arenas with static-ish footage (persistence 22 to 23 dB, e.g. arena 8 at D 0.115) have negative gain, arenas with violent footage (persistence 19 to 20 dB, e.g. arena 15 at D 0.127) have gain up to +1.3 dB. The model's absolute PSNR tracks persistence PSNR at 0.94. Deaths per episode do not matter (-0.1). LPIPS gain also does not track D within arenas (-0.13).

Rohan's reading: D was designed for a different situation. In his NVIDIA internship the question was whether a test driving scenario's *appearance* was covered by the training set, scored per scenario. Here the model predicts *transitions* (next latent given 32 context latents and an executed control), the maps of one WAD share appearance, and what varies between arenas is the motion and control statistics of the footage. A per-frame appearance distance cannot see that. He wants a rigorous analysis, not a patch.
