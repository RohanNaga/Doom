**1. What the literature supports.** Citations below were checked against primary arXiv abstracts/full text or proceedings; reported literature statistics are not our recomputations.

- **OTDD [2002.02923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02923), s-OTDD [2501.18901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18901):** transport feature/label distributions; model-agnostic once features exist; predict classification transfer outcomes. Continuous next-latent targets require a new construction and validation; plain D is not OTDD.
- **LEEP [2002.12462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12462)** uses classifier probabilities and target labels, predicts accuracy and binned convergence. **LogME [2102.11005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11005)** measures linear-head evidence on frozen model features, including regression. **TransRate [2106.09362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09362)** measures feature–label information via coding rate. **H-score**, ICIP 2019, DOI `10.1109/ICIP.2019.8803726`, requires model features and measures feature/label informativeness; bibliographic identity verified, original full text unavailable, interpretation checked through LEEP. **NCE [1908.08142](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08142)** measures label conditional entropy and originally needs paired task labels, not a trained model. These predict supervised transfer; LogME on latent innovations is plausible, none establishes diffusion-update cost.
- **Ben-David**, *Machine Learning* 79 (2010), [Theorem 2](https://www.alexkulesza.com/pubs/adapt_mlj10.pdf): domain distinguishability contributes to a target-error bound alongside source error and joint-task error. Proxy A-distance needs a domain classifier, not our predictor; saturation can show shift without ordering adaptation. Regression discrepancy [0902.3430](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3430) is a closer theoretical fit, still no optimizer-speed guarantee.
- **MMD [0805.2368](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.2368)** tests distributions via kernel means; no predictive model required. **FVD [1812.01717](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01717)** requires pretrained I3D and compares Gaussian video-feature distributions and was validated against human video-quality judgments, not transfer cost. Fréchet on **DINOv2 [2304.07193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07193)** or **V-JEPA 2 [2506.09985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985)** needs those encoders; neither representation paper validates that distance as an adaptation predictor. Encoder invariance can erase precisely our texture shift.
- **PAD [2007.04309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04309)** adapts policies through self-supervision, including ViZDoom; it needs a policy and demonstrates return improvements, not distance prediction. Dynamics-error/uncertainty approaches such as **MOPO [2005.13239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13239)** need learned dynamics and penalize uncertain imagined transitions. **Vista [2405.17398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17398), §3.3/4.3**, uses repeated-generation variance to rank action plausibility on unseen driving data. Neither validates adaptation cost; sampling variance conflates ambiguity with ignorance. For sim-to-real, **ReDRAW [2504.02252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02252)** learns latent-dynamics residual corrections to a pretrained world model, demonstrating adaptation, not a prospective cost distance.
- Closest evidence: **XEWorld [2608.05799](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05799v1), §5.4/Appendices C.5,E**, uses training-free HSV histograms plus silhouette moments. Its five-robot appearance/zero-shot-LPIPS correlation is reported r=.812, exact permutation p=.075; no cost predictor. **Westny [2606.30777](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30777v1)** learns reconstruction/forecasting scene embeddings; directional Gaussian KL predicts trajectory transfer error and forgetting. It needs an embedding model and spans heterogeneous datasets. **ScenarioNet [2306.12241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12241)** supplies scenario coverage/diversity infrastructure, not a validated video-adaptation-cost metric. Adaptation precedents **AdaWorld [2503.18938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18938)** and **AVID [2410.12822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12822)** demonstrate model adaptation, without an established distance-to-cost law.

**2. Binding diagnosis.** Marginal novelty, recoverable error, and optimization speed are different quantities. D ignores state-dependent transitions; current coverage averages heterogeneous controls, while G’s weak regressor obscures useful differences. The strong S0 ordering shows that small n alone is not the explanation. Shared WAD assets constrain variation, but do not eliminate observable-dynamics differences. The half-gap threshold depends on A0, so it mixes starting deficit, plateau height and speed. Dense early reads resolve ties; more late updates test plateaus. Neither fixes representation mismatch. Existing ladders/rate tests indicate recipe-specific plateaus, not an intrinsic arena ceiling. In `results/adapt/unet200k_arenas13_map{12,16}_r16_k8_s0_g8k/scores.jsonl`, live/stock `heldout_A_stock(8000)−heldout_A_stock(4000)` is −.003/−.011 dB. A matched full-fine-tune comparator would test capacity.
